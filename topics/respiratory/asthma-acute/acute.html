PEF as % of best or predicted. Near-fatal: raised PaCO2, or needing mechanical ventilation. A normal PaCO2 in a breathless asthmatic is a danger sign.

High risk of death [GINA 2026]: ever intubated or in ICU for asthma, a severe attack in the past year, no ICS or poor adherence, heavy SABA use, major psychosocial problems.

Not asthma? First asthma after 50: think LV failure [STG] (Heart failure). Also COPD, anaphylaxis, upper airway obstruction (stridor), pneumothorax, pneumonia, PE.

INVESTIGATE

PEF before treatment and at 1 hour if the patient can blow [GINA]; continuous SpO2. ABG if SpO2 < 92% or a poor response [SATS 2013]. U&E with K+ (repeated salbutamol lowers it).

CXR only if pneumothorax, consolidation or another diagnosis is suspected, or no response to treatment [GINA]. FBC and CRP do not change the first hour.

FIRST HOUR
  1. Anaphylaxis with wheeze: IM adrenaline first → Anaphylaxis [GINA].
  2. Oxygen to SpO2 93–95% in severe and life-threatening attacks [STG].
  3. SABA (salbutamol) by MDI and spacer or by oxygen-driven nebuliser, every 20–30 min in the first hour (dose box). Spacer works as well as a nebuliser [STG].
  4. Poor response after the first dose, or severe: add SAMA (ipratropium) to the salbutamol, for 3 doses.
  5. Corticosteroid (prednisone) 40 mg orally now. Vomiting, too breathless to swallow or life-threatening: hydrocortisone IV.
  6. No sedation of any kind [STG]. No routine antibiotics: only if consolidation on CXR → CAP.
AT 1 HOUR

Responding: PEF up by > 20% and > 60%, RR < 20, speech normal → salbutamol 2–4hrly as needed, continue prednisone.

Not responding: page 2 now. Recheck for stridor, pneumothorax and anaphylaxis [STG].

CALL

Call the IM consultant now if: any life-threatening feature · PaCO2 normal or rising · no response at 1 hour · magnesium needed · a ceiling-of-care decision is needed.

Dose box CHECK

Salbutamol [STG]:

  • MDI 100 mcg/puff with spacer: 4–10 puffs (up to 20 in severe), 1 puff at a time, 6 breaths each.
  • Nebulised: 5 mg (1 mL of 0.5%) made up to 4 mL with 0.9% saline, oxygen at 8 L/min.
  • Repeat every 20–30 min until PEF > 60%, then 2–4hrly as needed.
  • Life-threatening: 10 mg (2 mL) with ipratropium 0.5 mg, every 20–30 min, 4 doses over 2 hrs.

Ipratropium: 0.5 mg (2 mL) added to the salbutamol nebule, made up to 4 mL, every 20–30 min for 3 doses; or MDI 2–4 puffs (80–160 mcg) by spacer.

Prednisone 40 mg orally daily for 5–14 days, case by case, no taper [STG].

Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV (slowly) or IM 6hrly until able to swallow, then prednisone.

Magnesium sulfate, single dose for a poor response [STG]: {{recipe:magnesium}}. Under 40 kg: 50 mg/kg.

Mitigation · when something is missing
  • No nebuliser or oxygen point: salbutamol MDI with spacer, as effective [STG].
  • No PEF meter: grade on speech, RR, HR, SpO2 and alertness.
  • No ABG: VBG pH and PCO2; a normal venous PCO2 excludes hypercapnia.

RESPONDING
  1. Senior at the bedside; continuous salbutamol with ipratropium; hydrocortisone IV if not yet given.

  3. ABG now and after each change: rising PaCO2, falling pH or tiring → ICU and intubation.
  4. Recheck the diagnosis: CXR for pneumothorax; stridor; anaphylaxis.
NO NIV
OR HFNO

Severe or life-threatening asthma with respiratory failure is a contraindication to NIV and HFNO at George [George SOP 2024]. NIV only delays intubation (GRH 2016; SATS: not routinely recommended [SATS 2013]).

ICU AND
INTUBATION

Refer to ICU [SATS 2013]: drowsiness, confusion or exhaustion · silent chest or cyanosis · bradycardia or arrhythmia · rising PaCO2 or persistent acidosis · PEF falling despite treatment · respiratory arrest.

Intubation by the most senior doctor present; the IM team on call (MO and consultant) makes the ventilation plan → Intubation and ICU referral.

Ventilated: in-line salbutamol nebulisation. No aminophylline or theophylline (GRH; STG: more toxicity, no benefit [STG]).

WARD CARE

Salbutamol 2–4hrly as needed, by spacer once settled [STG]; start the ICS-containing inhaler on the ward. Oxygen only to keep SpO2 93–95%. Recheck K+ while salbutamol is frequent. PEF twice daily to track recovery.

DISCHARGE

Ready when symptoms are mild for 1–2 hrs after the last salbutamol, SpO2 ≥ 92% on room air and PEF > 70% [GINA], with support at home DECISION: PEF > 70% (GINA) or > 60% (GRH 2016, SATS).

  • Prednisone to complete the course (5–14 days), stop date on the script.
  • Every patient leaves on an ICS-containing inhaler: ICS-formoterol (budesonide/formoterol) as maintenance and reliever (GRH 2022): 160/4.5 mcg, 2 inhalations 12hrly plus 1 as needed; seek care if > 12 inhalations in a day [GINA]. The 320/9 inhaler: 1 inhalation 12hrly CHECK
  • Never SABA alone. Check inhaler and spacer technique and adherence; written asthma action plan; smoking.
  • Follow-up within 1 week at the clinic [GINA: 2–7 days]. Expert review (respiratory MOPD) after ICU or intubation, or > 1 prednisone course in the past year [GINA].
Avoid
  • Sedatives and opioids outside ICU
  • NIV or HFNO in severe or life-threatening asthma
  • Routine antibiotics: yellow sputum is eosinophils
  • Aminophylline or theophylline
  • Missing a normal or rising PaCO2
  • Discharge on salbutamol alone
  • Missing LV failure in a first "asthma" after 50
Tertiary option

ICU decided with the consultant using the George SOP: no ICU if the patient declines, CFS ≥ 6, ECOG 4, baseline mMRC 4 or life expectancy < 6 months; otherwise the WC Critical Care Triage score (1–2 high priority, > 8 palliative) [George SOP]. Tertiary ICU transfer is decided on the telemedicine rounds, consultant to consultant.

Mitigation · ICU full or transfer delayed
  • Stay in EC resus with a doctor present: continuous nebulised salbutamol with ipratropium, hydrocortisone IV, magnesium given.
  • Intubate in EC if needed (most senior doctor; video laryngoscope as backup) and ventilate in EC resus until a bed is free CHECK
  • ABG hourly; consultant informed of every change.
