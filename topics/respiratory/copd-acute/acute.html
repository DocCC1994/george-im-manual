RECOGNISE

Known or likely COPD with worse dyspnoea, cough or sputum over days (up to 14), beyond the usual day-to-day variation [GOLD 2026]. Usually viral or bacterial infection; also air pollution and smoke.

Look for a mimic or a second problem: pneumonia, heart failure, PE, pneumothorax, ACS or arrhythmia, and TB.

INVESTIGATE

ABG (note the FiO2) in anyone admitted or with SpO2 < 92%; repeat 30–60 min after any change in oxygen DECISION: GRH 2016 asked for the ABG on room air; GOLD and BTS: on controlled oxygen. CXR, ECG, FBC (eosinophils, Hb), U&E with K+, glucose, CRP.

Sputum culture if severe, ventilated or frequent exacerbations; sputum TB-NAAT if TB is possible. Troponin, D-dimer or echo only for a suspected mimic.

FIRST HOUR
  1. Controlled oxygen to SpO2 88–92%: nasal cannula 1–2 L/min or Venturi 24–28% [BTS/ICS 2016]. Never high-flow oxygen through a non-rebreather mask "to be safe".
  2. SABA (salbutamol) nebulised, with SAMA (ipratropium) if the response is poor [STG 2024]. Drive the nebuliser with air where possible DECISION: air-driven (GOLD) vs oxygen at 6–8 L/min (STG).
  3. Systemic corticosteroid (prednisone) 40 mg orally now, then daily for 5 days in total. IV only if unable to swallow.
  4. Antibiotic if purulent sputum plus more sputum or more dyspnoea, or a severe exacerbation (table). Not for every admission.
  5. Repeat ABG at 1 hour. Still acidotic (pH < 7.35, PaCO2 > 6.5 kPa, RR > 23) → NIV (page 2) DECISION: NIV threshold.
  6. VTE prophylaxis: LMWH (enoxaparin) for all admitted patients [GOLD 2026].
ANTIBIOTIC

Give if [STG 2024]: purulent sputum and either more sputum or more dyspnoea · or respiratory acidosis, severe dyspnoea or hypoxaemia despite oxygen · or NIV or ventilation needed [GOLD].

Consolidation on CXR: treat as pneumonia → Community-acquired pneumonia.

CALL

Call the IM consultant now if: pH < 7.25 · NIV needed · GCS falling, new confusion or drowsiness · no response after 1 hour of treatment [STG 2024] · SBP < 90 or new arrhythmia · SpO2 < 88% despite oxygen · a ceiling-of-care decision is needed.

Dose box CHECK

SABA (salbutamol), nebulised: 5 mg (1 mL of 0.5%) made up to 4 mL with 0.9% saline. Severe: repeat every 20 min or continuously for the first hour [STG]; then 4–6hrly. MDI with a large spacer works as well [GOLD].

SAMA (ipratropium), nebulised: 0.5 mg with the salbutamol; no more often than 4hrly (GRH 2016).

Systemic corticosteroid: prednisone 40 mg orally daily for 5 days, no taper. Unable to swallow: hydrocortisone 100 mg IV 6hrly until oral [STG].

Antibiotic, 5 days in total [STG]: aminopenicillin with β-lactamase inhibitor (amoxicillin/clavulanate) 875/125 mg orally 12hrly. Unable to swallow: 1.2 g IV 8hrly CHECK: not in STG §16.4. Severe penicillin allergy: macrolide.

LMWH (enoxaparin) 40 mg SC daily; 20 mg if eGFR < 30.

Mitigation · when something is missing
  • No ABG: VBG pH and PCO2 to screen (a normal venous PCO2 excludes hypercapnia); watch for drowsiness, headache and flap [STG].
  • No Venturi mask: nasal cannula 1–2 L/min, titrated to SpO2.
  • No air supply for nebulisers: MDI with spacer, or oxygen-driven nebuliser with SpO2 watched.
  • No NIV bed: see page 2.

NIV

Start when pH < 7.35 and PaCO2 > 6.5 kPa persist after 1 hour of optimal treatment and controlled oxygen [BTS/ICS 2016], or at once with marked fatigue (accessory muscles, paradox) [GOLD 2026]. Strong recommendation for hypercapnic failure [ERS/ATS 2017]. Age or a pH < 7.25 alone does not rule out a trial.

Before starting, write the plan if NIV fails: intubate, or NIV as the ceiling of care.

Not for NIV: facial deformity or burns, fixed upper-airway obstruction. Take extra care (consultant, HOCU or ICU): pH < 7.15, GCS < 8, agitation, vomiting, copious secretions, shock, undrained pneumothorax.

NIV SETTINGS

Full face mask · bilevel (S/T) · EPAP 3 cmH2O (higher if OSA or obese) · IPAP 15 (20 if pH < 7.25), raised over 10–30 min to 20–30 until the chest and abdomen move · backup rate 16–20 · I:E 1:2 to 1:3 · oxygen into the circuit to SpO2 88–92% [BTS/ICS 2016]. Above IPAP 30 or EPAP 8: consultant only.

Low IPAP is the commonest reason NIV fails. Check the mask fit and leak before calling it a failure.

ON NIV
  • ABG 1–2 hrs after starting and after each change, then 4–6 hrs CHECK timing. Continuous SpO2; ECG monitor if HR > 120.
  • Failing: pH < 7.25 or worse on optimised NIV, RR > 25, new confusion → consultant now; intubate if within the ceiling of care.
  • Nebulisers during breaks, or in-line if NIV-dependent. As many hours as tolerated in the first 24 hrs; wean over 2–3 days, daytime first, once pH and PaCO2 are normal.
  • Distress on NIV with no plan to intubate: opioid (morphine) 2.5–5 mg IV, consultant decision.
WARD CARE

Reduce nebulisers to 4–6hrly and restart the patient's own inhalers once settled. Stop oxygen when SpO2 stays ≥ 88% on room air. Fluid balance; NG tube for gastric distension on NIV. No aminophylline, no respiratory stimulants [GOLD].

DISCHARGE
  • Clinically stable on room air (or usual home oxygen) and walking; inhaler technique checked with a spacer.
  • Maintenance inhaler: LABA (formoterol), or LABA/ICS if eosinophils ≥ 0.1 × 109/L (measured off oral steroid) [STG] DECISION: LABA/ICS combination (GRH 2024: budesonide/formoterol; STG: salmeterol/fluticasone).
  • Stop dates for prednisone and the antibiotic on the script. Smoking cessation, influenza and pneumococcal vaccines, TB screen.
  • Follow-up within 4 weeks; pulmonary rehabilitation within 3 weeks of discharge [ERS/ATS]. Assess for home oxygen at follow-up, not on discharge → Reference Manual.
Avoid
  • Uncontrolled high-flow oxygen: CO2 rises, pH falls
  • Stopping oxygen suddenly in a hypercapnic patient (rebound hypoxaemia): step it down
  • Aminophylline: side effects, no benefit DECISION: SATS 2019 allows theophylline
  • Sedatives without a ceiling-of-care plan
  • Steroid courses longer than 5 days
  • Antibiotics for every admission
  • Calling NIV a failure before IPAP is ≥ 20 and the leak is controlled
  • Missing PE, heart failure, pneumothorax or TB
Tertiary option

ICU and intubation for: arrest or peri-arrest, GCS < 8, pH < 7.15 or worsening on NIV, NIV not possible, shock, life-threatening arrhythmia [BTS/ICS]. Outcomes are better than doctors predict; decide with the consultant, not on FEV1 alone.

Mitigation · no NIV bed or no ICU
  • NIV on the ward only with 1:1 nursing for the first hours, continuous SpO2 and a doctor nearby DECISION: where NIV may run at George.
  • ICU declined: NIV as ceiling, documented; symptom control if it fails.
