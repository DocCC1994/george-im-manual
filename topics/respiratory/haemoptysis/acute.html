RECOGNISE

Is it from the lungs? Coughed up, bright red and frothy, mixed with sputum, alkaline. Exclude epistaxis (look in the nose and pharynx) and haematemesis (vomited, dark, acidic, food, melaena) [Shaw 2020].

Life-threatening if any of [UpToDate]:

  • Airway or gas exchange affected: SpO2 falling, distress, blood pouring from the mouth
  • Haemodynamic effect: tachycardia, SBP < 90, Hb falling, transfusion needed
  • Volume ≥ 150 mL in 24 hrs (about half a cup), or ≥ 100 mL in 1 hr DECISION: volume threshold (UpToDate 150 mL; Metro West 250 mL; Tygerberg 500–600 mL)

High risk at any volume [Shaw 2020]: post-TB cavities, especially with an aspergilloma · active TB (Rasmussen aneurysm) · bleeding > 48 hrs despite treatment · on an anticoagulant · pregnant · little lung reserve.

Commonest causes here: post-TB structural lung disease and bronchiectasis, active TB, aspergilloma, lung cancer, lung abscess. Also PE, mitral stenosis or LV failure, vasculitis, coagulopathy.

INVESTIGATE

All: FBC, INR and PTT, U&E and creatinine, group and save (cross-match 2 units if life-threatening CHECK), SpO2 (ABG if low), CXR (side, cavity, mass, consolidation), urine dipstick for blood and protein, HIV test. Sputum TB-NAAT and TB culture, and MC&S [STG 2024].

Life-threatening, once stable enough to move: CT angiography of the chest to find the bleeding vessel and plan embolisation [Shaw 2020] DECISION: CTA at George before transfer, or transfer first. Echo if a murmur suggests mitral stenosis.

FIRST HOUR
(life-
threatening)
  1. Lie the patient bleeding side down (side from CXR, a known cavity or the history) to protect the good lung. Suction, oxygen.
  2. Call the consultant, anaesthetist and ICU now. Intubate if the airway or oxygenation fails: largest ETT possible (≥ 8.0). Bleeding from the left: advance the ETT into the right main bronchus. No NIV by full face mask (aspiration of blood) [UpToDate].
  3. Two large-bore IV lines; crystalloid, then blood. Transfuse to Hb ≥ 10 g/dL while bleeding [Paarl 2025] DECISION: Hb target.
  4. Correct bleeding risk: stop anticoagulants, antiplatelets, NSAIDs and prophylactic LMWH. Warfarin: plasma and vitamin K (dose box); a mechanical valve → consultant first. Platelets < 50 → platelet transfusion.
  5. Antifibrinolytic (tranexamic acid) IV DECISION: systemic, nebulised or none. Not with a current VTE.
  6. Suppress the cough: opioid (morphine) IV in small doses; not enough to stop the patient clearing the airway unless intubated.
  7. Refer for bronchial artery embolisation (tertiary box) as soon as bleeding is life-threatening or continues.
CALL

Consultant now: any life-threatening feature · a high-risk patient · bleeding again after settling · Hb falling or transfusion needed · warfarin with a mechanical valve.

Dose box CHECK

Tranexamic acid 1 g IV over 10 min, then 1 g IV or orally 8hrly [Shaw 2020]. Nebulised alternative: 500 mg (5 mL) 8hrly for up to 5 days [UpToDate].

Morphine 2.5–5 mg IV, repeated to suppress the cough [Paarl]. Ward: oral solution 5–10 mg 6hrly DECISION: oral morphine (Paarl) or codeine 30 mg 6hrly (Metro West).

Warfarin, bleeding: plasma (FFP or lyophilised) 15 mL/kg IV over 20–30 min, and vitamin K1 5 mg slow IV (10 mg if not on long-term warfarin) [STG §19.19].

Antibiotic (dose on page 2).

Tertiary options

Bronchial artery embolisation: stops bleeding in 60–92% [UpToDate]; temporary, so the cause still needs treatment.

Pulmonary artery plug for a Rasmussen aneurysm (Tygerberg) [Shaw 2023].

Bronchoscopy (pulmonology) and lung resection (thoracic surgery) for localised disease or an aspergilloma.

Mitigation · transfer delayed or declined
  • Bleeding side down, bed rest, cough suppression, tranexamic acid, transfuse.
  • Intubated: keep deeply sedated so the patient cannot cough.
  • Re-refer if bleeding continues; send CTA images with the referral.
  • End-stage post-TB lung, not for intervention: comfort care, dark towels, morphine and midazolam ready.

NOT LIFE-
THREATENING

Admit if fresh blood beyond streaks, high risk (page 1), hypoxic, or the cause is unclear. Discharge with follow-up if streaking only, a likely bronchitis, normal CXR, age < 40 and a non-smoker [UpToDate].

Ward orders: bed rest; record every episode with the volume in a measuring pot; Hb daily while bleeding; no prophylactic LMWH [Paarl 2025]; mechanical VTE prophylaxis.

Discharge when 48 hrs free of fresh blood [Paarl 2025].

TREAT
  • Antibiotic if purulent sputum, fever or structural lung disease (post-TB, bronchiectasis, COPD): aminopenicillin with β-lactamase inhibitor (amoxicillin/clavulanate), 7–14 days [SAASP 2026]. Severe penicillin allergy: macrolide (azithromycin).
  • TB: start treatment as soon as TB-NAAT is positive (Suspected TB topic; STG §16.9); in life-threatening bleeding with a typical CXR, do not wait for the result (consultant) DECISION.
  • Antifibrinolytic and cough suppression as on page 1 while fresh blood continues.
  • Bronchodilators may continue; a wheezy patient coughs more.
REFER

Pulmonology: recurrent haemoptysis, bleeding > 48 hrs, aspergilloma, suspected cancer, no cause found on CT. Thoracic surgery: localised disease after embolisation in a fit patient [Gross 2009].

Dose box CHECK

Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1 g orally 12hrly [SAASP]; STG: 875/125 mg 12hrly for 10 days [STG §16.3]. Unwell: 3rd-gen cephalosporin (ceftriaxone) 2 g IV daily until afebrile 24 hrs, then oral [STG] DECISION: IV agent (STG ceftriaxone or amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2 g IV 8hrly).

Azithromycin 500 mg orally daily for 10 days [STG].

Avoid
  • Prophylactic or "routine" LMWH while bleeding
  • Lying the patient bleeding side up
  • Full-face-mask NIV
  • Over-sedation of an unprotected airway
  • Calling it haemoptysis before looking in the nose and mouth
  • Judging severity by the Hb: patients drown before they exsanguinate
