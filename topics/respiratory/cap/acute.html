RECOGNISE

New cough (< 2 weeks) with fever ≥ 38 °C, dyspnoea, pleuritic pain or sputum, focal crackles or bronchial breathing, plus a new infiltrate on CXR [SAASP]. Severely ill with a clear CXR: treat, and repeat the CXR at 24–48 hrs [STG].

Severity: CURB-65, 1 point each: Confusion · Urea > 7 mmol/L · RR ≥ 30 · SBP < 90 · age ≥ 65 [GRH 2024]. Weigh hypoxia and comorbidity (HIV, diabetes, heart, lung, kidney or liver disease); a score never replaces judgement.

Severe CAP = any one of cyanosis, confusion, hypotension, RR > 30, or CURB-65 ≥ 3 [SAASP] [STG]. Shock or lactate ≥ 4 → also Sepsis.

Think TB or PJP: HIV, weeks of symptoms, weight loss, no response to recent antibiotics → airborne isolation now. HIV with bilateral diffuse infiltrates and hypoxia → PJP (Ongoing). Mimics: heart failure, PE, COPD, haemoptysis with a mass, aspiration.

INVESTIGATE

All: CXR · FBC, U&E (urea for CURB-65), glucose · HIV test · sputum Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra, even in typical pneumonia [STG] · sputum MCS in every admission (GRH practice; SAASP: rarely changes management) [GRH] · SpO2; blood gas with lactate if SpO2 < 92% or severe · ECG before a macrolide (QTc) [GRH].

Severe, or sepsis suspected: blood cultures before the first dose (never delaying it) · CRP at 0 hrs, to repeat at 48 hrs · Legionella urine antigen and a viral swab for respiratory PCR, especially June–September [GRH] [SAASP]. HIV-positive: urine LAM, CD4; sputum Xpert and TB culture.

FIRST HOUR
  1. Oxygen if SpO2 < 94% [STG]; 88–92% if at risk of hypercapnia. Face mask not enough → HFNO (page 2).
  2. Hypotension: cautious fluid boluses, then vasopressor → Sepsis.
  3. First antibiotic dose now, in EC, by severity (table). Cultures first only if they cause no delay.
  4. Analgesia (paracetamol) for pleuritic pain [STG].

OXYGEN, HFNO
AND NIV

HFNO for hypoxaemic respiratory failure needing more than face mask oxygen [George SOP] [ERS/ESICM 2023]. Candidate: ECOG 1–2, a reversible cause; HIV-positive with a first respiratory illness or CD4 > 100 and suppressed viral load. Not a candidate: consultant decides, case by case, or palliative care path.

NIV is not a George SOP indication for pneumonia (SOP: COPD with hypercapnia, cardiogenic pulmonary oedema); ERS/ESICM: an option for persistent hypoxaemia DECISION: NIV in severe CAP (GRH 2024: consider BiPAP) or HFNO only (SOP).

Not for HFNO or NIV (SOP): depressed consciousness, unstable or uncooperative, vomiting or copious secretions, needs intubation now, terminal illness. Write the plan if HFNO fails before starting.

ADJUNCTS

Corticosteroid (hydrocortisone): GRH 2024: immunocompetent with respiratory failure needing > 50% oxygen, no proven or suspected influenza: 50 mg IV 6hrly for 4–7 days, then taper. ERS/ESICM 2023: only if shock (methylprednisolone 0.5 mg/kg 12hrly for 5 days); not in influenza, uncontrolled diabetes or suspected TB. Shock: hydrocortisone as in Sepsis. STG: silent. DECISION: steroids for hypoxic severe CAP without shock (GRH) or shock only (ERS/ESICM)

Neuraminidase inhibitor (oseltamivir) 75 mg orally 12hrly, 5 days (10 if immunocompromised) [GRH]: severe CAP with suspected or proven influenza, best within 2 days of onset, after discussion with the consultant. ERS/ESICM: PCR-confirmed influenza, or empirically in flu season if no PCR. Not on the EML CHECK stock and renal dose

AT 48 HRS

Improving → switch to oral when HR < 100, RR < 25 on room air, no delirium and able to take oral medicines [SAASP].

Not improving, or CRP not down by 25% [GRH]:

  1. Empyema or abscess: CXR, POCUS → pleural emergencies.
  2. TB: Xpert (repeat), urine LAM; Xpert and LAM negative with ongoing suspicion: TB culture, abdominal US for disseminated TB.
  3. Atypical: add azithromycin if not started; Legionella positive: 5–7 days, stop the β-lactam [SAASP].
  4. HIV-positive: empiric PJP treatment if desaturating on exertion or bilateral ground glass. Also: culture result, obstruction (carcinoma, foreign body), wrong diagnosis.
PJP

HIV with dry cough, bilateral diffuse interstitial infiltrates and hypoxia: co-trimoxazole orally 6hrly for 21 days (80/400 mg tablets: < 60 kg 3 tablets, ≥ 60 kg 4); IV if vomiting. Hypoxic (PaO2 < 9.3 kPa or SpO2 < 92%): prednisone 80 mg daily for 5 days, then taper over 14 days [STG §10.2.9]. Exclude TB.

DISCHARGE

Complete 5 days in total; longer for MRSA or Pseudomonas (7 days minimum), empyema, abscess, extrapulmonary infection or an unusual organism [STG]. HIV test result acted on; TB result followed up.

Follow-up CXR 4–6 weeks after treatment if > 50 years or symptoms persist [STG]; not resolved by 6 weeks: think of another diagnosis, especially lung cancer [GRH] DECISION: routine CXR at 4–6 weeks if > 50 (STG) or only if symptoms persist (GRH).

Avoid
  • Delaying the first dose for a CXR or cultures
  • Azithromycin for every CAP: atypical suspected or severe only [SAASP]
  • Azithromycin beyond 3 days
  • Courses longer than 5–7 days without a reason
  • Missing TB or PJP in HIV
Tertiary option

ICU for intubation, failing HFNO or refractory shock, decided with the consultant using the George SOP: no ICU if the patient declines, CFS ≥ 6, ECOG 4, life expectancy < 6 months, or HIV with an AIDS-defining illness, CD4 ≤ 100 or viral load ≥ 10 000; otherwise the WC Critical Care Triage score (1–2 high priority, > 8 palliative) [George SOP] → ICU referral. Tertiary ICU transfer: telemedicine rounds, consultant to consultant.

Mitigation · ICU full or transfer delayed
  • HFNO in EC resus or the ward with continuous SpO2 and cardiac monitoring and intensive nursing (SOP).
  • Intubate in EC if needed (most senior doctor; video laryngoscope as backup) and ventilate in EC resus until a bed is free CHECK
  • Consultant informed of every change; ceiling of care documented.
