RECOGNISE

Sepsis = suspected infection + new organ dysfunction (SOFA rise ≥ 2). Septic shock = vasopressor needed for MAP ≥ 65 after adequate fluid, and lactate > 2 mmol/L [STG 23.10].

Screen with NEWS2, not qSOFA alone strong [SSC 2026]. At the bedside: RR ≥ 22, new confusion, SBP ≤ 100, SpO2 < 92%, CRT > 3 s, mottling, urine < 0.5 mL/kg/h, lactate ≥ 2.

Think of mimics: PE, haemorrhage, cardiogenic shock, adrenal crisis, DKA, pancreatitis, poisoning, anaphylaxis. Fever + neutrophils < 0.5 → Neutropenic sepsis. Travel to a malaria area → malaria test now.

FIRST HOUR
  1. ABC. Oxygen if SpO2 < 94% [STG 20.12]. Two large-bore IV lines.
  2. Blood cultures before antibiotics, 10 mL per bottle, 2 sets from separate sites (and from any line in situ) [SAASP]. Never delay antibiotics to get them. DECISION: 1 or 2 sets
  3. Lactate (venous gas is fine), glucose.
  4. Antibiotics (page 2): shock, or probable sepsis: within 1 h. Possible sepsis, no shock: rapid work-up; if infection still likely, give within 3 h [SSC]. STG says within 1 h of a presumptive diagnosis DECISION: 3-hour window
  5. Fluid if hypotensive or lactate ≥ 4: crystalloid 500 mL over 30 min, reassess after each bolus, up to 30 mL/kg in 3 h. Stop early for crackles, rising JVP, falling SpO2, or no response. Target urine > 0.5 mL/kg/h and good mentation, not a BP number [STG].
    Fluid: STG 20.12 gives sodium chloride 0.9% first; STG 23.10 and SSC prefer a balanced crystalloid (Ringer's lactate) DECISION
  6. MAP < 65 despite fluid → vasopressor (dose box). Start low-dose adrenaline in a large-bore cannula in a big vein that is not tissued rather than wait for a central line [SSC]; central line once doses rise. Most patients on adrenaline are referred to ICU.
  7. Find the source and plan source control (drain, debride, remove line or catheter) within 6 h [SSC].
INVESTIGATE

FBC, U&E, LFT, CRP, INR (bleeding or DIC), blood gas, HIV, urine dipstick + MCS, CXR, ECG. Site cultures (sputum, urine, pus at drainage; CSF if meningitis is suspected and LP is safe). Malaria RDT if travel. HIV and lung signs: sputum Xpert. Ultrasound or CT to find a drainable source. Procalcitonin does not decide whether to start antibiotics [SSC] (not available at George).

CALL

IM consultant and ICU now for: vasopressor need · lactate ≥ 4 or not falling · respiratory failure · falling GCS. ICU admission within 6 h [SSC]. Surgery or radiology the same hour for an abscess, obstruction, peritonitis or necrotising infection. Agree a ceiling of care early.

Dose box · vasopressor

Vasopressor (adrenaline) [STG, EML]
10 mg (10 ampoules of 1:1 000) in 1 L sodium chloride 0.9% = 10 mcg/mL.
Start 0.05 mcg/kg/min; titrate to MAP ≥ 65 every 10–30 min; usual range 0.01–1 mcg/kg/min.

mL/h = mcg/kg/min × kg × 6
70 kg: 0.05 → 21 mL/h; 0.1 → 42; 0.25 → 105 mL/h.

SSC 2026: noradrenaline first-line strong; STG: adrenaline, as noradrenaline adds cost without proven benefit. George stocks no noradrenaline DECISION CHECK

Patient ≥ 65 years: SSC suggests MAP 60–65.

No dopamine [STG].

Dose box · if shock persists

Adrenaline ≥ 0.25 mcg/kg/min for > 4 h: corticosteroid (hydrocortisone) 50 mg IV 6hrly until shock resolves [STG] CHECK

LV dysfunction and still hypoperfused: add inotrope (dobutamine), ICU only.

Tertiary option

ICU with ventilation, invasive monitoring, dialysis. George ICU first (6 beds, all can ventilate; admission case by case, sickest first, per the GRH ICU SOP). Transfer to a tertiary ICU is decided on the telemedicine ICU rounds, consultant to consultant.

Mitigation · no ICU bed
  • Resuscitation or high-care bed; hourly MAP, HR and urine output (catheter).
  • Adrenaline via a dedicated large peripheral line; inspect the site hourly.
  • Repeat lactate at 2 h; phone ICU again if worse.
  • Document the ceiling of care.
EMPIRIC

Choose by source and by where the infection was acquired. Give the first dose in full, even with AKI; adjust later doses to renal function [STG]. Anaerobic cover only for abdominal, pelvic, necrotising, head-and-neck or CNS sources [SSC]. No routine antifungal.

Every dose on this page CHECK pharmacist

FIRST 6 HOURS
  • Reassess hourly: MAP, HR, CRT, mentation, urine output. Hourly urine output in shock changes management.
  • Repeat lactate at 2 h; aim for a fall of > 20% [STG]. Not falling: more fluid only if still fluid-responsive (passive leg raise), otherwise vasopressor and ICU.
  • Beyond 30 mL/kg: give fluid only for proven responsiveness; stop once overloaded.
  • No sodium bicarbonate unless pH ≤ 7.2 with AKI [SSC].
ONGOING
  • Review antibiotics at 48–72 h: stop, de-escalate to culture results, or switch to oral [SAASP]. Usually 5–7 days once the source is controlled.
  • S. aureus in a blood culture is never a contaminant: remove lines, echo, repeat cultures at 72 h [SAASP Ch 14].
  • LMWH (enoxaparin) prophylaxis unless contraindicated. PPI only if GI bleeding risk.
  • Glucose 6–10 mmol/L [STG]. Transfuse at Hb < 7 g/dL. Feed within 72 h.
  • Goals of care with the family within 72 h [SSC].
Dose box · β-lactam infusions

SSC 2026 recommends prolonged infusion after a loading dose strong. STG 23.10 examples (ICU) [STG]:

  • Amoxicillin-clavulanate 1.2 g over 30 min, then 1.2 g over 4 h 6hrly
  • Piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g over 30 min, then 4.5 g over 4 h 6hrly
  • Meropenem 1 g over 30 min, then 1 g over 4 h 6hrly
