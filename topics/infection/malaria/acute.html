RECOGNISE

Fever + travel to a malaria area = malaria until proven otherwise (travel in the past 3 months, rarely up to a year; Odyssean malaria near airports, taxi ranks, truck routes). Almost all South Africans are non-immune [NDoH 2019]. If ill, treat before the lab confirms [GRH].

Severe = P. falciparum + any one [STG 9.7.2] [NDoH]:

Admit even if not yet severe: pregnancy, HIV, age > 65, asplenia, immunosuppression [GRH]; parasitaemia 2–4% [NDoH].

INVESTIGATE

Smear (with % parasitaemia) and RDT now; negative but suspected: repeat the smear in 12–24 h CHECK. Finger-prick glucose. FBC, U&E, ALT, bilirubin, INR, VBG with lactate, blood cultures, HIV, urine dipstick, β-hCG, CXR if breathless. LP if meningism or coma, once safe (Meningitis). Notify (NMC category 1).

FIRST HOUR
  1. ABC; weigh the patient. Unconscious: lateral position, protect the airway.
  2. IV artesunate within the hour (dose box) I A; do not wait for transfer or a positive smear.
  3. Glucose < 4: dextrose 50% IV (Hypoglycaemia); then hourly, otherwise 4hrly (2hrly on quinine).
  4. Conservative fluid [NDoH 7.4]: no rapid boluses. MAP ≥ 65 and passing urine: crystalloid 1–2 mL/kg per hour, all fluids counted. MAP < 65 and poorly perfused: 5 mL/kg over 15 min, reassess; stop for crackles or falling SpO2 (ARDS, worst in pregnancy).
  5. Shock, HIV, pneumonia, neutrophilia or deterioration: blood cultures, then 3rd-gen cephalosporin (ceftriaxone) 2 g IV daily [NDoH] (GRH 1 g) DECISION (Sepsis). Shock despite a bolus: vasopressor (adrenaline), as in the Sepsis dose box DECISION
  6. Seizures: benzodiazepine (diazepam) 10 mg IV slowly (Status epilepticus).
ONGOING
  • Smear 12–24hrly: may rise in the first 24 h, should fall by 48 h; gametocytes are not failure [STG]. Never follow up with the RDT.
  • Red cells if Hb < 7 (NDoH, GRH; STG < 6) DECISION. Platelets only if bleeding. No exchange transfusion [STG].
  • AKI: early dialysis, not more fluid (AKI).
  • Paracetamol; no NSAIDs or aspirin. No steroids, mannitol or heparin [NDoH].
  • FBC and smear at day 10 [GRH]; FBC at day 14 if parasitaemia was high (late haemolysis) CHECK
CALL

Call GRH IM for every severe case. ICU: coma, shock, respiratory failure, lactate > 5, dialysis (GRH ICU SOP) [SOP]. Obstetrics if pregnant.

Severe tick bite fever (rickettsiosis)

RECOGNISE

Fever, severe headache, myalgia 5–7 days after exposure: dogs (urban, R. conorii), farms, game, hiking or long grass (R. africae). A tick bite is often not noticed [Taljaard].

Look for the eschar: black crust about 5 mm with a red halo, often mistaken for an insect bite. Search the scalp, behind the ears, axillae, groin and anogenital area, umbilicus, under the breasts and between the toes. R. africae: often several eschars and tender regional nodes. Rash from about day 3: maculopapular, including palms and soles (R. conorii); sparse or vesicular (R. africae) [STG 9.10].

Severe (mostly R. conorii, usually after late treatment): confusion, seizures or coma (meningoencephalitis) · pneumonitis, ARDS · shock, myocarditis · AKI · hepatitis · bleeding, purpura (DIC is rare) · VTE [Taljaard].

INVESTIGATE

Clinical diagnosis: treat now, do not wait for tests. FBC (WCC normal or low, platelets low), CRP, U&E, ALT, INR. Exclude malaria (smear, RDT), meningococcaemia (blood cultures; LP if safe) and VHF (below). Serology is negative in week 1: send a paired sample; eschar swab PCR if available CHECK NHLS. No Weil-Felix.

TREAT
  • Tetracycline (doxycycline) 100 mg orally 12hrly for 7 days, or until 3 days after the fever settles [STG]. Doxycycline remains first choice in severe disease; if unable to swallow, crush via nasogastric tube CHECK DECISION
  • No oral route at all: FQ (ciprofloxacin) 400 mg IV 8hrly (less effective); back to doxycycline as soon as possible [STG].
  • Pregnant: doxycycline 100 mg 12hrly for 2 days, then macrolide (azithromycin) 500 mg orally daily for 3 days [STG].
  • Very ill and meningococcaemia not excluded: add ceftriaxone 2 g IV 12hrly. Rickettsiae do not respond to β-lactams, co-trimoxazole or aminoglycosides [Taljaard].
  • Still febrile 48 h after starting doxycycline: look for another or a second diagnosis [STG].

Discuss with ID: shock, encephalopathy, bleeding, severe AKI or hepatitis, poor response [Taljaard].

VHF red flags (Crimean-Congo, Rift Valley; imported Ebola, Marburg, Lassa)

SUSPECT

Fever + bleeding (petechiae, bruising, oozing at drip sites, epistaxis, haematemesis), or fever + low platelets + raised ALT/AST, and in the past 3 weeks [NDoH 2014]:

  • tick bite or crushed ticks; fresh blood or tissue of livestock or game (farm, abattoir, vet, hunter)
  • contact with a suspected VHF patient, including a needlestick in a health worker
  • travel to an outbreak area in tropical Africa

Congo fever: very sudden onset, severe headache, back and leg pain, flushed face, red eyes; bleeds from day 3–5 (meningococcaemia bleeds within 24 h). Normal platelets and transaminases make VHF unlikely. > 90% of suspected cases are a treatable common disease: malaria, meningococcaemia, tick bite fever, sepsis, leptospirosis, fulminant hepatitis [STG 9.5].

ACT NOW
  1. Isolate in a single room, door closed; few named staff; keep a list of everyone who has had contact.
  2. PPE for everyone entering (side box). No needle recapping; avoid IM injections and procedures that are not essential CHECK
  3. Phone the IM consultant, infection control and hospital management now. Notify.
  4. Before any further sample: call the NICD hotline; warn the lab; keep all earlier samples [NDoH 2014].
  5. Treat what is treatable: malaria test; ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily in every case until the diagnosis is confirmed [STG]. Red cells and FFP as needed.
  6. Transfer only once arranged with the ID consultant or regional virologist at the referral centre [STG]. Ribavirin for Congo fever only on NICD or ID advice DECISION
