Overview and definitions

  • Acute meningitis: inflammation of the meninges for < 7 days; any 2 of headache, fever, neck stiffness, altered mental state [SAASP 2026]. Bacterial: S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis, H. influenzae, L. monocytogenes; viral: enteroviruses, herpesviruses.
  • Subacute (days to weeks): TB and cryptococcal meningitis, nearly always with HIV at George; also neurosyphilis.
  • In SA adults with HIV, cryptococcal and TB meningitis outnumber bacterial meningitis CHECK: SA data, not read. Every patient with meningitis gets an HIV test.
  • CrAg screening: reflex serum CrAg on every CD4 < 100; separate request for CD4 100–199. A positive serum CrAg needs an LP even without symptoms [STG 2024, 10.2.4].
  • Notifiable: N. meningitidis, H. influenzae type b, listeriosis [STG 14.8.1], and TB.

Pathophysiology and manifestations

  • Bacterial: nasopharyngeal colonisation → bacteraemia → subarachnoid space; bacterial lysis drives the inflammation (the reason for giving corticosteroid (dexamethasone) before or with the first dose). Purulent exudate, vasculitis, cerebral oedema.
  • TB: a subependymal (Rich) focus ruptures → thick basal exudate → cranial nerve palsies (VI, III, VII), hydrocephalus (communicating or obstructive), vasculitis with basal ganglia infarcts, tuberculomas.
  • Cryptococcal: capsule polysaccharide blocks CSF outflow at the arachnoid villi → very high pressure with few cells → headache, vomiting, CN VI palsy, visual and hearing loss. Little inflammation until ART starts (IRIS).
  • Neurological: seizures, stroke, herniation, deafness (pneumococcal). Cardiorespiratory: septic shock, purpura fulminans (meningococcal). Renal and metabolic: hyponatraemia (SIADH or cerebral salt wasting, common in TBM); polyene (amphotericin B) nephrotoxicity, hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia. Haematological: DIC; anaemia (amphotericin), marrow suppression (flucytosine).

Stepwise workup

Step 1, first labs (before antibiotics if no delay): blood cultures; FBC, U&E, glucose (pair with CSF), CRP, HIV, CD4 if positive; serum CrAg (LFA, 10 min); INR and platelets before LP if bleeding is possible; serum RPR. Malaria test after travel.

Step 2, imaging: CT before LP only for: focal signs, new seizures, papilloedema, reduced consciousness [STG], VP shunt; SAASP uses GCS ≤ 10 [SAASP]. At George after hours only non-contrast CT brain; contrast CT (TBM enhancement, tuberculoma, toxoplasmosis) in daytime. No MRI on site: GSH or private (5 a month). Normal CT does not exclude raised pressure or herniation risk.

Step 3, pitfalls:

  • Advanced HIV: CSF can be normal in bacterial or TB meningitis [SAASP].
  • Antibiotics before LP: culture yield falls within hours; cells and protein change little. Gram stain and blood cultures still help.
  • TBM: early CSF may be neutrophilic. Xpert Ultra misses many cases; send ≥ 6–10 mL CHECK volume. Repeat the LP at 2–3 days on 3rd-gen cephalosporin (ceftriaxone) if bacterial vs TB is unclear: TB CSF stays the same or worsens [STG 14.8.1.1].
  • Crypto: India ink is observer dependent; not recommended at George (GRH 2021). CSF CrAg LFA is sensitive and fast. A positive serum CrAg with a negative CSF CrAg is cryptococcal antigenaemia, not meningitis.
  • Opening pressure is only valid lying on the side, legs extended, patient relaxed.

Escalation and red flags
  • Call GRH IM (MO or registrar on call) for every case; discuss with the IM consultant if GCS falls, seizures, shock, CSF and clinical picture disagree, or TB treatment is started without proof.
  • ICU per the GRH ICU SOP: GCS ≤ 8, airway at risk, status epilepticus, shock. Tertiary ICU via telemedicine rounds.
  • GSH neurosurgery by phone: obstructive hydrocephalus, shunt infection, crypto pressure high despite daily LPs.
  • Never: steroids in cryptococcal meningitis; ART in the first 4 weeks of crypto or 8 weeks of TBM; LP before CT when CT is indicated; delay the antibiotic for any test.

Management by cause

Bacterial [STG 14.8.1] [SAASP]

  • Ceftriaxone 2 g IV 12hrly; directed treatment and durations on the acute page. No renal adjustment. Repeat LPs are of no benefit in uncomplicated bacterial meningitis.
  • Dexamethasone: reduced unfavourable outcome and death in a European trial, mostly from pneumococcal meningitis [de Gans 2002]; no benefit in Malawi, where 90% had HIV and many presented late [Scarborough 2007] CHECK figures. Hence STG (none) vs ESCMID (all, stop if not pneumococcal or H. influenzae).
  • Listeria: aminopenicillin (ampicillin) + aminoglycoside (gentamicin); cephalosporins do not cover it.

TB [STG 14.8.1.1]

  • TB drugs (RHZE) 2 months + RH 7 months; pyridoxine. Weight bands: acute page [STG 16.9].
  • Dexamethasone cut death in HIV-negative TBM [Thwaites 2004]; in HIV, no survival benefit at 1 year [ACT HIV].
  • ART at 8 weeks whatever the CD4 [STG 10.1]: early ART in HIV TBM increased severe adverse events without lowering deaths.
  • Hyponatraemia: check sodium daily; fluid-restrict only if euvolaemic (SIADH); salt and fluid if volume depleted (salt wasting).

Cryptococcal [STG 10.2.4.2] [GRH 2021]

  • Single-dose liposomal amphotericin B + 14 days flucytosine and azole (fluconazole) was non-inferior to 7 days of amphotericin B deoxycholate + flucytosine, with less anaemia, hypokalaemia and renal injury [AMBITION]. One week of deoxycholate + flucytosine beat 2 weeks of deoxycholate + fluconazole [ACTA]: flucytosine is the drug that saves lives, so request it every time.
  • ART at 1–2 weeks raised mortality against 5 weeks [COAT]. Dexamethasone worsened disability and fungal clearance [CryptoDex].
  • Raised pressure is the main early killer: daily therapeutic LPs until symptoms settle. Fluconazole: halve the dose if CrCl < 50 CHECK. Flucytosine: adjust for eGFR (STG App II).
  • Pregnancy: fluconazole is teratogenic in the first trimester but benefit outweighs risk; ultrasound if < 20 weeks [STG].
Dosing pearls
  • Ceftriaxone: 2 g 12hrly for CNS (not the 1–2 g daily used elsewhere).
  • Gentamicin 5 mg/kg daily (Listeria): level and creatinine after day 2.
  • Amphotericin B deoxycholate: ≤ 1.5 mg/kg/day; dextrose only; infusion over 2–6 h (GRH: 4 h) [STG App II].
  • Liposomal amphotericin B: not with soya or peanut allergy; sucrose load in diabetes.
  • Antiviral (aciclovir) 10 mg/kg 8hrly: 14 days; 21 days if immunocompromised; reduce for eGFR.
