RECOGNISE

Any 2 of: headache, fever > 37.5 °C, neck stiffness, altered mental state [SAASP Ch 12]. Photophobia, vomiting, seizures. Purpuric rash = meningococcal until proven otherwise: antibiotic now.

Days to weeks, cranial nerve palsy, HIV with CD4 < 100 → think TB or cryptococcal (page 2). Behaviour change, focal seizures → HSV encephalitis: add antiviral (aciclovir). Mimics: cerebral malaria after travel, sepsis, stroke or subarachnoid bleed, post-ictal state, alcohol withdrawal, hyponatraemia.

FIRST HOUR
  1. ABC, GCS, finger-prick glucose. Seizures → Status epilepticus. Shock → Sepsis.
  2. Blood cultures, FBC, U&E, glucose (to pair with CSF), CRP, HIV; INR and platelets if bleeding is possible. Serum CrAg (LFA, 10 min) if HIV positive or unknown.
  3. CT brain before LP if: focal signs, new seizures, papilloedema, reduced consciousness [STG 14.8.1], VP shunt [SAASP]. SAASP sets the threshold at GCS ≤ 10 DECISION: GCS threshold. If CT is needed: cultures, antibiotic, then CT, LP when safe.
  4. LP: lateral position, legs extended; always measure opening pressure. Do not LP with shock, bleeding or infection over the site; give the antibiotic and LP later.
  5. Antibiotic within 1 hour of arrival (dose box). Never wait for CT or LP [STG]. CHECK: 1-hour target from ESCMID
  6. Corticosteroid (dexamethasone) with the first antibiotic dose? STG: not recommended; ESCMID: recommended (dose box) DECISION
  7. Suspected meningococcal: droplet isolation until 24 h of antibiotic.

48–72 H
  • Organism grown: switch to directed treatment (dose box).
  • Gram stain negative, cells, protein and glucose normal and CRP < 20, or CrAg positive: stop antibiotics and look for another cause [SAASP].
  • No growth and not better: repeat LP. Bacterial: cells, protein and pressure fall. Unchanged or worse: think TB, send 10 mL for Xpert Ultra and culture, consider empiric TB treatment [STG 14.8.1.1].
  • Check sodium daily (SIADH is common); avoid hypotonic fluid.
CALL

Call GRH IM (MO or registrar on call) for every case. IM consultant and ICU (GRH ICU SOP) if GCS ≤ 8 or falling, shock, status epilepticus, or signs of herniation. Hydrocephalus or a shunt: phone GSH neurosurgery.

Dose box · empiric

3rd-gen cephalosporin (ceftriaxone) 2 g IV 12hrly, 10 days if no organism [STG]. Penicillin allergy is not a contraindication [SAASP]. Anaphylaxis to a β-lactam: carbapenem (meropenem) 2 g IV 8hrly.

Listeria risk (age > 50, immunosuppression, alcohol, cancer, pregnancy): add aminopenicillin (ampicillin) 3 g IV 6hrly [SAASP]. STG gives no empiric Listeria cover DECISION

Encephalitis: add aciclovir 10 mg/kg IV 8hrly [STG 14.8.2].

Dexamethasone 10 mg IV 6hrly for 4 days, first dose with or just before the antibiotic; stop if not pneumococcal or H. influenzae [ESCMID] A I. STG: none, as trials in low- and middle-income countries showed no benefit. Never if CrAg positive or crypto likely DECISION

TB MENINGITIS

Suspect: symptoms > 5 days, cranial nerve palsy (often VI), confusion, lymphocytic CSF with high protein and low glucose; CT: hydrocephalus, basal enhancement, infarcts. A negative Xpert Ultra does not rule it out. Look for TB elsewhere: CXR, urine LAM (HIV, CD4 ≤ 200), sputum, abdominal ultrasound.

  • Start treatment on suspicion when TB is probable; do not wait for culture.
  • TB drugs (RHZE) by weight for 2 months, then RH for 7 months: 9 months in all [STG 14.8.1.1]. Pyridoxine 25 mg daily.
  • Steroid, HIV negative: GCS ≤ 14 or focal signs: dexamethasone or prednisone taper (dose box) [STG] [Thwaites 2004]. HIV positive: STG: benefit uncertain; ACT HIV showed no survival benefit [ACT HIV] DECISION
  • ART 8 weeks after starting TB treatment, whatever the CD4 [STG 10.1].
  • Raised pressure: head up 30°, treat seizures, keep sodium normal. CT for hydrocephalus. Obstructive hydrocephalus or falling GCS: phone GSH neurosurgery now (shunt). Communicating: discuss with the IM consultant CHECK: serial LP
  • Worse on treatment: paradoxical reaction or IRIS, tuberculoma, resistance, drug-induced hepatitis (in TBM, pyrazinamide may be re-introduced: STG 10.1, hepatotoxicity).
CRYPTO­COCCAL

Suspect: HIV with CD4 < 100, headache for days, high opening pressure, few cells. Serum CrAg positive → LP in all, even without symptoms [STG 10.2.4].

  • CSF CrAg negative (no meningitis): azole (fluconazole) 1 200 mg daily for 14 days, 800 mg daily for 8 weeks, then 200 mg; start ART now.
  • CSF CrAg positive: induction for 2 weeks (dose box), then fluconazole 800 mg daily for 8 weeks, then 200 mg daily for ≥ 1 year and until CD4 > 200 on ART [STG]. GRH 2021 also asks for a suppressed viral load DECISION
  • ART 4–6 weeks after starting antifungals; earlier ART increases deaths [COAT].
  • No steroids (harmful) [CryptoDex]; no mannitol.
  • Therapeutic LP: opening pressure ≥ 25 cmH2O or headache, vomiting, visual or cranial nerve symptoms: drain up to 30 mL to bring pressure to half the opening pressure, not below 20. Repeat daily until better [STG]. CHECK: threshold of 25; manometers in EC and wards
  • HIV negative: polyene (amphotericin B) 1 mg/kg daily for 4 weeks + fluconazole 800 mg 2 weeks, then 400 mg 2 months; specialist [STG 14.8.1.2.2].
Mitigation · GSH transfer delayed or declined

Hydrocephalus: head up, hourly GCS and pupils, treat seizures and hyponatraemia, ICU review; phone GSH again if GCS falls. Crypto with pressure that stays high: LP twice daily if needed and document each pressure.

Amphotericin B safety
  • Before each dose: 1 L Ringer's lactate + KCl 20 mmol + magnesium sulfate 2 g over 2 h (GRH; STG: sodium chloride 0.9%) DECISION
  • Amphotericin in 1 L dextrose 5% over 4 h; never with saline; flush the line with dextrose.
  • Oral potassium and magnesium 12hrly (GRH).
  • K+, Mg, creatinine at baseline and every 3 days; Hb weekly.
  • Creatinine doubles: stop amphotericin, pre-hydrate 1 L 8hrly, restart when better to complete the course [STG App II].
  • Rigors: paracetamol 1 g orally + hydrocortisone 25 mg IV 30 min before the dose.
