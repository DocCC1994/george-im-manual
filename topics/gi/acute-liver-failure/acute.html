RECOGNISE

Acute liver failure (ALF) = severe acute liver injury with encephalopathy and INR ≥ 1.5, without known cirrhosis [STG]. Hyperacute (jaundice to encephalopathy < 7 days, e.g. paracetamol), acute (8–28 days), subacute (5–12 weeks: worst outlook) [EASL]. Severe acute liver injury without encephalopathy can progress within hours: treat it the same way.

Causes in our setting: drugs (TB drugs, ART such as nevirapine or efavirenz, co-trimoxazole, paracetamol, traditional or herbal medicines) · viral hepatitis A, B (with or without D), E · alcohol · ischaemic hepatitis (shock, heart failure) · pregnancy (acute fatty liver, HELLP) · toxins (mushrooms, iron) · autoimmune, Wilson's disease, Budd-Chiari, malignant infiltration (lymphoma), severe malaria and sepsis [STG].

Danger signs: falling GCS, hypoglycaemia, rising lactate, AKI, bleeding, hypotension.

INVESTIGATE

Now: bedside glucose · blood gas with lactate · INR (repeat 12hrly) · FBC · U&E, Mg, phosphate · LFT, albumin · paracetamol level (always) · blood cultures · HIV, viral load if on ART · HBsAg, anti-HBc IgM, hepatitis A IgM, hepatitis E serology CHECK hepatitis E available · pregnancy test · urine toxicology · malaria test if travelled. Medication history for 3 months, including TB, ART, herbal and over-the-counter drugs.

Abdominal ultrasound with Doppler (Budd-Chiari, malignancy, cirrhosis) in office hours. Autoimmune screen and ceruloplasmin (young patient, haemolysis) later.

IMMEDIATE
  1. Glucose every 1–2 hrs; hypoglycaemia is common [STG]: dextrose 50% 50 mL IV, then a continuous dextrose 10% infusion CHECK rate. Thiamine first if alcohol use.
  2. Airway: GCS falling or grade 3–4 encephalopathy: intubate and ventilate (ICU referral). Head up 30°, avoid hypotonic fluids, keep Na+ 140–145 DECISION: EASL, not in STG.
  3. Paracetamol or unknown cause: start NAC (two-bag regimen, see paracetamol poisoning). Non-paracetamol ALF, early encephalopathy (grade 1–2): NAC may improve transplant-free survival [EASL] DECISION: not in STG.
  4. Stop every hepatotoxic drug: TB drugs and ART only after discussion with a specialist [STG]; a TB patient may need a liver-safe interim regimen CHECK STG TB DILI section.
  5. Coagulation: vitamin K 10 mg slow IV once [STG]. Do not correct the INR unless bleeding or before a procedure: the INR is a prognostic marker [EASL]. Bleeding: freeze-dried plasma 15 mL/kg over 20–30 min [STG].
  6. Circulation: crystalloid to euvolaemia; shock: adrenaline per sepsis. Treat infection early; no routine antibiotics unless sepsis [STG] DECISION: EASL favours a low threshold.
  7. Lactulose 10–30 mL orally 8hrly for encephalopathy [STG]. PPI (pantoprazole) 40 mg IV daily for stress ulcer prophylaxis while ventilated or coagulopathic CHECK.
ONGOING

Neuro obs and glucose 1–2hrly; INR, U&E, phosphate, lactate 12hrly. Protein 1–1.5 g/kg a day without encephalopathy; increments of 20 g a day to 1 g/kg with it [STG]. Avoid sedatives, NSAIDs, nephrotoxins and vigorous paracentesis. Acute hepatitis B: no antiviral; screen sexual contacts [STG].

REFER

Discuss every case of ALF with the internal medicine consultant and the liver unit early (R5: phone the liver unit on call) [STG], before transplant criteria are met. Ventilation, inotropes or dialysis: ICU per the GRH ICU SOP; acute dialysis via the IM consultant.

Dose box CHECK

Dextrose 50% 50 mL IV for glucose < 4, then dextrose 10% infusion.

NAC: two-bag regimen as for paracetamol.

Vitamin K 10 mg slow IV, undiluted, once (INR > 2).

Freeze-dried plasma 15 mL/kg over 20–30 min, only if bleeding.

Lactulose 10–30 mL orally 8hrly.

Thiamine 300 mg orally daily (alcohol) [STG].

Transplant referral criteria DECISION

King's College criteria [EASL]:

  • Paracetamol: arterial pH < 7.30 after resuscitation; or all of INR > 6.5, creatinine > 300 µmol/L and grade 3–4 encephalopathy.
  • Other causes: INR > 6.5; or 3 of: age < 10 or > 40, jaundice to encephalopathy > 7 days, INR > 3.5, bilirubin > 300 µmol/L, drug or unknown cause.

Lactate > 3.5 after fluids is an early warning.

Mitigation · transfer delayed
  • Hourly glucose and neuro obs in ICU or resus; NAC running; intubate before the transfer if GCS falling.
  • Not a transplant candidate (alcohol, multi-organ failure): agree a ceiling of care.
