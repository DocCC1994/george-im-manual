RECOGNISE

Known or suspected cirrhosis (alcohol, hepatitis B or C, fatty liver, autoimmune) with new ascites, jaundice, confusion, GI bleeding, infection or AKI. Signs: spider naevi, palmar erythema, splenomegaly, caput medusae, asterixis.

Hepatic encephalopathy (HE): West Haven grade 1 sleep reversal, poor attention · 2 drowsy, disoriented to time, asterixis · 3 somnolent but rousable, confused · 4 coma [EASL]. Always look for a precipitant: infection, GI bleed, constipation, dehydration or over-diuresis, low K+ or Na+, sedatives or opioids, alcohol, missed lactulose [STG]. Exclude hypoglycaemia, Wernicke (alcohol withdrawal), subdural haematoma, meningitis.

SBP (spontaneous bacterial peritonitis): ascites with fever, abdominal pain or tenderness, more ascites, or new or worse encephalopathy [SAASP]; often silent, or presents as AKI.

INVESTIGATE

Diagnostic ascitic tap in every admission with ascites, before antibiotics: 10 mL into a blood culture bottle, 5 mL in a sterile container (Gram stain), EDTA tube for the cell count [SAASP]. Ascitic neutrophils ≥ 250 cells/mm3 = SBP [STG]. Also ascitic albumin (with serum albumin, SAAG) and protein DECISION: SAAG and protein (not in STG). INR or platelets do not stop a tap; no plasma first [EASL] DECISION.

Blood: glucose · FBC · INR · U&E, Mg · LFT, albumin · blood cultures · HIV, HBsAg, hepatitis C antibody · urine dipstick and culture · CXR. Ammonia does not guide treatment (very limited at George, L9). Abdominal ultrasound (HCC, portal vein thrombosis): office hours only (I10).

IMMEDIATE
  1. ABC, glucose. HE grade 3–4: nurse on the side, protect the airway; ICU only after a ceiling-of-care decision (ICU referral).
  2. Treat the precipitant; stop sedatives, opioids and diuretics. Alcohol: thiamine before glucose.
  3. Lactulose 10–30 mL orally 8hrly (by NG tube if drowsy), aim 2–3 soft stools a day [STG]. Grade 3–4: lactulose enema as well DECISION (not in STG).
  4. SBP, community-acquired: 3rd-gen cephalosporin (ceftriaxone) 1 g IV daily [STG] [SAASP]. Hospital-acquired [SAASP]: bilirubin > 100 or eGFR < 50: carbapenem (ertapenem) 1 g IV daily; otherwise piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g IV 8hrly + amikacin 15 mg/kg IV daily for 3 days. Add ampicillin 1 g IV 6hrly only if: fulminant liver failure, bilirubin > 100, creatinine doubled, worse after 48 hrs, Enterococcus, or biliary disease [SAASP].
  5. SBP: albumin 20% 1.5 g/kg IV on day 1 and 1 g/kg on day 3 (prevents HRS-AKI) [EASL] DECISION: not in STG; albumin supply
  6. Hold the BB (propranolol) while hypotensive, septic or in AKI [Baveno VII] DECISION. Bleeding: upper GI bleeding.

Antibiotic course DECISION

SAASP: 5 days; switch to oral ciprofloxacin if the culture allows; longer only after discussion [SAASP]. STG: ceftriaxone, then ciprofloxacin 500 mg orally 12hrly to 14 days in total [STG]. Draft follows SAASP.

No response after 48 hrs: repeat the tap; neutrophils should fall by 25% [EASL] CHECK.

Avoid
  • Benzodiazepines and opioids (unless alcohol withdrawal)
  • NSAIDs, ACEi or ARB, aminoglycosides outside the SAASP regimen
  • Plasma or vitamin K to correct the INR for a tap
  • Long protein restriction
IMMEDIATE
  1. Tense ascites (breathless, tight, painful): large-volume paracentesis after checking U&E. ≥ 5 L drained, or kidney dysfunction already: albumin 20% 40 g IV [STG]. EASL gives 8 g per litre drained DECISION.
  2. AKI (creatinine up ≥ 26.5 µmol/L in 48 hrs or ≥ 50% in 7 days) [EASL]: stop diuretics, NSAIDs, nephrotoxins and BB; treat infection and bleeding; replace losses. Stage 2–3 or no response: albumin 20% 1 g/kg IV daily for 2 days (maximum 100 g a day) DECISION: EASL, not in STG. See AKI.
  3. HRS-AKI (no response to albumin, no shock, no nephrotoxins, no structural kidney disease): terlipressin 1 mg IV 4–6hrly + albumin 20–40 g IV daily [EASL] DECISION: not in STG CHECK terlipressin stocked (M9). Discuss with the IM consultant and the nephrologist.
  4. Na+ < 125: stop diuretics; fluid restriction to 1–1.5 L a day if euvolaemic or overloaded [EASL] DECISION. Do not correct faster than in hyponatraemia. K+ < 3.5: replace (drives ammonia).
ONGOING

Diuretics once Na+, K+ and creatinine allow [STG]: spironolactone 100 mg + furosemide 40 mg orally daily; raise by 100 mg and 40 mg every 3–5 days to a maximum of 400 mg and 160 mg. Not spironolactone if eGFR < 30. Daily weight: lose at most 0.5 kg/day without oedema, 1 kg/day with oedema. Salt ≤ 2 g (88 mmol) a day.

Diet: protein 1–1.5 g/kg a day; restrict only briefly in acute HE, with a dietitian [STG]. EASL: no protein restriction, late-evening snack DECISION.

Continue lactulose after HE. Restart propranolol 20–40 mg orally 12hrly to a resting HR of 50–60 once stable (varices) [STG]. After SBP: secondary prophylaxis with ciprofloxacin DECISION: EASL, not in STG or SAASP.

Before discharge: cause (alcohol counselling, hepatitis B or C treatment, HIV), Child-Pugh score, HCC ultrasound, endoscopy for varices [Baveno VII].

REFER

Call GRH IM for HE grade 3–4, SBP not better at 48 hrs, AKI stage 2–3, Na+ < 120 or bleeding. Liver unit (R5): urgent cases by phone to the liver unit on call; transplant or TIPS assessment (refractory ascites, recurrent HE, MELD ≥ 15) by email [STG] CHECK liver unit contacts. End-stage, not a transplant candidate: palliative care team.

Mitigation · liver unit cannot take
  • Manage HE, SBP and HRS here with the IM consultant; repeat the tap at 48 hrs.
  • Agree a ceiling of care early with the family: ICU benefit is poor in multi-organ failure.
Tertiary options
  • Liver unit: transplant, TIPS for refractory ascites or variceal rebleeding [STG].
