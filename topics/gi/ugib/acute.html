RECOGNISE

Haematemesis, coffee-ground vomit, melaena; fresh blood per rectum with shock can be a brisk upper bleed. Shock: HR > 100, SBP < 100, cold, confused, syncope. Hb may be normal early.

Suspect varices in known cirrhosis or portal hypertension, heavy alcohol use, jaundice, ascites, spider naevi, splenomegaly. Other causes: peptic ulcer (NSAIDs, aspirin, H. pylori), oesophagitis, Mallory-Weiss tear, cancer.

Risk: Glasgow-Blatchford score 0–1 = very low risk: may go home for outpatient endoscopy [ESGE] DECISION: use the Glasgow-Blatchford score for discharge (not in STG).

INVESTIGATE

Now: Hb on blood gas (with lactate) · FBC · group and crossmatch · INR, PTT · U&E (urea rises out of proportion), LFT, albumin · glucose · ECG if older or chest pain. HIV if unknown.

IMMEDIATE
  1. Resuscitate: two large-bore cannulas; crystalloid for shock, with care in varices (overfilling raises portal pressure). Haematemesis with reduced GCS: protect the airway (intubate) before endoscopy.
  2. Transfuse restrictively: Hb < 7 g/dL (STG lists upper GI bleeding with this trigger); in active bleeding judge by rate of loss, not one Hb [STG]. One unit at a time, then reassess [STG]. ESGE: target 7–9, or ≥ 10 with cardiovascular disease DECISION: Hb target. Massive bleeding: see transfusion.
  3. PPI (pantoprazole) 80 mg IV, then 40 mg IV 12hrly until endoscopy [ESGE] DECISION: STG has no dose for bleeding (IV pantoprazole 40 mg daily for stress ulcer prophylaxis only)
  4. Suspected varices, before endoscopy [Baveno VII]:
    • vasoactive drug for 2–5 days: terlipressin 2 mg IV 4hrly (1 mg if < 50 kg) CHECK dose and stock (M9), or octreotide 50 mcg IV bolus, then {{recipe:octreotide}}
    • antibiotic: 3rd-gen cephalosporin (ceftriaxone) 1 g IV daily, up to 7 days
    DECISION: STG and SAASP are silent on vasoactive drugs and antibiotic prophylaxis in variceal bleeding
  5. Coagulation: reverse anticoagulants (anticoagulant bleeding). Bleeding with INR > 2: freeze-dried plasma 15 mL/kg; platelets < 50: one pool [STG]. In cirrhosis the INR does not measure bleeding risk: do not correct it routinely [Baveno VII] DECISION
  6. No tranexamic acid: no benefit and more clots in GI bleeding [HALT-IT]; not an STG indication [STG].
  7. Stop NSAIDs, aspirin, anticoagulants; nil by mouth for endoscopy.
ONGOING

Endoscopy within 24 hrs of presentation once resuscitated [ESGE]; suspected varices within 12 hrs [Baveno VII] DECISION: timing (not in STG). Obs hourly; Hb 6–12hrly; watch for rebleeding (fresh haematemesis, melaena, falling BP). Cirrhosis: lactulose to prevent encephalopathy.

After endoscopy [STG]: ulcer: H. pylori eradication if positive (amoxicillin 1 g 12hrly + metronidazole 400 mg 12hrly + pantoprazole 40 mg 12hrly for 14 days), then pantoprazole 40 mg daily (duodenal up to 2 weeks, gastric up to 6 weeks and rescope). Varices: band ligation, then BB (propranolol) 20–40 mg orally 12hrly to a resting HR of 50–60. Restart aspirin for secondary prevention early CHECK timing.

REFER

Call GRH IM and the surgical team on call for shock, ongoing bleeding, rebleeding or suspected varices: urgent endoscopy. Uncontrolled variceal bleeding: balloon tamponade as a bridge CHECK Sengstaken tube stocked; ICU (ICU referral); TIPS or transplant: specialist unit [STG].

  • NSAIDs
