Overview and definitions

  • Anaemia: Hb below the reference range: men < 13.0, women < 12.0 g/dL [STG 2024]. Alpha-thalassaemia trait (common in African ancestry) shifts the range [UpToDate].
  • Severe: Hb < 7 g/dL DECISION D2; danger signs (shock, bleeding, cardiac failure, angina, severe dyspnoea, syncope) override any number.
  • By MCV: microcytic < 80 · normocytic 80–100 · macrocytic > 100 fL [STG].
  • By mechanism: loss · destruction (haemolysis) · underproduction. Several often coexist; look for more than one cause [MGH 2025].
  • SA ward context: HIV (marrow suppression, ART, opportunistic infections, lymphoma), TB (chronic disease, marrow infiltration), hookworm and urinary schistosomiasis, malnutrition and alcohol, CKD [STG] [GRH 2022].

Pathophysiology and manifestations

  • O2 delivery depends on Hb, blood volume, cardiac output and perfusion: hypovolaemia, low output and coronary disease worsen symptoms at any Hb; chronic anaemia is compensated (2,3-BPG, plasma volume), so symptoms come late [UpToDate].
  • Cardiorespiratory: exertional dyspnoea, tachycardia, flow murmur, angina (with CAD), high-output heart failure. Neurological: fatigue, light-headedness, syncope; B12: peripheral neuropathy, subacute combined degeneration, dementia [STG 2.1.2].
  • Anaemia of chronic disease: IL-6 → hepcidin → iron trapped in macrophages; IL-1 and TNF-α blunt EPO. Usually Hb 7–9 g/dL [GRH 2016], but it can fall by 2–3 g/dL in 1–2 days in acute illness [MGH 2025].

Stepwise workup

Step 1, initial labs before transfusion (list on the acute page).

  • Reticulocyte index = retic % × (Hct ÷ normal Hct) ÷ maturation factor: < 2 underproduction; > 2 loss or haemolysis [HemeOnc 2022].
  • Bleeding in the last 5–7 days still gives a low reticulocyte count (the marrow has not responded yet) [UpToDate].
  • Raised LDH with low haptoglobin: about 90% specific for haemolysis; normal LDH with haptoglobin > 25 mg/dL: about 92% sensitive for ruling it out [UpToDate].

Step 2, direct the rest by reticulocytes, MCV and smear:

  • RI > 2: haemolysis screen (LDH, bilirubin, haptoglobin, DAT, smear, urine dipstick) or recent bleeding.
  • RI < 2, microcytic: ferritin and TSAT (table); Hb electrophoresis if iron replete.
  • RI < 2, normocytic: U&E, LFT, TSH, ferritin and TSAT, CRP, HIV; protein electrophoresis and free light chains if myeloma is possible.
  • RI < 2, macrocytic: B12, RBC folate, TSH, LFT, alcohol, drugs; smear for hypersegmented neutrophils and oval macrocytes [STG 2.1.2].
  • At George: gastroscopy and colonoscopy by surgery (usually daytime); abdominal ultrasound for splenomegaly (usual hours); CXR or CT for TB or malignancy; bone marrow CHECK C8.

Clues to the cause

Pica, koilonychia, restless legs (iron) · glossitis (B12, folate, iron) · jaundice, dark urine (haemolysis) · splenomegaly (cirrhosis, infection, malignancy, chronic haemolysis) · bone pain (myeloma) · petechiae (pancytopenia, coagulopathy) [HemeOnc 2022].

Step 3 · pitfalls
  • After transfusion: serum iron rises falsely, DAT can be transiently positive, smear and indices show donor cells: draw first [HemeOnc 2022].
  • Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant: use the GRH cut-offs, not the lab range [GRH 2022].
  • Mixed iron + B12/folate deficiency: normal MCV with a high RDW (dimorphic).
  • G6PD level can be normal during acute haemolysis; repeat at about 3 months [MGH 2025].
  • Metformin causes B12 deficiency: low threshold to check [STG 2.1.2]. Faecal occult blood is not recommended in the IDA workup [GRH 2022].

Management by cause

Transfusion [STG 2.1.1] [STG 23.5.2]

  • Heart failure or severe dyspnoea, active bleeding, urgent procedure. Not bleeding: Hb < 7 g/dL (septic shock, UGIB included); stable CAD and older age need no higher trigger; Hb 7–9 may be appropriate in ACS, stroke, brain injury and oncology DECISION D3.
  • One unit at a time, reassess before the next; the exception is massive bleeding.
  • Restrictive (Hb 7) was non-inferior to liberal (Hb 10) in ICU patients [TRICC]. In MI with anaemia, liberal (Hb < 10) did not significantly reduce MI or death at 30 days but leaned towards benefit [MINT] CHECK C11: trial details from background knowledge; verify against the papers
  • Works less well in haemolysis (short red cell survival). Each unit carries about 200–250 mg iron [HemeOnc 2022].

Iron deficiency (GRH 2022 base) [GRH 2022]

  • Oral iron (ferrous sulfate) first; don't defer for endoscopy DECISION D4: dose. Alternate-day dosing if not tolerated (better absorption) [MGH 2025].
  • IV iron (iron dextran): GRH offers it for moderate to severe anaemia or oral intolerance; STG limits it to no response after 3 months, intolerance, malabsorption, dialysis/EPO, ≥ 36 weeks' pregnancy, with a specialist DECISION D5. Not in the critically ill [STG 23.5.2]. Dose and recipes: acute page.
  • Refractory IDA with negative bidirectional endoscopy: discuss small bowel and urinary tract imaging.

Megaloblastic [STG 2.1.2]

  • B12 + folate together until the results are back; K+ in the first days; iron and folate during recovery.
  • No response: coexisting deficiency, MDS, hypothyroidism, alcohol, drugs. After gastrectomy or ileal resection: B12 1 mg IM every second month for life.

Haemolysis [STG 2.1.4]

  • Folic acid 5 mg daily. Warm AIHA: corticosteroid (prednisone) 1 mg/kg until Hb stable and > 10 g/dL, then a slow taper. Still severe after 3 weeks, relapse on a low dose, or intolerance → haematologist for second line (immunosuppression, splenectomy with vaccination).
  • Cold agglutinin disease: keep warm, warm all blood; corticosteroids and splenectomy do not help [MGH 2025]. G6PD: stop the trigger. Malaria; TTP/MAHA.

Chronic disease and HIV

  • Treat the cause; iron only if deficient (TSAT < 20%). Erythropoietin for CKD via the nephrologist CHECK C9: ESA access at George; not in malignancy [GRH 2016].
  • Pancytopenia in PLHIV: HIV itself, drugs (carbamazepine, valproate, phenytoin; zidovudine; red cell aplasia with emtricitabine or lamivudine), opportunistic infections, malignancy, nutrition. Marrow if no cause is found or it persists [STG 2.1.5].
Escalation and ward red flags
  • Haematologist (on site), same day: schistocytes + thrombocytopenia (TTP/MAHA) · blasts or leucoerythroblastic film · severe pancytopenia · AIHA · suspected aplastic anaemia.
  • Call GRH IM; discuss with the internal medicine consultant: Hb falling despite transfusion; haemodynamic instability; transfusion in heart failure.
  • ICU (GRH ICU SOP): haemorrhagic shock needing ventilation or ongoing resuscitation.
  • Never: platelet transfusion in TTP without the haematologist · folic acid alone in undiagnosed macrocytic anaemia · transfusion before the samples in suspected haemolysis, unless it cannot wait.
Mitigation · referral or transfer delayed

Document the discussion and plan. Transfuse to symptoms, one unit at a time. Start replacement once samples are drawn; folic acid in all haemolysis; prednisone for warm AIHA after haematology advice.

Tertiary options

Via the haematologist: plasma exchange (TTP) CHECK C7, marrow CHECK C8, second-line AIHA therapy, Hb electrophoresis, PNH flow.

Local layer · George

As on the acute page: blood bank off site, two forms, CosmoFer, haematologist on site.
