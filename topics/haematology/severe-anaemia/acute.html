RECOGNISE

Anaemia: Hb below the reference range (men 13.0–17.0, women 12.0–15.0 g/dL) [STG 2.1]. Severe: Hb < 7 g/dL DECISION D2: < 7 or < 8 g/dL (WHO, non-pregnant adults) CHECK C1: WHO cut-off not in the project sources, or any Hb with danger signs.

Danger signs (decide on transfusion now): shock or active bleeding · cardiac failure · angina · severe dyspnoea · syncope or confusion [STG 2.1.1].

How fast it fell matters more than the number. Acute bleeding or haemolysis causes symptoms at a higher Hb; chronic anaemia may be well tolerated at a very low Hb. After acute bleeding the Hb underestimates the loss for 36–48 h [UpToDate].

Clues to the cause: melaena, haematemesis, heavy menses · jaundice, dark urine (haemolysis) · fever and travel (malaria) · bruising, petechiae, infections (pancytopenia) · neuropathy, glossitis (B12) · spleen, nodes · drugs (zidovudine, anticonvulsants, chemotherapy).

Can't-miss patterns: schistocytes + low platelets (TTP/MAHA) · pancytopenia or blasts · fever + haemolysis (malaria) · rapid fall in Hb with dark urine (acute haemolysis).

INVESTIGATE

Draw these BEFORE the first unit of blood: transfusion makes most of them uninterpretable [MGH 2025].

  • FBC with differential, MCV, MCH, RDW · reticulocyte count · peripheral smear (ask NHLS for a smear review).
  • Group and crossmatch early: Coombs-positive haemolysis is hard to crossmatch [STG 2.1.4].
  • Ferritin, transferrin saturation (TSAT) · vitamin B12, RBC folate.
  • Haemolysis screen: LDH, bilirubin (total and conjugated), haptoglobin, DAT (Coombs) CHECK C2: which of these NHLS George offers, and turnaround (local facts D3)
  • U&E, creatinine, LFT, CRP, HIV · INR and PTT if bleeding or low platelets · malaria rapid test and smear if fever or travel to a malaria area.
  • Urine dipstick for blood (haematuria; haemoglobinuria in haemolysis). Faecal occult blood is not useful [GRH 2022].
IMMEDIATE
  1. Bleeding or shock: ABC, 2 large-bore IV lines, resuscitate, find and stop the source: Upper GI bleeding, Bleeding on anticoagulants. Emergency blood is on site (local notes). The Hb trigger does not apply to the bleeding patient: transfuse by the rate of loss and the physiology [STG 23.5.2].
  2. Transfuse red cell concentrate (RCC) for severe anaemia with cardiac failure or severe dyspnoea, active ongoing bleeding, or before an urgent procedure [STG 2.1.1]. Stable, not bleeding: Hb < 7 g/dL; Hb 7–9 may be appropriate in acute coronary syndrome, stroke or oncology [STG 23.5.2] DECISION D3: the STG trigger is written for critical care; adopt it for ward patients? → Red cell transfusion
  3. One unit at a time, then review symptoms and Hb before ordering the next [STG 23.5.2]. Aim to relieve symptoms, not to normalise the Hb.
  4. At risk of fluid overload (older, cardiac disease, chronic severe anaemia): run it slowly and give a loop diuretic (furosemide) IV with each unit (dose box). Watch for transfusion-associated circulatory overload.
  5. Suspected haemolysis: draw the bloods first unless transfusion cannot wait. If life-threatening, give the most compatible unit and call the haematologist now. Cold agglutinins: blood warmer for every unit. Possible G6PD deficiency: avoid aspirin, sulphonamides (including co-trimoxazole), dapsone, methylene blue, primaquine [STG 2.1.4].
  6. Schistocytes + low platelets (TTP/MAHA): no platelet transfusion unless the haematologist advises it [STG 2.6]. Call the haematologist now → Thrombocytopenia and pancytopenia.
  7. Macrocytic or suspected megaloblastic anaemia: once B12 and folate are drawn, give vitamin B12 IM and folic acid together, never folic acid alone (it can precipitate permanent neurological deficit). K+ daily for the first few days: hypokalaemia [STG 2.1.2].
REFER

Haematologist (on site), discuss now: TTP/MAHA · pancytopenia or blasts · haemolysis (AIHA or unexplained) · suspected aplastic anaemia · no cause found, or no response to treatment. They refer on to the tertiary centre. Call GRH IM: danger signs or transfusion need in EC or at a district hospital. ICU: haemorrhagic shock, per the GRH ICU SOP.

ONGOING
  • Hb the morning after transfusion. Keep phlebotomy to a minimum: blood taken for tests adds to the anaemia [STG 23.5.2].
  • Iron deficiency: start oral iron now; do not wait for endoscopy [GRH 2022]. Men and postmenopausal women: gastroscopy and colonoscopy (gastroscopy first if upper GI symptoms) [GRH 2022] [STG 2.1.1]. Premenopausal women with no obvious cause: treat and watch the response. IV iron: dose box DECISION D5: who gets IV iron
  • B12 or folate deficiency: dose box. Also give iron and folic acid until the Hb is normal [STG 2.1.2].
  • Haemolysis: folic acid 5 mg daily. Warm AIHA: corticosteroid (prednisone), with the haematologist [STG 2.1.4]. G6PD: stop the trigger. Malaria → Severe malaria.
  • Anaemia of chronic disease (HIV, TB, malignancy, CKD, RA): treat the cause; no iron, B12 or folate without a documented deficiency. TSAT < 20% suggests coexisting iron deficiency [STG 2.1.3]. Transfusion is seldom needed.
  • PLHIV: consider the ART regimen (zidovudine; pure red cell aplasia with emtricitabine or lamivudine), opportunistic infections and malignancy [STG 2.1.5]. Discuss before changing ART.
  • Critically ill: no IV iron and no erythropoietin; they do not reduce transfusion or improve outcome [STG 23.5.2].
  • Expected response: reticulocytes rise from day 3–5 after B12 (peak about day 7); WCC and platelets normal in 7–10 days, Hb in 1–2 months. On iron, Hb rises by ≥ 2 g/dL at 1 month; a rise of < 1 g/dL at 2 weeks predicts failure [STG] [GRH 2022].
  • No response to iron: non-adherence, continued loss, malabsorption, mixed deficiency, wrong diagnosis. Refer if anaemia persists despite adherence [STG 2.1.1].

Dose box · replacement

Vitamin B12 IM CHECK C12: product at George [STG 2.1.2]

  • Serious complications (neurological, severe anaemia): 1 mg daily for 1 week → 1 mg weekly for 1 month → 1 mg monthly for life.
  • Uncomplicated pernicious anaemia: 1 mg on alternate days for 1–2 weeks → weekly until the FBC is normal → 1 mg monthly for life.

Folic acid 5 mg orally daily (always with B12 until the levels are known).

Oral iron (ferrous sulfate compound BPC) 170 mg (about 55 mg elemental) 12hrly [STG]; GRH 2022: ferrous sulphate 200 mg once daily DECISION D4. Not tolerated: alternate days with meals, then once weekly. Empty stomach; not with tea, antacids, milk or calcium. Continue 3 months after the Hb is normal.
