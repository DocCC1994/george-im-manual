RECOGNISE

Pesticide ingestion (often deliberate), or skin or inhalational exposure (farm work). Street "rat poison" ("two-step", aldicarb, a carbamate) behaves the same and is treated the same. Solvent or garlic smell.

  • Muscarinic: pinpoint pupils, bradycardia, hypotension, bronchorrhoea, bronchospasm, salivation, lacrimation, sweating, vomiting, diarrhoea, incontinence.
  • Nicotinic: fasciculations, weakness, paralysis (respiratory failure). Tachycardia, hypertension and dilated pupils can mask the picture.
  • CNS: confusion, coma, seizures.

Mimic: amitraz (cattle and dog tick dip): coma, small pupils, bradycardia, hypothermia, hyperglycaemia, but no sweating, salivation or fasciculations. Supportive care; atropine only for symptomatic bradycardia [STG 2024 §19.18.1]. Unknown "rat poison": also consider a superwarfarin (INR).

INVESTIGATE

Clinical diagnosis: do not wait for tests. Plasma cholinesterase supports it (NHLS, 1–2 days).

Glucose, blood gas with K+ and lactate, U&E, Mg2+, FBC, ECG (bradycardia, heart block, long QTc), CXR if aspiration. Paracetamol level (co-ingestion) and pregnancy test [GRH 2016].

IMMEDIATE
  1. Staff: gloves, gown, eye protection. Resuscitate first, then decontaminate: remove clothes into closed bags, wash skin with soap and water.
  2. Airway: suction often. Intubate for GCS ≤ 8, respiratory failure or secretions not controlled by atropine. No suxamethonium (prolonged paralysis): use a non-depolarising muscle relaxant (rocuronium).
  3. Atropine 2 mg IV now DECISION: STG starts at 2 mg; GRH 2016 at 2–5 mg. Reassess every 3–5 min: no response → double the dose (2, 4, 8, 16 mg…); some response → repeat the same dose. Keep a running total.
  4. Atropinised when all are met: clear chest (no secretion crackles), no wheeze, HR > 80, SBP > 80, dry axillae, pupils no longer pinpoint [GRH 2016]. Tachycardia and dilated pupils are not reasons to withhold atropine during resuscitation [STG 2024].
  5. Hypotension: crystalloid (sodium chloride 0.9% or Ringer's lactate) bolus.
  6. Seizures or severe agitation: benzodiazepine (diazepam) 5–10 mg IV, repeat after 30–60 min if needed. No phenytoin.
  7. Activated charcoal 50 g orally once stable and airway protected DECISION: STG gives it once stabilised; GRH 2016 only within 1 hour of ingestion.
  8. Oxime (pralidoxime): not in the STG or EML; not used CHECK: local facts M6, is any oxime stocked?
ONGOING
  • Atropine infusion: 10% of the total bolus dose per hour, titrated up to 20% (dose box). Secretions, wheeze or bradycardia return: bolus again (as in step 3), then reset the rate to 10% of all boluses given. Atropine toxicity (agitation, fever, no bowel sounds, urinary retention): reduce the rate. Wean over ≥ 24 h; never stop abruptly.
  • Intermediate syndrome 1–4 days after the cholinergic crisis: neck flexor, bulbar, proximal and respiratory weakness. Test neck flexion and watch respiratory rate and blood gas daily; ventilate early. Fat-soluble agents (fenthion, malathion) relapse up to 5 days.
  • Cardiac monitor while on atropine; VT or torsades: ACLS plus magnesium [GRH 2016].
  • Delayed neuropathy can follow weeks later: warn at discharge.
DISPOSITION

Discuss every symptomatic patient with GRH IM. ICU referral if intubated or atropine needs keep rising (per the George ICU SOP; ICU referral). Asymptomatic exposure: observe ≥ 24 h before discharge DECISION: STG and GRH 2016 give no observation period.

All pesticide poisoning is a notifiable medical condition (NICD). Deliberate: psychiatry and social work once medically stable.
