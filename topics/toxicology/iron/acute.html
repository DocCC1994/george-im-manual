RECOGNISE

Elemental iron: ferrous sulfate 170 mg = 65 mg; ferrous fumarate 200 mg = 65 mg; ferrous gluconate 300 mg = 35 mg [STG]. Toxic > 20 mg/kg; severe > 60 mg/kg [SU 2026].

GI phase (0–6 hrs): vomiting, diarrhoea, GI bleeding. A false recovery at 6–36 hrs, then shock, metabolic acidosis, coma, AKI and liver failure (12–96 hrs) [STG].

INVESTIGATE

Serum iron at 4–6 hrs, then 6hrly to the peak; uninterpretable once desferrioxamine runs [STG]. µmol/L: < 60 mild, 60–90 moderate, > 90 severe, > 180 risk of death [SU 2026]. VBG with lactate, glucose, U&E, LFT, INR, FBC, group and save. Abdominal X-ray: tablets are radio-opaque; a clear film does not exclude.

TREAT

IV fluid for hypotension; adrenaline for shock. No charcoal (does not bind iron). Whole bowel irrigation if > 60 mg/kg, slow-release, or tablets on X-ray [STG].

Desferrioxamine if: severe symptoms (altered mental state, haemodynamic instability, metabolic acidosis), serum iron > 90, or peak > 60 with ongoing GI symptoms [STG]. Severe: start without waiting for the level [SU 2026]. Stop when shock and acidosis resolve. Safe in pregnancy.

No chelation if iron < 54 and no symptoms at 6 hrs [STG].

DISPOSITION

Shock or acidosis: ICU. Discuss with GRH IM if > 20 mg/kg or any symptoms; GI symptoms that settle are not recovery. Haemodialysis clears ferrioxamine in AKI.

Dose box CHECK

Desferrioxamine IV infusion 15 mg/kg/hr, total 80 mg/kg (about 6 hrs) [STG]; slow if hypotensive. > 24 hrs of high dose risks ARDS.
