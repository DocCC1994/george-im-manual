RECOGNISE

Brazier ("mbawula") or gas heater in a closed room, house fire, generator or car exhaust (deliberate). Headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, syncope, seizures, coma, chest pain. Several people from one household; better away from the source.

INVESTIGATE

SpO2 and PaO2 are falsely normal. Carboxyhaemoglobin (COHb) on a co-oximetry blood gas CHECK: local facts L4, do George analysers report COHb? COHb falls once oxygen starts (note the time) and matches severity poorly [STG 2024 §19.20]. Lactate, glucose, ECG, troponin (chest pain, ECG change or coma), CK, pregnancy test.

IMMEDIATE
  • 100% oxygen now: tight-fitting face mask, or FiO2 1.0 if intubated. Continue ≥ 6 h and until symptom-free DECISION: duration not in STG.
  • Seizures: benzodiazepine (diazepam) as for status epilepticus; no phenytoin. Treat acidosis only if profound and persistent.
  • House fire with coma, shock or lactate ≥ 8 mmol/L: consider cyanide → hydroxocobalamin 5 g IV over 15 min CHECK: dose not yet checked CHECK: local facts M16, stocked? DECISION: not in STG
DISPOSITION

Admit if any loss of consciousness, neurological signs, ischaemia, acidosis or pregnancy. Discharge when symptom-free on air with a normal neurological examination; follow up for delayed memory and mood problems (2–6 weeks). Discuss with GRH IM if comatose, ischaemic or pregnant.
