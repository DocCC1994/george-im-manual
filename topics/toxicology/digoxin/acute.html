RECOGNISE

Chronic (most): older patient on digoxin with AKI, hypokalaemia or low body mass [STG]. Acute: intentional overdose. Nausea, vomiting, confusion, visual change (yellow-green).

ECG: any arrhythmia. Typical: frequent ectopics, bigeminy, bidirectional VT, atrial tachycardia with block, slow regular AF, AV block [Pincus 2016].

INVESTIGATE

K+ now and 2–4hrly: hyperkalaemia in acute overdose marks severe toxicity. U&E, magnesium, calcium; continuous ECG. Digoxin level (therapeutic 0.6–1 nmol/L [STG]) at least 6 hrs after the last dose CHECK; it takes 2–3 days at George, so treat on the clinical picture.

RESUSCITATE

Stop digoxin. Correct K+ to 4–5 and magnesium. Bradycardia: atropine. Hyperkalaemia: insulin with dextrose → Hyperkalaemia DECISION: calcium for hyperkalaemia on digoxin (old "stone heart" warning)

Digoxin Fab if available [Pincus 2016]: cardiac arrest, life-threatening arrhythmia, K+ > 5 in acute overdose, > 10 mg ingested; in chronic toxicity, arrhythmia not tolerated or symptoms with AKI. Without Fab: pacing for unstable bradycardia; magnesium for ventricular arrhythmia CHECK

Charcoal within 1 hr if awake [STG]. Digoxin is not dialysed.

DISPOSITION

Arrhythmia, K+ > 5 or acute overdose: monitored bed, discuss with GRH IM now. Mild chronic toxicity: ward with ECG and K+ monitoring; review the indication and dose before restarting.

Dose box CHECK

Atropine 0.5–1 mg IV, repeat to 3 mg.

Digoxin Fab (40 mg vial binds 0.5 mg digoxin) [Austin 2022]: acute, 5 vials IV, repeat once after 5–10 min; chronic, 2 vials IV, repeat if needed. Dose formulas overestimate. Levels are meaningless after Fab; watch for hypokalaemia. DECISION: no SA source; Australian doses

Activated charcoal 50 g orally.
