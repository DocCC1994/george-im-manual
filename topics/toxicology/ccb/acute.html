RECOGNISE

Hypotension in any CCB overdose; it can collapse within minutes [GRH 2024].

  • Non-dihydropyridine (verapamil, diltiazem): bradycardia, AV block, poor contractility, cardiogenic shock.
  • Dihydropyridine (amlodipine, nifedipine): vasodilatory shock with reflex tachycardia; bradycardia only when severe. Amlodipine needs more vasopressor.
  • Often alert until perfusion fails, then sudden deterioration. Hyperglycaemia in a non-diabetic points to CCB rather than BB. Lactic acidosis, hypokalaemia, pulmonary oedema [STG].
  • Slow-release: peaks 6–12 hrs, up to 24 hrs in overdose. Ask about co-ingested BB and paracetamol.
INVESTIGATE

Continuous ECG monitor; 12-lead ECG (PR, bradycardia, AV block) now and 12hrly. Glucose. VBG with lactate and K+; U&E, calcium, magnesium; paracetamol level at 4 hrs (and salicylate if intentional); CXR if crackles or hypoxia; pregnancy test.

RESUSCITATE

In resus: 2 large-bore lines, early central line. Reassess every 15 min and move to the next step if no response [GRH 2024]:

  1. Crystalloid (sodium chloride 0.9%) 20 mL/kg if not overloaded; stop at signs of pulmonary oedema.
  2. Atropine for bradycardia (dose box): often fails, so do not wait on it.
  3. Calcium gluconate 10% IV with ECG monitoring [STG].
  4. Shock or MAP < 65: adrenaline infusion, titrated up without a fixed ceiling (dose box). Not yet central? Start peripherally and place the line.
  5. No response to adrenaline in 15 min: high-dose insulin euglycaemic therapy (HIET), alongside the adrenaline; it takes 30–60 min to work. STG: HIET with inotropes is the mainstay [STG].
  6. Refractory shock: lipid emulsion, then methylene blue (dose box) DECISION: both from GRH 2024 only; the STG does not mention them

Bradycardia with low output despite this: dobutamine [STG] or transvenous pacing (capture often fails). No haemodialysis: CCBs are protein bound.

DECONTAMINATE

Activated charcoal only if < 1 hr, fully awake (or intubated) and not yet symptomatic. Whole bowel irrigation for slow-release tablets (dose box). Both raise vagal tone and can worsen bradycardia [STG].

MONITOR

Targets: MAP ≥ 65, HR ≥ 60, sinus rhythm, urine ≥ 1 mL/kg/hr, lactate < 2. On HIET: glucose every 15–30 min until stable, then hourly; K+ every 30 min until stable, then 1–2hrly; magnesium; calcium 2hrly after repeated calcium doses. Wean HIET last, slowly, after adrenaline is off; dextrose may be needed for hours after [GRH 2024].

DISPOSITION

Asymptomatic: monitor 6–8 hrs (immediate-release) or at least 24 hrs (slow-release). Symptomatic, or on HIET: ICU or high care [STG] → ICU referral. Psychiatry and social work once medically stable.

CALL

Discuss every CCB overdose with GRH IM [GRH 2024], now if hypotensive or bradycardic.
