RECOGNISE

Triad of seizures (often refractory to the usual drugs), high anion gap metabolic acidosis and coma [STG]. Seizures likely above 20 mg/kg [GRH 2021]. Ask who in the household is on TB treatment or TPT. Also tachycardia, fever after seizures, aspiration.

INVESTIGATE

Glucose; VBG or ABG with lactate (acidosis); U&E, CK, LFT; ECG (QRS, QTc); paracetamol level; CXR if aspiration. INH levels are not useful (too slow).

RESUSCITATE

ABC, glucose; intubate if GCS < 8 or ongoing seizures → ICU referral.

Seizures: pyridoxine is the antidote; a benzodiazepine (lorazepam) buys time until it is in [STG]. Phenytoin does not work; phenobarbital as an add-on if seizures persist or pyridoxine is delayed [GRH 2021] → Status epilepticus.

Charcoal within 1–2 hrs if awake or intubated. Haemodialysis only for a massive overdose without enough pyridoxine.

DISPOSITION

Seizures or intubation: ICU; discuss with GRH IM [GRH 2021]. Asymptomatic: observe 6 hrs CHECK. Psychiatry and social work once stable; the TB patient in the household needs their treatment replaced.

Dose box CHECK

Pyridoxine [STG]: 1 g per 1 g INH taken, maximum 5 g; amount unknown: 5 g. Crushed tablets orally or by NGT (5 g = 200 tablets of 25 mg). DECISION: GRH 2021 gives it IV at 0.5 g/min, repeatable, up to 70 mg/kg

Lorazepam 4 mg IV or IM, repeat once after 5–10 min (alternatives: diazepam, clonazepam, midazolam) [STG].

Activated charcoal 50 g orally.
