RECOGNISE

Bradycardia, AV block and hypotension, progressing to cardiogenic shock [STG]. Hypoglycaemia (CCB gives hyperglycaemia). Bronchospasm.

  • Propranolol: sodium-channel block, so wide QRS, seizures and coma.
  • Sotalol: long QT and torsades.

Ask about co-ingested CCB, digoxin or paracetamol.

INVESTIGATE

Continuous ECG; 12-lead for QRS and QTc. Glucose hourly; VBG with lactate and K+; U&E, calcium, magnesium; paracetamol level.

RESUSCITATE

As for CCB, step by step every 15 min → Calcium channel blockers: fluid, atropine, calcium gluconate, adrenaline, then HIET (same doses and bag) [STG]. Give dextrose with HIET from the start: BB poisoning is hypoglycaemic. Pacing or dobutamine for bradycardia with low output.

QRS > 120 ms (propranolol): sodium bicarbonate 8.4%. Torsades (sotalol): magnesium. Seizures: benzodiazepine → Status epilepticus. DECISION: bicarbonate and magnesium are not in STG §19.11

DECONTAMINATE

Activated charcoal binds BBs: within 1 hr, only if fully awake or intubated [STG]. Whole bowel irrigation for slow-release tablets.

DISPOSITION

Asymptomatic: monitor 6 hrs; 24 hrs for slow-release or sotalol CHECK. Symptomatic or on HIET: ICU or high care. Discuss every symptomatic BB overdose with GRH IM.

Dose box CHECK

Atropine, calcium, adrenaline, HIET, charcoal: CCB dose box.

Sodium bicarbonate 8.4% 1–2 mL/kg (1–2 mmol/kg) IV over 5 min, repeat to QRS < 120 ms.

Magnesium sulfate 2 g IV over 10 min for torsades.

Dextrose 50% 50 mL IV if glucose < 4.

Local notes · George

No glucagon at George (first-line BB antidote elsewhere). HIET up to 10 units/kg/hr is used.
