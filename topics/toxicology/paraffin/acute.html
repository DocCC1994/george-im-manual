RECOGNISE

Paraffin, petrol, turpentine, mineral turps; deliberate in adults, or siphoning petrol. The danger is aspiration: cough, choking, tachypnoea, wheeze, crackles and hypoxia within 6 h. Fever and CXR shadows in the first 48 h are chemical, not infection. Also drowsiness, seizures, coma, arrhythmias [STG 2024 §19.15]. Solvent sniffing (glue, toluene) is not covered here.

INVESTIGATE

SpO2, CXR (if symptomatic, or at 6 h; changes lag), blood gas if hypoxic, glucose, ECG.

IMMEDIATE
  • Remove clothes and wash skin. No emesis, no lavage, no activated charcoal.
  • Oxygen for hypoxia; bronchodilator (salbutamol) nebulised for wheeze.
  • No prophylactic antibiotics [STG 2024] and no steroids. Treat infection only if fever or new shadows appear after 48 h DECISION: ERH paediatric guideline gives amoxicillin to all with pneumonitis.
DISPOSITION

No symptoms, normal SpO2 and chest at 6 h after ingestion: discharge DECISION: STG gives no period; ERH paediatric guideline observes 24 h. Symptomatic: admit. Rising oxygen need or ARDS: discuss with GRH IM for ICU. Deliberate: psychiatry and social work.
