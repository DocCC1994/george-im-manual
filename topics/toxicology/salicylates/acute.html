RECOGNISE

Aspirin, or topical oil of wintergreen (methyl salicylate, almost 100%) [STG].

Mild to moderateSevere
Nausea, vomiting, tinnitus or deafness, fever, sweating, tachypnoea; respiratory alkalosisMetabolic acidosis, confusion or agitation, seizures, coma, non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema

Blood gas: early respiratory alkalosis, then a raised anion gap metabolic acidosis; both together are typical. A falling pH or a "normal" PCO2 in a tiring patient means severe toxicity. Unexplained acidosis with tachypnoea: think salicylate.

Dose: > 150 mg/kg toxic, > 300 mg/kg severe DECISION: STG gives no dose bands (international practice). Enteric-coated or slow-release tablets: toxicity can start or worsen late.

INVESTIGATE

Blood gas (pH, PCO2, HCO3, K+, lactate, glucose) now and 2hrly. U&E, INR, paracetamol level (co-ingestion). Salicylate level now and repeated until falling [STG]; it takes days at George, so treat on the clinical picture and the gas, never wait for it.

FIRST HOURS
  1. Avoid intubation if at all possible. A ventilated patient loses the hyperventilation that protects them: pH falls, more salicylate enters the brain, and cardiac arrest can follow. If intubation is unavoidable: senior intubates, bicarbonate bolus first, and ventilate at a high rate to match the pre-intubation PCO2 DECISION: STG silent (international practice).
  2. Glucose: treat hypoglycaemia; a normal glucose can still mean low brain glucose, so run dextrose-containing fluids [STG].
  3. Activated charcoal 50 g if the airway is safe; useful later than usual because gastric emptying is slow [STG]. Enteric-coated or slow-release: whole bowel irrigation [STG].
  4. Mild: rehydrate with dextrose-containing crystalloid (dose box), rate by fluid balance.
  5. Moderate or severe, or any acidosis: alkalinise with sodium bicarbonate (dose box) after discussing with the IM consultant [STG]. Targets: urine pH 7.5–8.5, blood pH 7.45–7.5, urine output 2 mL/kg/hr.
  6. Potassium: correct hypokalaemia early; the urine will not alkalinise while K+ is low [STG].
  7. Seizures: benzodiazepine (dose box), and check glucose and pH → Status epilepticus.
MONITOR

Blood gas with K+ and glucose 2hrly; urine pH 1–2hrly; fluid balance (pulmonary oedema). Salicylate level repeated until it falls.

DIALYSIS

Acidosis not responding to bicarbonate: haemodialysis [STG]. Also [EXTRIP 2015]: confusion, coma or seizures · new hypoxaemia needing oxygen · pH ≤ 7.20 · level > 7.2 mmol/L (> 6.5 with impaired kidney function) · deterioration despite treatment DECISION: EXTRIP criteria (STG names only refractory acidosis). The IM consultant decides → AKI.

DISPOSITION
AND CALL

Discuss with the IM registrar or consultant on call: every moderate or severe case, any acidosis, altered mental state, pulmonary oedema, before bicarbonate and before any intubation. ICU for pulmonary or cerebral oedema [STG] → ICU referral.

Medically fit when well, pH normal and the level falling. Self-harm: psychiatry and social work once medically stable.

Dose box CHECK

Dextrose-containing fluid (mild) [STG]: remove 100 mL from 1 L Ringer's lactate or sodium chloride 0.9% and add 100 mL dextrose 50%.

Sodium bicarbonate 8.4% (moderate, severe or acidosis) [STG]:

  • Bolus: 1–2 mL/kg (1–2 mmol/kg) IV over 30 min.
  • Then infusion: {{recipe:bicarbonate-salicylate}}. Titrate to the urine and blood pH targets.
  • Watch Na+, K+ and fluid overload.

Activated charcoal 50 g in 100 mL water [STG].

Seizures: benzodiazepine (lorazepam) 4 mg IV, or diazepam 10 mg IV over at least 5 min [STG §14.5].
