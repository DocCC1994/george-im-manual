RECOGNISE

Alkali: drain and oven cleaners (sodium hydroxide), hair straightener; deep injury and perforation. Acid: pool acid (hydrochloric), descalers; stomach injury. Bleach is usually mild. No mouth burns does not exclude oesophageal injury [Tygerberg ENT]. Red flags: stridor, hoarseness, drooling, painful swallowing, chest or abdominal pain, haematemesis, shock, acidosis.

INVESTIGATE

Airway first. Erect CXR and AXR (free air, mediastinal air); CT chest and abdomen if perforation is suspected. Blood gas (acidosis = severe injury), FBC, U&E, group and save.

IMMEDIATE
  • Stridor or hoarseness: early intubation by the most experienced person (oedema progresses); no blind nasal intubation.
  • Rinse the mouth with water. Nil by mouth, IV access and fluids [STG 2024 §19.16]. No charcoal, emesis or lavage; no blind NG tube; do not neutralise DECISION: Tygerberg ENT advises milk; STG silent.
  • Analgesia: opioid (morphine) IV. PPI (pantoprazole) IV DECISION: STG silent. No routine steroids or antibiotics DECISION: Tygerberg ENT gives steroids for grade 2 injury.
DISPOSITION

Suspected perforation: surgeons now. Persistent vomiting, drooling or dysphagia: admit; endoscopy at 24–48 h (surgery, daytime) [STG 2024]. Accidental, small amount, no symptoms and drinking without pain: discharge after observation DECISION: observation period (STG silent). Strictures can follow weeks later. Deliberate: psychiatry and social work.
