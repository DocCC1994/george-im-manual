INVESTIGATE

ECG (QRS, QTc), blood gas, U&E with Na+, glucose, albumin, paracetamol level at 4 hrs, drug level. Carbamazepine: repeat the level if the patient worsens or does not improve.

FIRST HOURS
  1. Airway: intubate for GCS ≤ 8, loss of airway reflexes or respiratory depression → ICU referral.
  2. Activated charcoal 50 g once the airway is safe or protected [STG]. Carbamazepine: repeat 50 g 4hrly (multiple doses) while bowel sounds are present; it removes absorbed drug [STG]. Stop if ileus or vomiting.
  3. Seizures: benzodiazepine (dose box) → Status epilepticus. No phenytoin in carbamazepine poisoning or with a broad QRS [STG].
  4. Carbamazepine with QRS > 100 ms or ventricular arrhythmia: sodium bicarbonate as for TCA (dose box) DECISION: extrapolated from TCA.
  5. Hypotension: sodium chloride 0.9% bolus; then adrenaline → Sepsis dose box for the George bag.
  6. Phenytoin IV toxicity: stop the infusion; fluids; atropine for bradycardia → Arrhythmias. Restart only after the level and ECG, no faster than 50 mg/min CHECK.
  7. Hyponatraemia → Hyponatraemia.
DIALYSIS

Carbamazepine [EXTRIP 2014]: haemodialysis for repeated seizures not controlled by treatment or life-threatening arrhythmia; consider it for prolonged coma or ventilation, or a rising level despite multiple-dose charcoal. Phenytoin [EXTRIP 2016]: rarely; only prolonged coma or disabling ataxia. The IM consultant decides DECISION: EXTRIP criteria.

MONITOR

GCS and airway; cardiac monitor while QRS is broad or the patient is drowsy; Na+ daily (carbamazepine). Ataxic: falls and aspiration risk, stay in bed until steady. Phenytoin: level daily until in range.

DISPOSITION
AND CALL

Discuss with the IM registrar or consultant on call: drowsy or worse, seizures, broad QRS or arrhythmia, hypotension, Na+ < 125 mmol/L, or dialysis being considered.

Medically fit when walking safely, alert and ECG normal; carbamazepine: improving over at least 2 checks (late peaks). Restart the antiepileptic at a lower dose once the signs have gone (phenytoin: level in range) and plan the review. Self-harm: psychiatry and social work once medically stable.

Dose box CHECK

Activated charcoal 50 g in 100 mL water [STG]. Carbamazepine: 50 g 4hrly.

Seizures: benzodiazepine (lorazepam) 4 mg IV, or diazepam 10 mg IV over at least 5 min [STG §14.5].

Sodium bicarbonate 8.4% (broad QRS, carbamazepine): 1–2 mL/kg IV bolus, then the infusion as in TCA; target pH 7.45–7.55.

Atropine 0.5 mg IV, repeat to 3 mg (bradycardia with IV phenytoin) CHECK

Therapeutic ranges CHECK lab ranges: carbamazepine 4–12 mg/L; phenytoin 10–20 mg/L (40–80 µmol/L).

Avoid
  • Phenytoin for seizures in either poisoning
  • Charcoal with an unprotected airway
  • Discharge on one good carbamazepine check (late peaks)
  • Restarting the full old dose
