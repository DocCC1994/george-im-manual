RECOGNISE

Sources: methanol in stove and model fuel, antifreeze, windscreen wash and illicit alcohol; ethylene glycol in radiator coolant, brake fluid and home-made drain cleaners [STG]. Ask family and friends; others may have drunk the same batch.

  • Both: first look drunk (vomiting, ataxia, drowsiness), then after a latent period (12–24 hrs for methanol, longer if ethanol was drunk too) coma, seizures, Kussmaul breathing and a high anion gap metabolic acidosis.
  • Methanol: blurred vision, scotomata, blindness, fixed dilated pupils.
  • Ethylene glycol: flank pain, haematuria, AKI; hypocalcaemia (tetany, long QT); oxalate crystals in urine.
INVESTIGATE

Blood gas, U&E, glucose, calcium, lactate, measured serum osmolality; urine microscopy for crystals; ECG; paracetamol level. Methanol, ethylene glycol and ethanol levels are not done at George.

Anion gap = Na − (Cl + HCO3), normal 8–16 [STG]. Osmolal gap = measured − (2 × Na + glucose + urea); > 25 suggests a toxic alcohol [GRH 2021]. Late presenters: the osmolal gap falls as the anion gap rises, so a normal osmolal gap does not exclude.

RESUSCITATE

ABC, glucose; seizures → Status epilepticus. Intubate if GCS < 8; intubation removes respiratory compensation, so match the patient's minute ventilation and recheck the gas CHECK. Sodium chloride 0.9% 1 L for hypotension, then bicarbonate (dose box) [GRH 2021]. No charcoal or lavage [STG].

ANTIDOTE

Oral ethanol blocks the toxic metabolites; fomepizole and IV ethanol are not available [GRH 2021]. Start if ingestion plus 2 of: pH < 7.3, HCO3 < 20, urinary oxalate crystals (ethylene glycol) or visual disturbance (methanol) [STG]. Methanol with acidosis or visual symptoms: start now. Titrate to the acidosis and clinical state (no levels); continue for days if needed; increase during dialysis. Watch for hypoglycaemia and airway loss.

Cofactors (dose box). Correct severe hypocalcaemia before bicarbonate: rapid alkalinisation can bring on seizures [STG].

DIALYSE

Early haemodialysis is the treatment of choice for severe poisoning with profound acidosis [STG]. GRH: high anion gap acidosis or end-organ damage (AKI, visual change). Continue until pH > 7.35; gas and K+ hourly [GRH 2021]. Keep ethanol running.

DISPOSITION

Discuss every suspected case with GRH IM [GRH 2021]; the IM consultant decides on dialysis. Acidotic, on ethanol or dialysis: ICU or high care. Visual symptoms: ophthalmology review once stable CHECK. Notify others who drank the same batch.

Dose box CHECK

Ethanol 20%, orally or by NGT [STG]. Never give commercial ethanol IV.

  • Make up: ethanol 96% 1 part + 4 parts water or juice (250 mL + 1 000 mL); or spirits 40–43% (gin, vodka, whisky) 1:1 (500 mL + 500 mL).
  • Load: 4 mL/kg over 15–30 min (60 kg: 240 mL).
  • Maintain: 0.5 mL/kg/hr; chronic drinker 1 mL/kg/hr (60 kg: 30 or 60 mL each hour).

DECISION: GRH 2021 loads 5 mL/kg over 60 min and maintains at 0.5 mL/kg/hr for all

Sodium bicarbonate 8.4% 50–100 mmol (50–100 mL) IV over 30–45 min [STG]. DECISION: GRH 2021 gives 1–2 mmol/kg if pH < 7.3, then dextrose 5% with 200 mL of 8.5% bicarbonate per litre at 150–250 mL/hr to pH > 7.35

Cofactors: thiamine 100 mg and pyridoxine 100 mg orally daily [STG]. DECISION: GRH 2021 adds folic acid 50 mg 6hrly (methanol), gives thiamine 100 mg IV 8hrly and pyridoxine 40 mg daily
