Staging from STG Table 23.1 [STG] [KDIGO].

Causes: often several together [STG]. Pre-renal: vomiting, diarrhoea, bleeding, sepsis, heart failure, cirrhosis (hepatorenal). Renal: ATN after shock · drugs: NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, contrast, tenofovir, high-dose co-trimoxazole, rifampicin · rhabdomyolysis · malaria · tumour lysis · glomerulonephritis, HIVAN · TTP-HUS (thrombocytopenia). Post-renal: prostate, cervical or bladder cancer, stones, blocked catheter.

INVESTIGATE

Now: blood gas (K+, pH, HCO3) · U&E, creatinine (compare with any previous value), Ca, Mg, phosphate · FBC, smear if platelets low · ECG if K+ high · urine dipstick before a catheter (blood and protein suggest glomerular disease; blood with no red cells: myoglobin) · bladder scan or catheter for retention.

By cause: CK · HIV · malaria test · LFT, glucose · blood cultures · urine PCR and microscopy · renal ultrasound if obstruction is possible or the cause is unclear (usual hours only).

IMMEDIATE
  1. Life threats first: hyperkalaemia, pulmonary oedema, severe acidosis.
  2. Volume and perfusion [STG]: dry patient: balanced salt solution in boluses, reassessed each time [STG]; target MAP ≥ 65. Overloaded: no IV fluid.
  3. Stop nephrotoxins (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, ACEi, ARB, MRA, metformin, contrast if avoidable) and dose-adjust every drug for eGFR [STG].
  4. Relieve obstruction: urinary catheter; hydronephrosis: discuss with urology.
  5. Fluid overload, dialysis not immediately available: a short trial of high-dose loop diuretic (furosemide), with the specialist [STG] CHECK dose. Diuretics do not prevent or treat AKI otherwise; nor do low-dose dopamine or NAC [STG].
ONGOING

Fluid balance chart and daily weight; urine output hourly if catheterised and unstable. Creatinine, K+ daily (more often if rising). Avoid hyperglycaemia; 20–30 kcal/kg/day, do not restrict protein to delay dialysis [STG]. Thromboprophylaxis: LMWH (enoxaparin) 20 mg daily if eGFR < 30 [STG]. No recovery: manage as CKD [STG].

REFER

Urgent dialysis: discuss with the IM consultant now [STG]:

  • pulmonary oedema refractory to medical therapy
  • metabolic acidosis pH < 7.15 refractory to medical therapy
  • K+ > 7 mmol/L refractory to medical therapy
  • uraemic pericarditis, encephalopathy or bleeding
  • a dialysable poison (lithium, methanol, ethylene glycol, carbamazepine, valproate: toxic alcohols, carbamazepine) [STG]

HIV is not a contraindication to acute dialysis [STG]. Suspected glomerulonephritis or no clear cause: discuss with the nephrologist.
