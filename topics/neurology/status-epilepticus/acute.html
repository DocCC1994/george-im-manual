RECOGNISE

Convulsive status epilepticus: a generalised or bilateral tonic-clonic seizure lasting ≥ 5 min, or ≥ 2 seizures without full recovery in between [STG]. Brain injury likely after 30 min. Refractory: continues after a benzodiazepine and an IV antiseizure medicine at the right dose [GRH 2022]. Not waking 20–30 min after the jerking stops, subtle twitching or eye deviation: suspect non-convulsive status (EEG not readily available, I9).

Causes [STG]: missed or low antiseizure medicine · hypoglycaemia, Na+, Ca2+, Mg2+, uraemia, liver failure · meningitis, encephalitis, toxoplasmosis, TB, neurocysticercosis · stroke, tumour, trauma · alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal · isoniazid, TCA, tik or cocaine · eclampsia · hypertensive encephalopathy.

INVESTIGATE

Glucose now. Blood gas · U&E, Ca, Mg, phosphate · FBC · LFT · HIV · syphilis (TPHA, not RPR) · drug levels if on phenytoin, valproate or carbamazepine (L7) · pregnancy test · urine toxicology · ECG [GRH 2022]. CT brain in all, once stable; LP only when safe: see first seizure.

0–5 MIN

Recovery position, nothing in the mouth, O2 to SpO2 ≥ 95%, two IV lines (phenytoin may not share a line with benzodiazepines or dextrose), cardiac monitor, time the seizure [STG] [GRH 2022]. Glucose < 4: dextrose 50% 50 mL IV (thiamine first if alcohol). Alcohol or malnutrition: thiamine 500 mg IV [GRH 2022].

5–10 MIN

Benzodiazepine (lorazepam) 4 mg IV, not faster than 2 mg/min [STG]; or midazolam 10 mg IV, or diazepam 10 mg IV over 5 min, or clonazepam 1 mg IV. No IV access: midazolam 10 mg IM or buccal (parenteral solution). Still seizing 5 min later: repeat once. Watch breathing; bag-mask if needed [STG] CHECK which benzodiazepines George stocks.

10–30 MIN

Load an IV antiseizure medicine [GRH 2022] [ESETT]:

  • Phenytoin 20 mg/kg IV with ECG monitoring; slow or pause it for arrhythmia or hypotension [STG]. Not for drug- or toxin-induced seizures or phenytoin toxicity: go straight to the next step [STG].
  • Or valproate 40 mg/kg IV (max 3 g) [GRH 2022] DECISION: GRH 2022, not in STG Table 1. Avoid in women who could become pregnant, pregnancy and liver disease [STG].
  • No IV access: levetiracetam 60 mg/kg (max 4.5 g), tablets crushed by NG tube [STG].

Load even if the benzodiazepine stopped the seizure [GRH 2022] DECISION: STG loads only if recurrence is likely. Still seizing at 15–20 min: further phenytoin 10 mg/kg, or the other drug; prepare to intubate [GRH 2022].

> 30 MIN

Refractory: RSI and ventilate, then infusion: midazolam 0.1–0.2 mg/kg IV bolus, then 0.05–0.5 mg/kg/hr (GRH prefers midazolam), or propofol 1–2 mg/kg, then 1.2 mg/kg/hr, titrated [STG]. Induce with midazolam or propofol; no suxamethonium; paralysis hides seizures [GRH 2022]. PaCO2 4.0–4.5 kPa. Continue 12–24 hrs after the last seizure, then wean [STG]. Consider pyridoxine [GRH 2022].

REFER

Call GRH IM at 10 min if still seizing. Refractory status: ICU per the GRH ICU SOP (ICU referral); EEG via neurology after the IM consultant (I9).

RECOGNISE

Was it a seizure? Eyewitness account or video: abrupt onset, stiffening then jerking, lateral tongue bite, incontinence, cyanosis, post-ictal confusion [STG]. Mimics: syncope (brief jerks are common; cardiac syncope: exertional, no warning, abnormal ECG), TIA, functional (dissociative) seizures, migraine, hypoglycaemia.

Provoked (acute symptomatic) seizure: a cause within days: metabolic, infection, stroke, head injury, alcohol or drug withdrawal, toxins, eclampsia [STG]. Epilepsy: ≥ 2 unprovoked seizures > 24 hrs apart, or 1 with a high (≥ 60%) recurrence risk, e.g. an old stroke [STG].

INVESTIGATE

Glucose, U&E, Ca, Mg, FBC, LFT, HIV, syphilis serology (TPHA), pregnancy test, urine toxicology, ECG (QT, heart block, Brugada) [GRH 2022].

CT brain: everyone with a first seizure [GRH 2022]. Urgent, before discharge: focal seizure or focal signs, not back to baseline, HIV-positive, head injury, anticoagulant, fever or headache, cancer, age > 40 DECISION: urgent-CT list (GRH says urgency depends on recovery and findings). HIV-positive: contrast CT for toxoplasmosis, TB (contrast mostly in daytime, I1).

LP (after CT, if safe) only [GRH 2022]: features of CNS infection (fever, headache, meningism, serum CrAg positive) · CT suggests it · all HIV-positive patients · serum TPHA positive. Otherwise no LP: the seizure itself can cause a mild CSF lymphocytosis and raised protein.

ADMIT OR DISCHARGE

Admit DECISION: criteria (not in STG or GRH 2022): not back to baseline · more than one seizure or status · focal signs or abnormal CT · HIV-positive (CT, LP, CD4) · a cause that needs treatment (infection, metabolic, withdrawal, poisoning) · pregnancy · injury · suspected cardiac syncope · no adult to observe at home.

Discharge when all are true: fully recovered, normal examination, normal glucose, Na+ and Ca2+, normal CT, HIV-negative, a responsible adult present. Advise no driving until reviewed CHECK SA legal period, no swimming alone, heights, open fires or machinery. Follow-up: MOPD or the clinic with the CT result.

ONGOING

Start an antiseizure medicine only if the criteria for epilepsy are met, or after discussion (e.g. structural lesion; continuing after one seizure may be advisable over 65) [STG]. Provoked seizure: treat the cause; wean any loading drug once it is corrected [STG]. Choice and dose: STG §14.6.1 CHECK George first line given M10. Women who could become pregnant: no valproate [STG].

Agitated after the seizure: post-ictal delirium, see delirium. Counsel the patient and family.
