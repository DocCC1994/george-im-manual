RECOGNISE

Delirium: acute (hours to days), fluctuating disturbance of attention and awareness, with disordered thinking, memory or perception; worse in the evening [STG]. Hyperactive (agitated, hallucinating) or hypoactive (quiet, drowsy: often missed, worse outlook). It is a medical emergency, not a psychiatric diagnosis.

Bedside screen: acute onset or fluctuating course, inattention (months of the year backwards), and either disorganised thinking or altered level of consciousness (CAM) DECISION: CAM screen (not in STG).

Risk: age > 65, dementia, previous delirium or falls, stroke or epilepsy, HIV, many comorbidities, anticholinergics, hypnotics, opioids, polypharmacy, substance use, severe illness [STG].

INVESTIGATE

DIMTOP [STG]: Drugs (intoxication, withdrawal, Wernicke) · Infection (sepsis, pneumonia, UTI, peritonitis, meningitis) · Metabolic (glucose, Na+, Ca2+, liver or kidney failure, CO2 narcosis) · Trauma (subdural) · Oxygen (hypoxia, carbon monoxide) · Pain, urinary retention, constipation, psychiatric.

Now: glucose · SpO2, blood gas · FBC · U&E, Ca, Mg, phosphate · LFT · CRP · HIV · urine dipstick · ECG (QTc before antipsychotics) · medication chart review. Then as indicated: blood cultures, CXR, syphilis serology, B12, TSH. CT brain: focal signs, head injury, anticoagulant, HIV, or no cause found. LP: fever, meningism or HIV-positive (after CT).

IMMEDIATE
  1. Treat the cause; stop or reduce sedatives, anticholinergics and unnecessary drugs [STG]. Correct hypoxia, dehydration, pain, retention and constipation.
  2. Alcohol use or malnutrition: thiamine 200 mg IM now [STG]; suspected Wernicke: see alcohol withdrawal.
  3. Calm room, the same staff, a family member at the bedside, glasses and hearing aids, day-night rhythm [STG].
  4. Agitated and a danger, despite the above: antipsychotic (haloperidol) 0.75–1.5 mg orally 12hrly, repeat 4hrly if needed (max 10 mg in 24 hrs); or 0.5–1 mg IM, repeat after 30–60 min, then 4hrly (max 10 mg in 24 hrs) [STG]. Lowest dose, shortest time (usually ≤ 7 days). Halve doses in the frail, elderly or with HIV.
  5. Haloperidol IM not available: olanzapine 2.5–5 mg orally (dispersible) or IM, repeat after 30–60 min, then 6hrly (max 20 mg in 24 hrs) [STG].
  6. Benzodiazepine only for alcohol or sedative withdrawal, Parkinson's disease or Lewy body dementia, or antipsychotic intolerance: lorazepam 0.25–1 mg IM 2–4hrly (max 3 mg in 24 hrs) [STG]. Benzodiazepines can worsen delirium.
ONGOING

Review the cause and doses daily; stop antipsychotics when settled. Restraint is a last resort, short, documented, with 15-min observations [STG]. Severe aggression: STG §15.1 CHECK (not read). Delirium can last weeks after the cause is treated; warn the family.

REFER

Call GRH IM for falling GCS, sepsis, focal signs or when sedation is needed repeatedly. Psychiatry and social work once medically stable (S13).

Watch for

Acute dystonia, other extrapyramidal effects, neuroleptic malignant syndrome (fever, rigidity, high CK) and respiratory depression [STG].

RECOGNISE

Time since the last drink: tremor, sweating, anxiety, tachycardia from 6–12 hrs · hallucinations and seizures from 12–48 hrs · delirium tremens (DT) usually day 2–3, peak around day 5: fluctuating confusion, visual hallucinations, agitation, fever, tachycardia, hypertension; mortality 1–5% [STG].

Score severity with CIWA-Ar: < 10 mild, 10–18 moderate, > 18 severe [CIWA-Ar] DECISION: CIWA-Ar and cut-offs (not in STG).

Wernicke encephalopathy: confusion, ataxia, nystagmus or ophthalmoplegia; the full triad is uncommon, so treat on suspicion. Look for: hypoglycaemia, head injury (subdural), GI bleed, pancreatitis, alcoholic hepatitis, hepatic encephalopathy, aspiration pneumonia, ketoacidosis, methanol, other sedative withdrawal.

INVESTIGATE

Glucose · blood gas (anion gap, ketones) · FBC (MCV, platelets) · U&E, Mg, phosphate, Ca · LFT, INR, lipase · CXR if febrile or hypoxic. CT brain: head injury, focal signs, first seizure or not improving (first seizure).

IMMEDIATE
  1. Thiamine before glucose, but never delay dextrose for hypoglycaemia [STG]. Suspected Wernicke: thiamine 500 mg IM daily for 3–5 days, then 100 mg orally 8hrly. At risk: 200 mg IM daily or 100 mg orally 8hrly for 14 days [STG].
  2. Benzodiazepine (diazepam) 10 mg orally now, then 5 mg 6hrly for 3 days, 5 mg 12hrly for 2 days, 5 mg daily for 2 days, then stop; higher doses may be needed [STG]. Symptom-triggered dosing by CIWA-Ar instead DECISION. Liver failure: lorazepam CHECK dose.
  3. Delirium tremens: diazepam 10 mg slow IV (< 5 mg/min, not IM); repeat after 5–10 min; then 10 mg every 5–10 min for 1–3 more doses, max 50 mg [STG]. No IV: clonazepam 2 mg IM, repeat after 60 min; or lorazepam 1–4 mg IM every 30–60 min. Aim for light somnolence, then keep it with diazepam 5–20 mg orally regularly. Cardiac monitor and SpO2 during large doses.
  4. Still agitated: add haloperidol 0.75–2.5 mg orally 12hrly (max 5 mg a day) for hallucinations; raising the benzodiazepine is preferred (haloperidol lowers the seizure threshold, prolongs QT) [STG]. No IM olanzapine.
  5. Fluids: replace deficits but avoid overhydration (a common error) [STG]. Replace K+, Mg2+ and phosphate; watch for refeeding.
  6. Withdrawal seizures: brief, generalised; treat with a benzodiazepine; no long-term antiseizure medicine [STG]. Status: status epilepticus.
REFER

Call GRH IM for DT, seizures, Wernicke, or > 50 mg IV diazepam without control: ICU for sedation and airway (ICU referral) CHECK: no phenobarbital at George (M10). On recovery: social services or a rehabilitation programme for every patient [STG]; psychiatry for comorbid mental illness (S13).
