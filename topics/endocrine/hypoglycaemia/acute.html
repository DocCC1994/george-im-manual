RECOGNISE

Act on the finger-prick glucose; confirm in the lab only if uncertain, and never delay treatment for it [STG]. Treat glucose < 4.0 mmol/L in anyone on insulin or a sulphonylurea [JBDS] DECISION: treatment threshold (STG gives none). Severe = needs another person's help, cannot swallow, fitting or unconscious.

Symptoms: anxiety, sweating, palpitations, hunger, headache, behaviour change. Signs: tremor, tachycardia, confusion, bizarre neurological signs (can mimic stroke), seizures, coma [STG]. BB and autonomic neuropathy blunt the warning signs.

Causes: insulin or sulphonylurea (missed meal, dose error, AKI or CKD, deliberate overdose) · alcohol · sepsis · liver failure (acute liver failure, paracetamol) · adrenal crisis, hypopituitarism · severe malaria (and quinine) · BB or salicylate poisoning · starvation · rarely insulinoma.

IMMEDIATE

Unconscious, fitting, or cannot swallow safely [STG]:

  1. Dextrose 50% 50 mL rapid IV into a large vein; flush (extravasation causes tissue necrosis).
  2. Recheck glucose and clinical state at 5–10 min. No response: a second 50 mL.
  3. Keep the line open with dextrose 10%; to prevent recurrence continue dextrose 10% about 1 L 6hrly.

JBDS prefers dextrose 10% 150–200 mL or 20% 75–100 mL over 15 min (less irritant) DECISION: dextrose 50% 50 mL (STG) or 10%/20% (JBDS) as first line CHECK JBDS volumes against the PDF

Awake and able to swallow: 15–20 g fast-acting carbohydrate orally (for example 150–200 mL fruit juice), recheck after 10–15 min, repeat up to 3 times, then a starchy snack or the next meal [JBDS] DECISION: oral route first (JBDS; STG silent) CHECK amounts

Alcohol use or malnutrition: thiamine 100 mg IV or IM with or straight after the dextrose; never delay the dextrose for it CHECK dose: STG gives no hypoglycaemia dose.

ONGOING

Once glucose is normal and the patient awake: glucose hourly for several hours; check K+ (hypokalaemia) [STG]. Snack once awake.

Not awake 30 min after a normal glucose: look for another cause of coma [STG] (head injury, stroke, post-ictal state or status, meningitis, overdose).

Sulphonylurea (glibenclamide, gliclazide): admit; prolonged IV dextrose; observe ≥ 12 hrs after the dextrose infusion stops [STG]. Recurrent despite dextrose: somatostatin analogue (octreotide) SC DECISION: octreotide for sulphonylurea hypoglycaemia (not in STG §8.6.1) CHECK dose and stock

Insulin overdose: dextrose infusion titrated to hourly glucose for as long as needed (long-acting insulin: often > 24 hrs); K+ 4–6hrly; psychiatry once medically stable → Poisoning.

BEFORE
DISCHARGE

Find the cause: dose too high, missed meals, poor adherence, alcohol, exercise, renal failure, hypoglycaemia unawareness, factitious insulin, Addison's disease or hypopituitarism [STG]. Adjust the insulin or sulphonylurea (avoid glibenclamide in CKD and the elderly CHECK STG §8.5). Teach the patient and family; give a written plan.

Not diabetic with no clear cause: discuss with the IM consultant; draw glucose, insulin, C-peptide and cortisol during a hypo if possible CHECK NHLS availability.
