Hypercalcaemia = albumin-corrected calcium above the upper limit of normal [STG]. Corrected Ca = measured Ca + 0.02 × (40 − albumin g/L) CHECK formula and George lab reference range. Severe or crisis: corrected Ca > 3.5 mmol/L, or any level with confusion, AKI or arrhythmia [SfE] DECISION: severity cut-offs (STG gives none; it treats > 3 mmol/L or symptoms with a bisphosphonate)

Features: thirst, polyuria, dehydration, nausea, vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain (pancreatitis), confusion, drowsiness, coma, weakness, renal colic, AKI; ECG: short QT, bradycardia, arrhythmia.

Causes: in hospital, malignancy (65%: myeloma, breast, lung, renal, bone metastases) and hyperparathyroidism (25%) [STG] · granulomatous disease (TB, sarcoid) · immobilisation with high bone turnover · vitamin D or calcium excess · drugs: thiazide, lithium.

INVESTIGATE

Same sample: PTH with calcium, phosphate, Mg, albumin, creatinine, Na+, K+, 25-hydroxy-vitamin D [STG]. A detectable PTH in hypercalcaemia means PTH-dependent disease [STG]; a suppressed PTH points to malignancy or granulomatous disease.

ECG · FBC, ESR, total protein and globulin, serum protein electrophoresis (myeloma) · CXR · amylase or lipase if abdominal pain CHECK myeloma screen available at George (SPEP, free light chains).

IMMEDIATE
  1. Rehydrate: sodium chloride 0.9% 4–6 L in 24 hrs for symptomatic hypercalcaemia; monitor urine output [STG]. Reduce in heart failure or oliguric AKI.
  2. Stop thiazides (raise Ca), calcium and vitamin D supplements, lithium. Furosemide has not been shown to help [STG]: only for fluid overload once rehydrated.
  3. Bisphosphonate (zoledronic acid) 4 mg IV over 15 min, specialist initiated, if still symptomatic after 24 hrs of adequate hydration, or if the first corrected Ca is > 3 mmol/L [STG]. eGFR 35–60: adjust the dose with a specialist; eGFR < 35: do not use [STG]. Takes 2–4 days to act CHECK.
  4. Granulomatous disease or haematological malignancy (myeloma, lymphoma): prednisone 40 mg orally daily, adjusted to response [STG].

Refractory, with AKI or eGFR < 35: haemodialysis with a low-calcium bath DECISION: dialysis for refractory hypercalcaemia (not in STG). Calcitonin is not on the EML CHECK.

ONGOING

Ca, U&E, Mg, phosphate 12hrly for 48 hrs, then daily CHECK interval. Fluid balance and weight daily; watch for overload, hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia. After bisphosphonate: hypocalcaemia, phosphate fall, flu-like reaction. Mobilise early.

REFER

Call GRH IM for confusion, Ca > 3.5 or AKI. Malignancy: discuss with the on-site haematology and oncology team. Confirmed hyperparathyroidism or no obvious cause: refer [STG]. Dialysis: the IM consultant decides.

RECOGNISE

Hypocalcaemia = albumin-corrected calcium below the lower limit of normal [STG]. Severe: corrected Ca < 1.9 mmol/L or ionised Ca < 1.0, or any level with symptoms [SfE] DECISION: severity cut-offs (not in STG). Use ionised Ca on the blood gas when albumin is low or pH is abnormal.

Features: perioral and finger paraesthesia, cramps, carpopedal spasm, tetany, Chvostek and Trousseau signs, laryngospasm, bronchospasm, seizures, confusion; ECG: long QT, arrhythmia; hypotension and heart failure.

Causes [STG]: renal failure · hypoparathyroidism (after neck surgery, radiotherapy, idiopathic) · vitamin D deficiency · hypomagnesaemia · malabsorption. Also: pancreatitis, rhabdomyolysis, tumour lysis, sepsis, massive transfusion (citrate), bisphosphonate, alkalosis (hyperventilation lowers ionised Ca).

INVESTIGATE

Ionised Ca on a blood gas · Ca, albumin, Mg, phosphate, U&E, creatinine · PTH (before treatment if feasible), 25-hydroxy-vitamin D · CK, amylase or lipase, uric acid by cause · 12-lead ECG (QTc) and cardiac monitor.

IMMEDIATE

Neurological features (tetany, seizures) or long QT [STG], with ECG monitoring:

  1. Calcium gluconate 10%: {{recipe:calcium-gluconate-bolus}}.
  2. Then calcium gluconate 10%: {{recipe:calcium-gluconate-maint}} [STG]. Repeat the bolus if symptoms recur.
  3. Correct magnesium if low [STG]: calcium will not normalise until Mg does. Magnesium sulfate: {{recipe:magnesium}} CHECK Mg dose for hypomagnesaemia (recipe is the asthma dose).

Cautions: use a large vein (extravasation burns); never in the same line as bicarbonate or phosphate (precipitates); on digoxin, give slowly with ECG monitoring CHECK. High phosphate (AKI, tumour lysis): give calcium only for symptoms, as calcium phosphate may precipitate DECISION: calcium in hyperphosphataemia (not in STG).

ONGOING

Ionised or corrected Ca 4–6hrly during the infusion CHECK interval. Start oral treatment early. Hypoparathyroidism: vitamin D analogue (alfacalcidol) 1–3 mcg orally daily and elemental calcium 500–1 500 mg daily in divided doses [STG]. Renal failure: as for CKD. Treat the cause.

REFER

Call GRH IM for seizures, laryngospasm, arrhythmia or long QT. Refer if the cause is uncertain or hypoparathyroidism is suspected [STG].
