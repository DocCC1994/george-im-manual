RECOGNISE

Thyroid storm (thyroid crisis) = thyrotoxicosis with organ decompensation: fever, tachycardia or fast AF, heart failure, agitation, delirium, seizures or coma, vomiting, diarrhoea, jaundice. A clinical diagnosis: do not wait for TFTs. Thyrotoxicosis with any other life-threatening illness (e.g. heart failure) is treated as thyroid crisis [STG].

Score: Burch-Wartofsky ≥ 45 suggests storm, 25–44 impending storm DECISION: use the Burch-Wartofsky score (not in STG) CHECK cut-offs.

Precipitants: infection (sepsis), stopping antithyroid drugs, surgery, trauma, iodine contrast, amiodarone, radioactive iodine, labour, DKA, ACS, PE.

INVESTIGATE

TSH, free T4 (free T3 if T4 normal) [STG]; results take about a day. Glucose · U&E, Ca, Mg · LFT · FBC (baseline WCC before thionamide) · blood cultures, urine, CXR · ECG (AF) · troponin if chest pain · pregnancy test.

IMMEDIATE

Supportive [STG]: oxygen, IV fluids (balanced salt solution, often several litres), active cooling (ice packs, cool fluids), paracetamol for fever. Avoid aspirin CHECK (not in STG).

  1. Block synthesis: thionamide (carbimazole) 40–60 mg orally (or by NG tube) 6hrly until the crisis is controlled [STG]. JTA/JES and ATA give a lower total, with PTU (propylthiouracil) preferred in the first trimester [ATA] DECISION: STG carbimazole dose (up to 240 mg/day) vs JTA/ATA (60–80 mg/day thiamazole) CHECK PTU stocked
  2. Block release: Lugol's iodine 10 drops in milk 8hrly, 30 min after the first carbimazole dose [STG] (ATA: at least 1 hr after) DECISION: interval 30 min (STG) or 1 hr (ATA)
  3. Block effects: BB (atenolol) 50 mg orally daily, up to 100 mg [STG]. ATA prefers propranolol 60–80 mg 4hrly (also lowers T3) DECISION: atenolol (STG) or propranolol (ATA). Caution in heart failure; asthma: CCB (diltiazem) for rate CHECK.
  4. Life-threatening: add hydrocortisone 100 mg IV 8hrly [STG].
  5. Treat the precipitant; AF: rate control and anticoagulation as in arrhythmias.
ONGOING

ICU admission is desirable [STG]. Temperature, HR, fluid balance, glucose, electrolytes. Reduce carbimazole and stop iodine as the crisis settles CHECK taper (not in STG). Carbimazole: stop and check a WCC with differential if fever or sore throat (agranulocytosis) [STG].

RECOGNISE

Myxoedema coma = severe hypothyroidism with altered mental state, lethargy, hypothermia, bradycardia, hypotension, hypoventilation (CO2 retention), and other organ failure; mortality up to 30%. Coma is not needed for the diagnosis and is a poor sign [STG].

Clues: known hypothyroidism off treatment, thyroidectomy scar or radioactive iodine, dry skin, non-pitting oedema, macroglossia, slow-relaxing reflexes, pericardial effusion (tamponade), hyponatraemia, hypoglycaemia.

Precipitants: infection, cold, sedatives and opioids, stroke, GI bleed, surgery, amiodarone, lithium.

INVESTIGATE

TSH, free T4 (high TSH in primary; low or normal TSH with low T4 suggests pituitary disease) · cortisol before hydrocortisone · glucose · blood gas (CO2) · U&E (Na+), CK · FBC · cultures, CXR · ECG (bradycardia, low voltage) · core temperature (low-reading thermometer) · echo or POCUS if effusion suspected.

IMMEDIATE
  1. Airway and breathing: low threshold for ventilation; beware upper airway oedema (macroglossia, supraglottic myxoedema) [STG].
  2. Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV 8hrly [STG], given before thyroxine in case adrenal insufficiency coexists CHECK order (not stated in STG).
  3. Levothyroxine 200 mcg orally (by NG tube) as a single loading dose, then 100 mcg daily [STG]. ATA uses IV levothyroxine 200–400 mcg, lower in the elderly and IHD DECISION: STG oral 200 mcg load vs higher IV load (not on EML)
  4. Passive rewarming (blankets); [STG]; active warming can cause vasodilatation and collapse.
  5. Correct glucose and sodium; avoid hypotonic fluids. Hypotension: sodium chloride 0.9% first, then adrenaline as in sepsis.
  6. Treat the precipitant: empirical antibiotics if infection is possible.
ONGOING

Monitor in ICU or EC resus: temperature, glucose, Na+, ventilation, ECG (ischaemia as metabolism rises). Avoid sedatives and opioids. Drugs are cleared slowly: reduce doses. Repeat free T4 after 1–2 weeks CHECK. Stop hydrocortisone once cortisol is normal CHECK (not in STG).

REFER

Call GRH IM now; ICU referral (ICU referral). Secondary hypothyroidism: discuss with endocrinology CHECK route.
