Causes to look for

Infection (including TB, and HIV-related infection) · missed or rationed insulin (supply, storage without a fridge, cost of transport) · new type 1 diabetes · MI or stroke · pancreatitis · pregnancy · alcohol · drugs: corticosteroids, atypical antipsychotics, SGLT2i, thiazides · stopped insulin during illness.

Complications

  • Hypokalaemia (the commonest serious one) and hypoglycaemia: keep dextrose running with the insulin once glucose < 14.
  • Fluid overload in the elderly and in cardiac or renal disease; hyperchloraemic acidosis after large saline volumes (a persistent low bicarbonate with a normal anion gap is not ongoing DKA).
  • Cerebral oedema: rare in adults, more likely with fast falls in sodium or osmolality.
  • VTE (especially in HHS), rhabdomyolysis (check CK in HHS), aspiration (NG tube if drowsy).
Tertiary options

1. ICU for severe DKA or HHS with shock, coma or ventilation needs, if a George ICU bed is not available CHECK S1.

2. Endocrinology advice (telephonic) for recurrent DKA, euglycaemic DKA, insulin pumps, or DKA in pregnancy (with obstetrics).

How to refer: centre and contact CHECK

Mitigation
  • No ketone meter: diagnose with urine ketones (≥ 2+); track resolution with pH, bicarbonate and the anion gap.
  • No pump or syringe driver: hourly IM soluble insulin (STG regimen).
  • Potassium results slow: VBG K+ 2hrly; ECG for hyper- or hypokalaemia; 20 mmol/L once passing urine.
  • No phosphate available: replace only if < 1.0 mmol/L with weakness, or cardiac or respiratory compromise.

Long-acting analogues and SGLT2i were not added to the EML in 2025, and IM glucagon was removed [NEMLC 2025].

Before discharge

  • Cause found and treated; HbA1c and HIV result known.
  • Sick-day plan: never stop insulin when ill; check glucose every 4 hrs; drink fluids; come in early with vomiting or drowsiness.
  • Insulin technique, storage (no fridge: cool, dark place), meter and strips, follow-up within 1–2 weeks.
  • Euglycaemic DKA on an SGLT2i: stop it; restart only after specialist review.
  • Recurrent DKA: insulin supply and cost, alcohol, depression, eating disorders; involve social work.
Monitoring targets
  • Glucose falls ≥ 3 mmol/L/hr (STG); in HHS no faster than 5–7 mmol/L/hr
  • Osmolality falls ≤ 3 mOsm/kg/hr [STG]
  • Na+ falls ≤ 10 mmol/L in 24 hrs (HHS)
  • K+ kept at 4–5 mmol/L
  • Urine output > 0.5 mL/kg/hr
