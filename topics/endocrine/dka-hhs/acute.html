RECOGNISE

DKA, all three [ADA/EASD/JBDS 2024]: D glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/L or known diabetes · K blood ketones ≥ 3.0 mmol/L or urine ketones ≥ 2+ · A pH < 7.3 and/or bicarbonate < 18 mmol/L.

HHS: glucose ≥ 33.3 mmol/L, effective osmolality > 300 mOsm/kg, ketones < 3.0, pH ≥ 7.3 and bicarbonate ≥ 15. Older, type 2 diabetes, days to weeks of illness, severe dehydration, confusion. Mixed DKA/HHS: treat as DKA.

Euglycaemic DKA: ketoacidosis with glucose < 14 mmol/L, on an SGLT2i, in pregnancy, or with starvation or alcohol.

INVESTIGATE

Capillary glucose hourly; if it reads "HI", send a formal glucose (grey tube). Blood ketones if a meter is available, otherwise urine ketones. VBG (pH, bicarbonate, K+, Na+, lactate), U&E, Mg, phosphate, FBC.

Find the cause: urine dipstick and culture, blood cultures, CXR, ECG (K+ effects, MI), pregnancy test, HbA1c, HIV; lipase if abdominal pain persists after the acidosis clears.

Anion gap = Na+ − (Cl− + HCO3−); normal about 12. Effective osmolality = 2 × Na+ + glucose.

FIRST HOUR
  1. ABC, two IV lines, urine output. NG tube if drowsy or vomiting.
  2. Fluid: 0.9% saline 15–20 mL/kg (about 1–1.5 L) in the first hour [STG 2025] DECISION: saline or Ringer's lactate (GRH 2024, ADA prefers balanced). Older, cardiac or renal patients: 250 mL boluses, reassess each.
  3. Check K+ before insulin. K+ < 3.5: give potassium first and hold insulin until ≥ 3.5 [ADA 2024] (GRH 2024 used 3.3).
  4. Insulin: short-acting insulin (soluble) IV infusion at 0.1 units/kg/hr, no bolus needed (table). Fluids must already be running.
  5. VTE prophylaxis for all: LMWH (enoxaparin) 40 mg SC daily. Vitamin (thiamine) 100 mg IV with the first litre (GRH 2024).
POTASSIUM

Add to each litre of fluid once urine output is adequate [STG 2025]: K+ < 3.5 → 40 mmol · 3.5–5.5 → 20 mmol · > 5.5 → none, recheck in 2 hrs. No result yet: 20 mmol/L once passing urine.

Maximum 20 mmol/hr and 40 mmol/L through a peripheral line DECISION: GRH 2024 limit; STG allows 40 mmol/hr. KCl 1 ampoule = 20 mmol = 10 mL.

CALL

Call the IM consultant now if: severe DKA · HHS · GCS falling or < 13 · K+ < 3.5 or > 6.0 at presentation · SBP < 90 after the first litre · oliguria after fluids · pregnancy · suspected sepsis, MI or pancreatitis · euglycaemic DKA · headache or falling GCS during treatment (cerebral oedema) · not improving after 4–6 hrs (GRH 2024).

Mitigation · when something is missing
  • No ketone meter: urine ketones to diagnose; follow pH, bicarbonate and anion gap on the VBG every 2–4 hrs.
  • No infusion pump: hourly IM insulin as in the dose box, in high care.
  • No high-care bed: mild DKA only on a ward with hourly glucose and 2hrly K+; call the consultant.

MONITOR
  • Glucose hourly; GCS and fluid balance hourly; ketones 2–4hrly if a meter is available.
  • VBG with K+ 1–2 hrs after starting insulin, then 2hrly until stable, then 4hrly. U&E, Mg and phosphate at 4–6 hrs.
  • Glucose should fall ≥ 3 mmol/L/hr. If not: check the line, pump and fluids, then increase the insulin [STG 2025: double the rate].
GLUCOSE < 14

Keep the insulin running: the ketosis takes longer to clear than the glucose. Add dextrose 10% (or 5%) alongside the saline; do not replace the saline with dextrose alone.

Target glucose 8–11 mmol/L in DKA and 11–14 mmol/L in HHS [ADA 2024]. Reduce insulin to 0.05 units/kg/hr DECISION: ADA 0.05 units/kg/hr vs STG 1–2 units/hr.

HHS
  • Fluid is the main treatment: 0.9% saline as above, more cautiously in the elderly.
  • Insulin 0.05 units/kg/hr; 0.1 if ketones ≥ 3.0 (mixed picture).
  • Slow correction: glucose fall ≤ 5–7 mmol/L/hr and Na+ ≤ 10 mmol/L in 24 hrs [ADA 2024]; osmolality ≤ 3 mOsm/kg/hr [STG 2025].
  • 0.45% saline only if osmolality is not falling despite adequate fluid DECISION: STG uses 0.45% if corrected Na+ > 140.
  • High VTE risk: prophylaxis for all DECISION: GRH 2024 preferred full-dose Enoxaparin in HHS.
RESOLVED

DKA: ketones < 0.6 mmol/L and pH ≥ 7.3 or bicarbonate ≥ 18. Without a ketone meter: pH ≥ 7.3, bicarbonate ≥ 18 and a normal anion gap. HHS: osmolality < 300, glucose < 14, urine output > 0.5 mL/kg/hr and better cognition [ADA 2024].

SWITCH TO SC INSULIN
  • When resolved and eating: give SC insulin with a meal and stop the IV infusion 1–2 hrs later [STG 2025].
  • Known diabetes: restart usual doses. New diagnosis: 0.5–1 unit/kg/day; biphasic 30/70 two-thirds before breakfast and one-third before supper, or pre-meal short-acting insulin (soluble) plus intermediate-acting insulin (isophane) at night (Reference Manual).
DISCHARGE

Cause treated · insulin technique, meter and strips checked · never stop insulin when ill (sick-day plan) · SGLT2i stopped · clinic review in 1–2 weeks · recurrent DKA: ask about insulin supply, storage, alcohol and mood → Reference Manual.

Avoid
  • Stopping insulin because the glucose is normal
  • Replacing saline with dextrose 5% alone (sodium falls)
  • Insulin before K+ is known, or with K+ < 3.5
  • Routine bicarbonate: consider only if pH < 7.0, after discussion DECISION: STG says no role
  • Big boluses in the elderly, or fast sodium falls in HHS
  • Stopping IV insulin before the first SC dose has had 1–2 hrs
  • Missing the cause: infection, MI, pregnancy, missed insulin, SGLT2i
Tertiary option

ICU for severe DKA or HHS with falling GCS, shock or ventilation needs. Cerebral oedema: osmotic agent (mannitol) or hypertonic saline, ICU and CT; it is rare in adults.
