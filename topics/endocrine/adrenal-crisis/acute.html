RECOGNISE

Adrenal crisis = acute cortisol deficiency: shock that responds poorly to fluids and adrenaline, with any of: depressed mentation, fever, vomiting, abdominal pain, weakness, dehydration [STG]. Not every feature occurs: a high index of suspicion is needed.

Think of it in a thin, hypotensive, hypoglycaemic patient, in shock during stress (sepsis, surgery, vomiting), and when hyponatraemia with hyperkalaemia appears [STG]. Chronic clues: hyperpigmentation, weight loss, postural dizziness.

Causes: stopping long-term steroids (oral, high-dose inhaled, intra-articular) or failing to increase them in illness, the commonest [STG] · adrenal destruction: disseminated TB, fungal infection, HIV-related infection (CMV) · adrenal haemorrhage: meningococcal sepsis (meningitis), anticoagulation · pituitary disease · rifampicin started in a patient on steroid replacement (faster cortisol clearance) CHECK rifampicin dose adjustment (not in STG).

INVESTIGATE

Never delay treatment for a result [STG]. Before the first hydrocortisone, if feasible: random serum cortisol (and plasma ACTH) [STG].

Now: bedside glucose · blood gas (pH, K+, Na+, glucose) · U&E, creatinine · FBC · blood cultures, CRP, CXR, urine, and an LP if meningitis is possible (sepsis) · ECG for K+.

Cortisol (in crisis, any time): > 500 nmol/L virtually excludes it (> 450 with newer assays) · 100–450 indeterminate: ACTH stimulation test once stable (ACTH depot 1 mg IM, cortisol at 60 min; normal > 550 or double the baseline) [STG]. Hydrocortisone is measured as cortisol: draw before the dose.

IMMEDIATE
  1. Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV now (IM if there is no IV access), then 100 mg IV 6hrly [STG]. ESE/ES 2024: 100 mg, then 200 mg/24 hrs as a continuous infusion or 50 mg 6hrly [ESE/ES] DECISION: 100 mg 6hrly (STG) or 50 mg 6hrly / 200 mg per 24 hrs (ESE/ES)
  2. Sodium chloride 0.9% guided by BP; the deficit is often several litres [STG]. ESE/ES: 1 L in the first hour DECISION: first-hour volume CHECK ESE/ES figure against the PDF
  3. Glucose: check often; dextrose 50% boluses if needed (hypoglycaemia). Beware overload if sodium chloride 0.9% / dextrose 5% is used [STG].
  4. Treat the precipitant: antibiotics for sepsis within the hour.

Shock persisting after hydrocortisone and fluids: adrenaline as in sepsis; refer to ICU. Hyperkalaemia usually corrects with hydrocortisone and fluids; treat it as hyperkalaemia if severe. Hyponatraemia: steroid and fluid correct it fast; watch the rate of rise (hyponatraemia).

ONGOING

Na+, K+ and glucose 4–6hrly on day 1 CHECK interval. When stable and eating, change to oral maintenance [STG]: hydrocortisone 10 mg in the morning and 5 mg at night (up to 20 + 10 mg), or prednisone 5 mg daily (maximum 7.5 mg). Taper from stress dose over 1–3 days CHECK taper (not in STG). Primary adrenal insufficiency: add fludrocortisone 50–100 mcg daily, titrated with a specialist [STG].

Before discharge: sick-day rules: double the oral dose for minor illness (flu, diarrhoea, chest infection) until well; 100 mg IV 6hrly for surgery, trauma or severe illness [STG]. Steroid card; tell the patient never to stop steroids suddenly.

REFER

Call GRH IM for every suspected crisis. ICU referral for shock needing adrenaline (ICU referral). Every suspected case needs full evaluation before lifelong replacement [STG]: discuss with the IM consultant; endocrinology referral CHECK George endocrinology route (tertiary centre).
