RECOGNISE

Hyponatraemia = Na+ < 135 mmol/L [STG]; profound < 125 [ESE 2014]. A water problem, not a sodium problem [GRH 2024]. Symptoms decide the urgency, not the number.

Severity [STG]Features
SevereSeizures, obtundation, coma, respiratory arrest (also vomiting with confusion) → hypertonic saline now
Mild to moderateHeadache, nausea, vomiting, fatigue, gait disturbance and falls, confusion

Acute (< 48 hrs: water intoxication, post-operative, ecstasy, beer potomania binge) tolerates faster correction. Chronic or unknown onset: treat as chronic and correct slowly.

Rule out hyperglycaemia (→ DKA/HHS) and pseudohyponatraemia (high lipids or protein: myeloma) [STG].

INVESTIGATE

U&E, glucose, urea and creatinine · K+, Mg, phosphate · calculated osmolality = 2 × Na + urea + glucose [GRH] · TSH · cortisol if adrenal insufficiency is possible · LFT, albumin · CXR (pneumonia, TB, mass) · HIV test.

Assess volume at the bedside (STG approach, Figure 7.2): BP and postural drop, JVP, oedema, ascites, crackles, mucosa; POCUS (IVC, B-lines) in EC or ICU. Urine sodium (< 20 or > 20 mmol/L) and osmolality are on the STG algorithm but effectively unavailable at George: send them, never wait for them.

IMMEDIATE

Severe symptoms (any Na+; STG: Na+ < 120 or acute water intoxication): sodium chloride 5% 60 mL IV bolus over 15 min (raises Na+ by about 2–3 mmol/L). Symptoms persist or Na+ not rising: discuss with a specialist [STG]. GRH: 50 mL boluses, up to 3 [GRH] DECISION: bolus 60 mL over 15 min (STG) or 50 mL up to 3 times (GRH)

Target: up 4–6 mmol/L, then stop boluses; this rise alone usually stops the seizures. Never more than 8 mmol/L in 24 hrs [STG].

Mild to moderate symptoms with Na+ < 120 (chronic): sodium chloride 5% 30 mL IV over 15 min [STG], then treat by volume status (page 2). Seizing: also a benzodiazepine → Status epilepticus. Protect the airway.

No or minimal symptoms: no hypertonic saline. Stop the culprit drug, treat by volume status (page 2), recheck Na+ in 4–6 hrs.

Dose box CHECK

Sodium chloride 5% (855 mmol/L) [STG Table 7.2]:

  • Severe symptoms: 60 mL IV bolus over 15 min.
  • Mild to moderate, Na+ < 120: 30 mL IV bolus over 15 min.
  • Expect 2–3 mmol/L rise per 60 mL.

Through a large vein; use a syringe or pump, never free flow. Recheck Na+ (blood gas) after each bolus.

Sodium chloride 0.9% (dry, Na+ > 120, mild or no symptoms): 1 L 8hrly [STG].

Avoid
  • Correcting chronic hyponatraemia by > 8 mmol/L in 24 hrs
  • Sodium chloride 0.9% in SIADH (Na+ can fall further)
  • Dextrose 5% or hypotonic fluids
  • Waiting for urine sodium or osmolality

BY VOLUME
STATUS

Dry: rehydrate with sodium chloride 0.9% [STG]. As volume is restored the kidneys pour out water and Na+ can rise fast: check Na+ and urine output 2–4hrly.

Normal volume (SIADH): fluid restriction (about 800–1 000 mL a day CHECK volume), treat the cause (antibiotics, TB treatment), stop the culprit drug [GRH]. Hypothyroidism: levothyroxine; cortisol deficiency: hydrocortisone [GRH].

Overloaded: water and salt restriction; loop diuretic (furosemide) 40 mg orally 12hrly, increased to control overload and improve the Na+ [STG]. Treat the heart, liver or kidney disease.

Volume unclear and not overloaded: a trial of 1 L sodium chloride 0.9%, then recheck Na+: it rises if dry, falls in SIADH DECISION: saline challenge when urine sodium is unavailable (not in STG or GRH)

ONGOING

Na+ and urine output 2–4hrly while Na+ is being corrected or urine output is high [GRH]. Correct K+ (K+ replacement also raises Na+).

Hypertonic infusion after the boluses: GRH continues sodium chloride 5% at 0.5 mL/kg/hr with Na+ 2–4hrly until normal, within the limits [GRH]; STG: rate set with a specialist DECISION: GRH infusion or bolus-only (STG) CHECK recipe for recipes.yaml if kept

Overcorrection (above the limit): stop the saline; dextrose 5% IV or tap water by NG tube at 3 mL/kg/hr until back within target; vasopressin analogue (desmopressin) 2 mcg IV or SC 6hrly if available [GRH] CHECK desmopressin injection stock.

ODS (days later: dysarthria, dysphagia, quadriparesis, locked-in) is often irreversible [STG]: prevention is the only treatment.

REFER

Discuss with the IM consultant before any hypertonic saline (STG: consult a specialist before IV sodium chloride when not overloaded) [STG], and for: severe symptoms, Na+ < 120, overcorrection, or no rise after 24 hrs. Hypertonic infusion or 2hrly Na+: EC resus or ICU level of monitoring CHECK where (no medical HOCU).

Mitigation · when something is missing
  • No urine sodium: bedside volume, POCUS, response to 1 L sodium chloride 0.9% (DECISION above).
  • No hypertonic saline on hand: seizure control and airway; call pharmacy; do not improvise with 0.9% in severe symptoms CHECK
  • No ICU bed: EC resus with Na+ on the blood gas 2hrly.
Pearls
  • Thiazides (HCTZ) are a common cause in older women: stop and do not restart.
  • A sudden large urine output during treatment means the Na+ is about to overshoot.
  • Beer potomania, tea-and-toast diet and malnutrition: high ODS risk.
  • In HIV, think TB, PJP, meningitis and adrenal insufficiency.
