Why PE kills, and who gets it (ESC 2019, §3)

Clot raises pulmonary vascular resistance; the thin-walled RV dilates, its wall tension and oxygen demand rise, septal shift squeezes the LV, and cardiac output falls. RV ischaemia then makes the spiral worse, which is why RV dysfunction and troponin predict early death, and why fluid loading and positive-pressure ventilation can tip a borderline patient into shock. Risk factors: surgery, fracture, immobility, cancer, oestrogen, pregnancy, previous VTE; none in about 40%. In South Africa, HIV (2–10 × risk) and TB add to the burden and mimic PE (tachycardia, pleurisy, haemoptysis). Public-sector CTPA yields are 30–33% (Tygerberg, Baragwanath, Mitchell's Plain), against 13% in North American EDs.

Pre-test probability (ESC 2019, §4); AHA/ACC 2026, §3

  • Wells and revised Geneva perform alike: low 10%, intermediate 30%, high 65% prevalence; two-level PE unlikely 12%, likely 30%. Gestalt is acceptable but less standard. In HIV/TB, scores can over-call (tachycardia, haemoptysis) and under-call: in one Cape Town cohort 71% of confirmed PE were "unlikely" by simplified Geneva. No score is validated locally.
  • YEARS (3 Wells items plus a sliding D-dimer cut-off of 1 000 or 500 µg/L) avoided CTPA in 48% vs 34% with Wells, missing 0.6% at 3 months. Pregnancy-adapted YEARS is the only rule tested in pregnancy (AHA 2b).
  • PERC (age < 50, HR < 100, SaO2 > 94%, no leg swelling, haemoptysis, recent surgery, previous VTE or oestrogen) rules out testing only where PE prevalence is low; not suitable for George's referral population.
  • D-dimer: laboratory (ELISA-type) sensitivity ≥ 95%; point-of-care 88%, so only for low probability. Age-adjusted cut-off raised exclusion in over-75s from 6% to 30%. Number needed to test rises from 3 to > 10 in inpatients, cancer, infection and pregnancy. Never in high probability.

Imaging

  • CTPA: diagnostic at segmental level or above. Isolated subsegmental clot: possible false positive; review with the radiologist, check the legs, and treat if DVT or cancer (ESC IIb). Report the RV/LV ratio (≥ 1.0 = RV dysfunction). CT venography adds radiation, not yield.
  • V/Q: normal perfusion excludes PE; useful in pregnancy, contrast allergy or renal failure, if available.
  • Echo: not for diagnosis in stable patients (normal in many), but in shock a dilated RV with no other cause justifies lysis (ESC Figure 4). Leg ultrasound: proximal DVT confirms VTE in 30–50% of PE.
  • Pregnancy: leg ultrasound first if leg symptoms; CXR; then CTPA (abnormal CXR) or perfusion scan. Fetal dose is low with either; withholding a needed scan is the bigger risk.

CTPA: RV/LV diameter ratio ≥ 1.0. Echo: RV/LV > 1.0, TAPSE < 16 mm, McConnell sign, flattened septum, 60/60 sign, right heart thrombus. Biomarkers: troponin above the 99th centile; NT-proBNP ≥ 600 ng/L adds prognosis but not a treatment decision.

Anticoagulation in practice

  • Start before imaging when probability is intermediate or high and bleeding risk is low (ESC I C; AHA 2a for category C2 or worse). Withholding anticoagulation outside a validated pathway raised VTE and sudden death at 3 months.
  • Intermediate–high risk: in PEITHO, decompensation came at a mean of 1.8 days, so keep LMWH for 2–3 days before switching. Full-dose lysis halved decompensation but caused 2% stroke and more major bleeding; rescue lysis on deterioration gives most of the benefit.
  • DOAC vs VKA: equal recurrence (2.0 vs 2.2%), less major bleeding (RR 0.60) and intracranial bleeding (RR 0.37). Rivaroxaban needs no LMWH lead-in; the 15 mg 12hrly phase lasts 21 days. After 6 months of extended treatment ESC allows 10 mg daily.
  • TB and HIV: rifampicin lowers rivaroxaban and warfarin levels; ritonavir raises rivaroxaban. On rifampicin, use LMWH or warfarin with close INR checks CHECK with pharmacist; dolutegravir has no listed interaction.
  • Warfarin at George: 5 mg daily for 4 doses, INR on day 5, then by the GRH table; no loading doses (GRH warfarin protocol 2012). STG: INR at 48 hrs, then every 1–2 days. ESC: LMWH until INR 2–3 on 2 consecutive days.
  • APS (triple positive): warfarin indefinitely, never a DOAC. HIT: stop heparin, discuss with haematology (STG). IVC filter: only for PE with an absolute contraindication to anticoagulation, retrievable.
Recurrence risk off treatment

Low (< 3%/yr): surgery > 30 min, admission ≥ 3 days, fracture → stop at 3 months.

Intermediate (3–8%): minor surgery, short admission, oestrogen, pregnancy, long-haul travel, IBD, autoimmune disease, no cause.

High (> 8%): active cancer, previous unprovoked VTE, APS (ESC Table 11).

Follow-up

Review at 3–6 months for every patient. Persistent dyspnoea is common; CTEPH is uncommon (0.1–9% in the first 2 years). Symptomatic with mismatched perfusion defects after 3 months → CTEPH centre (ESC I C). AHA: ask about symptoms at every visit for 1 year.

Tertiary options

Catheter-directed thrombolysis or thrombectomy, surgical embolectomy and ECMO (with embolectomy): Western Cape tertiary centres only CHECK which.
