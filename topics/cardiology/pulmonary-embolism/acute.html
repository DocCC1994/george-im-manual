RECOGNISE

Dyspnoea (often sudden), pleuritic chest pain, syncope or presyncope, haemoptysis, unexplained tachycardia or hypoxaemia. In heart or lung disease, worse dyspnoea may be the only sign. Up to 40% have a normal SpO2; 40% have no risk factor [ESC 2019].

First ask: is the patient unstable? Cardiac arrest · SBP < 90 (or a fall ≥ 40) for > 15 min · vasopressor needed · with poor perfusion (confusion, cold skin, oliguria, lactate up), not from arrhythmia, bleeding or sepsis → high-risk PE, page 2 now.

Mimics and second problems (common at George): pneumonia, TB, pneumothorax, ACS, aortic dissection, tamponade, heart failure. HIV and TB both raise VTE risk and mimic PE [SA cohorts].

PROBABILITY

Score every stable patient before imaging (Wells, side box) [ESC I A] DECISION: Wells two-level (Calgary, ESC) or YEARS (fewer CTPAs; ESC IIa, AHA 2a)

  • PE unlikely (Wells ≤ 4) → D-dimer. Below the cut-off: PE excluded, look for another cause. Raised → CTPA. Age-adjusted cut-off over 50 years (age × 10 µg/L FEU) [ESC IIa B] CHECK NHLS assay, units and turnaround
  • PE likely (Wells > 4) → no D-dimer; go straight to CTPA.
  • D-dimer is raised in most inpatients, cancer, infection, TB and pregnancy: low value there.
INVESTIGATE

CTPA: segmental or larger clot = PE; normal CTPA excludes PE in a PE-unlikely patient [ESC I A]. A single subsegmental clot: review with the radiologist before treating.

Leg compression ultrasound if DVT signs: a proximal DVT plus symptoms is enough to treat. Also ECG (sinus tachycardia, RV strain, new RBBB, AF), CXR, FBC, U&E (eGFR sets the dose), INR, LFT, HIV, troponin. β-hCG in women of child-bearing age.

WHILE WAITING

PE likely and no active bleeding → therapeutic LMWH (enoxaparin) now, before CTPA [ESC I C]. Oxygen if SpO2 < 90%. Avoid intubation if at all possible (RV collapse).

sPESI (1 point each)

Age > 80 · cancer · chronic heart failure or lung disease · HR ≥ 110 · SBP < 100 · SaO2 < 90%. 0 = low risk (30-day mortality 1%); ≥ 1 = 11% [ESC].

Mitigation · no CTPA soon
  • After hours, at a district hospital or awaiting transfer: PE likely → therapeutic LMWH now and arrange CTPA; don't wait for a D-dimer result.
  • Leg ultrasound: proximal DVT with chest symptoms → treat as VTE.
  • Bedside echo: a dilated RV supports PE; a normal RV does not exclude it in a stable patient.
  • Unstable and too sick to move: an RV dilated on echo with no other cause is enough to lyse [ESC].

HIGH RISK
  1. Call the IM consultant now. Resuscitation area, monitor, two IV lines. Do not transfer before stabilising [AHA 3: Harm].
  2. UFH IV bolus now, then infusion [ESC I C].
  3. Fibrinolysis unless contraindicated (side box): fibrin-specific fibrinolytic (alteplase) [ESC I B] DECISION: alteplase (ESC, AHA) or non-fibrin-specific fibrinolytic (streptokinase, cheaper; first-line for STEMI at George). Pause UFH during the infusion; restart when aPTT < 2 × control CHECK.
  4. Fluids: at most 500 mL over 15–30 min, only if the IVC is small; more fluid dilates the RV and worsens shock.
  5. Vasopressor (norepinephrine) for persistent hypotension; add inotrope (dobutamine) for low output [ESC IIa C].
  6. Oxygen or high-flow nasal oxygen; intubate only if unavoidable, with a vasopressor running and no hypotensive induction agent.
  7. Cardiac arrest from suspected PE: give the fibrinolytic and continue CPR for 60–90 min [ESC].

Lysis contraindicated or failed (still unstable at 36 hrs): tertiary option (side box).

INTERMEDIATE

LMWH (enoxaparin), not routine lysis [ESC III B]. Intermediate–high: high care with BP and HR monitoring for 2–3 days, LMWH throughout, then oral agent. Deteriorates → rescue fibrinolysis [ESC I B] DECISION: where to monitor; discuss transfer for catheter therapy (AHA 2b).

LOW RISK

Early discharge (within 24–48 hrs) if sPESI 0, RV normal on CTPA or echo, no Hestia reason to stay (oxygen, IV analgesia, bleeding risk, eGFR < 30, pregnancy, social), oral agent in hand and follow-up booked [ESC IIa A] DECISION: adopt at George.

ORAL AGENT

DOAC (rivaroxaban) for 3 months is first choice [STG 2024]. Not if: eGFR < 30 · pregnancy or breastfeeding · antiphospholipid syndrome · active cancer · weight > 120 kg or BMI > 40 · rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin or a ritonavir-boosted PI · age > 65 (STG) DECISION: STG excludes age > 65; ESC and AHA do not.

Otherwise VKA (warfarin) with LMWH overlap: LMWH for ≥ 5 days and until INR 2–3 for ≥ 24 hrs [STG]. Also for long-term treatment (> 6 months) and APS.

Pregnancy: LMWH only; involve obstetrics. Active cancer: LMWH for 6 months [ESC IIa A] DECISION: LMWH or warfarin.

DURATION

At least 3 months for all. Stop at 3 months after a major transient cause (surgery, admission ≥ 3 days, fracture). No cause, recurrence or persistent cause → discuss extended (indefinite) treatment with the consultant DECISION: STG: discuss with a specialist · ESC IIa, AHA class 1: extend.

FOLLOW-UP

Review at 3 months: bleeding, adherence, stop or continue. Dyspnoea or poor exercise tolerance after 3 months → echo and refer for CTEPH work-up [ESC I C].

UFH 80 U/kg IV bolus, then 18 U/kg/h; aPTT 1.5–2.5 × control at 6 hrs CHECK: ESC supplement.

Fibrin-specific (alteplase) 100 mg IV over 2 hrs; cardiac arrest: 0.6 mg/kg over 15 min (max 50 mg). Non-fibrin-specific (streptokinase) 1.5 million IU over 2 hrs; stop UFH during it [ESC].

DOAC (rivaroxaban) 15 mg orally 12hrly with food for 21 days, then 20 mg daily for 3 months [STG].

VKA (warfarin) 5 mg orally daily (lower if > 70 yrs or INR raised); INR on day 5; target 2–3 (GRH warfarin protocol).

Norepinephrine 0.2–1.0 µg/kg/min · dobutamine 2–20 µg/kg/min [ESC].

No fibrinolysis if

Absolute: haemorrhagic or unknown stroke ever · ischaemic stroke < 6 months · CNS tumour · major trauma, surgery or head injury < 3 weeks · bleeding disorder · active bleeding.

Relative: TIA < 6 months · oral anticoagulant · pregnancy or first week postpartum · non-compressible puncture · traumatic CPR · SBP > 180 · advanced liver disease · endocarditis · active peptic ulcer [ESC].

Tertiary option

Surgical embolectomy, catheter-directed therapy or ECMO when lysis is contraindicated or fails [ESC I C / IIa C]: tertiary centres only; which, and how to refer CHECK.
