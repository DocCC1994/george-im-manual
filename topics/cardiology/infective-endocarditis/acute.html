RECOGNISE

Think of IE in any fever with: a new or changed murmur · rheumatic or other valve disease · a prosthetic valve, pacemaker or previous IE · IV drug use · an embolic event (stroke in a young patient, limb ischaemia, splenic or renal infarct) · S. aureus, viridans streptococcus or E. faecalis in a blood culture.

Look for: splinter haemorrhages, Janeway lesions, Osler nodes, Roth spots, conjunctival haemorrhages, splenomegaly, clubbing, haematuria, new HF, new heart block.

In SA, patients are young, often have RHD, and present late. S. aureus is now the commonest organism, and up to 43% are culture-negative (prior antibiotics; Bartonella most often) [Engelbrecht 2026].

INVESTIGATE
  1. Three sets of blood cultures before any antibiotic, from separate venepunctures, ideally 30–60 min apart (all three within 1 h if the patient is septic). Fill each bottle properly CHECK NHLS volume per bottle
  2. FBC, U&E, CRP, LFT, HIV, urine dipstick and microscopy (haematuria, casts), ECG (new PR prolongation or AV block suggests an aortic root abscess), CXR.
  3. Echo early: TTE first I B. TOE if TTE is negative and suspicion stays high, if there is a prosthetic valve or device, or to look for complications I B. Repeat in 5–7 days if negative but suspicion persists I C.
  4. Save serum. If cultures are negative at 48 h: Bartonella serology (homelessness, cats, lice); Coxiella (farm animals) and Brucella (unpasteurised milk) serology; look for TB [SAASP 2026].
  5. CT or MRI brain if any neurological sign I B.
FIRST HOURS
  • Septic, in HF, or rapidly progressing: take the cultures, then start empiric antibiotics now (dose box).
  • Subacute and stable: STG says wait for culture results before starting [STG 2024]; ESC and SAASP start once three sets are taken DECISION: wait or start
  • Prosthetic valve: PVE regimen, and refer every case [STG].
  • Treat HF (→ Decompensated HF page), but don't let it delay referral.
  • Stroke with suspected IE: no thrombolysis III C.
  • On warfarin and surgery likely (e.g. S. aureus): change to UFH IIa C. Stop anticoagulants and antiplatelets for major or intracranial bleeding I C. IE alone is not an indication for either.
CALL

Call the IM consultant now, and discuss with GSH cardiology the same day, for: HF or shock · prosthetic valve or device · new AV block · embolism or stroke · vegetation ≥ 10 mm · S. aureus, fungi or a resistant organism · cultures still positive after 7 days · AKI. These usually need a surgical centre (tertiary box).

Tertiary option · surgery

Emergency (< 24 h): acute severe regurgitation or fistula with pulmonary oedema or shock I B.

Urgent (3–5 days): HF; abscess, false aneurysm, fistula, enlarging vegetation or new AV block; fungi or multiresistant organism I C; vegetation ≥ 10 mm with an embolus I B; cultures positive > 1 week IIa B; S. aureus PVE IIa C.

Endocarditis team at the receiving centre I B: GSH cardiology, who decide on cardiothoracic referral.

Mitigation · while waiting, or if declined
  • Phone GSH cardiology with the echo and culture results; ask for a written plan.
  • Treat HF; agree a ceiling of care.
  • Repeat cultures every 48–72 h until negative CHECK interval
  • Declined surgery: full IV course, then discuss long-term suppression.
DIAGNOSE

ESC 2023 modified Duke criteria [ESC 2023]. Major: typical organism in 2 separate cultures, or persistently positive cultures · imaging positive for IE (echo, cardiac CT, PET/CT). Minor: predisposing heart condition or IV drug use · fever > 38 °C · embolic phenomena (including those found only on imaging) · immunological phenomena · positive culture or serology not meeting a major criterion.

Definite: 2 major, or 1 major + 3 minor, or 5 minor. Possible: 1 major + 1–2 minor, or 3–4 minor → repeat cultures and echo, look for emboli. Full table → Reference Manual.

ONGOING
  • Daily: temperature, new murmur, HF, neurology, emboli. Creatinine twice weekly on gentamicin or vancomycin.
  • Repeat cultures until negative. Fever after 7 days: abscess, embolus, line infection, drug fever, wrong organism → repeat cultures and echo I B; discuss.
  • Repeat echo for any new complication (murmur, embolus, HF, AV block) I B; ECG for PR interval.
  • Dental review during admission; extract septic teeth.
ORAL STEP-DOWN

Left-sided IE from streptococci, E. faecalis, S. aureus or CoNS; ≥ 10 days IV; afebrile > 2 days; CRP < 25% of peak or < 20; WCC < 15; TOE shows no abscess IIa A [POET]. Two-drug oral regimen; total duration unchanged [SAASP]. STG gives IV courses only DECISION: offer at George (needs a TOE: GSH only)

Avoid
  • Antibiotics before three culture sets in a stable patient
  • Thrombolysis in suspected IE
  • Gentamicin in native-valve S. aureus IE
  • Gentamicin without levels and creatinine
  • Calling a fever "drug fever" before repeating cultures and echo
  • Waiting for "possible" IE to become definite before referring a complicated case
Before discharge
  • Echo at the end of treatment I C
  • Dental care booked; teach the signs of relapse (fever in the first months)
  • RHD: secondary prophylaxis (benzathine penicillin) and cardiology follow-up
  • Prophylaxis before dental work → Reference Manual
