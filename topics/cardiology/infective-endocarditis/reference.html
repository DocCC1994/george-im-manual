Infective endocarditis in South Africa (Engelbrecht 2026)

  • Patients are young, and most have rheumatic heart disease. Many have mechanical valves with poorly controlled INRs, which adds bleeding and embolic risk.
  • S. aureus has overtaken streptococci as the commonest organism. HIV and IV drug use (including nyaope) bring right-sided IE.
  • Blood cultures are positive in only about half of African series; about 43% of SA cases are culture-negative. Prior antibiotics are the usual reason; Bartonella is the commonest true cause. Others: Coxiella, Brucella, TB [SAASP 2026].
  • Patients present late, TTE misses 40–60% of cases that TOE finds, and cardiac surgery is scarce, so many patients with a surgical indication are never operated on CHECK figures

ESC 2023 modified Duke criteria (ESC 2023, Table 10)

MajorBlood cultures: a typical organism (oral streptococci, S. gallolyticus, HACEK, S. aureus, E. faecalis) in 2 separate cultures; or any organism consistent with IE in persistently positive cultures (≥ 2 drawn > 12 h apart, or 3 of 3, or most of ≥ 4, first and last ≥ 1 h apart); or one culture with C. burnetii or phase I IgG > 1:800.
Imaging: valvular, perivalvular or prosthetic lesion typical of IE on echo (TTE or TOE), cardiac CT, PET/CT or WBC SPECT/CT. New in 2023: CT and nuclear imaging count.
MinorPredisposing heart condition or IV drug use · fever > 38 °C · embolic vascular dissemination, including emboli seen only on imaging (septic emboli, spondylodiscitis, mycotic aneurysm, intracranial lesions, conjunctival haemorrhage, Janeway lesions) · immunological phenomena (glomerulonephritis, Osler nodes, Roth spots, rheumatoid factor) · microbiological evidence not meeting a major criterion.
ClassifyDefinite: 2 major; 1 major + ≥ 3 minor; or 5 minor. Possible: 1 major + 1–2 minor; or 3–4 minor. Rejected: neither. Reclassify as results arrive.

Complications
  • Heart failure: the commonest, and the main reason for urgent surgery.
  • Stroke in up to 35% (silent lesions up to 80%). No thrombolysis III C; thrombectomy may be considered IIb C. CTA for a mycotic aneurysm if there is haemorrhage I B.
  • Splenic infarct in about 20%; abscess if fever persists.
  • Perivalvular abscess: new AV block or PR prolongation.
  • AKI: glomerulonephritis, emboli, drugs.
Tertiary options

Cardiothoracic surgery, TOE, cardiac CT, PET/CT, neurointervention. Complicated IE should be managed with an endocarditis team at a centre with surgery I B. Centre CHECK

Oral step-down (POET 2019; ESC 2023 Fig. 9; SAASP 2026)

In POET, 400 stable patients with left-sided IE were switched to two oral drugs after at least 10 days IV. Oral treatment was non-inferior, and the benefit held at 5 years. ESC uses the POET criteria: streptococci, E. faecalis, S. aureus or CoNS; ≥ 10 days IV (≥ 7 after surgery); no fever for 2 days; CRP < 25% of peak or < 20 mg/L; WCC < 15; no new surgical indication or abscess on TOE. SAASP adds: likely to absorb and take tablets reliably, and no social reason (homelessness, substance use) to keep IV. The switch does not shorten the course.

Dental prophylaxis

Only for procedures that manipulate the gingiva or periapical region, or perforate the mucosa (extraction, scaling, implants). None for GI or GU procedures.

Aminopenicillin (amoxicillin) 2 g orally 30–60 min before; ampicillin 2 g IV or IM if nil by mouth.

  • Teach the signs of relapse; fever → blood cultures before antibiotics.

  • Cardiology follow-up for residual valve disease; RHD: secondary prophylaxis.
