Definitions (ESC 2026)

TermMeaning
Heart failureSymptoms or signs caused by a structural or functional cardiac abnormality, supported by raised natriuretic peptides or objective evidence of congestion.
HFrEFLVEF < 50%. ESC 2026 drops HFmrEF, so LVEF 41–49% now counts as HFrEF. The trial evidence for BBs and RAS inhibitors is strongest at LVEF ≤ 40%. Drug and device thresholds still apply: ICD, CRT and ivabradine ≤ 35%; digoxin and hydralazine-ISDN ≤ 40%; vericiguat < 45%.
HFpEFLVEF ≥ 50% plus objective evidence of structural or functional abnormality or raised filling pressures. An LVEF on its own is not enough (criteria on p. ).
HF with improved EFLVEF was reduced and has improved. Continue therapy at the maximum tolerated doses I C. Withdraw only in highly selected patients whose function has fully normalised, under close follow-up IIb C.
Stages A–DA at risk (hypertension, diabetes, obesity, cardiotoxins) · B pre-HF (structural disease or raised NP, no symptoms) · C symptomatic HF · D advanced HF.
Decompensated HFReplaces “acute HF” → Acute Handbook.

Diagnosis at George

  • ECG (a normal ECG makes HF unlikely), CXR, and bloods: FBC, U&E and eGFR, Na+, K+, LFT, TSH, glucose or HbA1c, lipids, HIV, iron studies (ferritin and transferrin saturation), urine dipstick or ACR.
  • NT-proBNP, where available (not at George): < 125 pg/mL makes chronic HF unlikely. Values are higher in AF and the elderly and lower with obesity (ESC 2021).
  • Formal echo for every new diagnosis: LVEF, valves, RV function, PA pressure, pericardium. POCUS can triage but does not replace it. Repeat the echo about 3 months after therapy is optimised, before any device decision.

Tertiary options · work-up

1. Cardiology review and formal echo, if not available at George.

2. Cardiac MRI for unexplained cardiomyopathy, myocarditis or infiltration.

3. Coronary angiography if ischaemia is suspected and revascularisation would be considered.

4. Genetic testing and family screening (familial DCM or HCM).

All four classes, started early at low doses, and each up-titrated every 1–2 weeks to the target or maximum tolerated dose I C. Do not wait for one drug to reach its target before starting the next. Continue even if symptoms or LVEF improve I C.

Tertiary options · devices and interventions

ICD: LVEF ≤ 35% after ≥ 3 months of optimised FMT and an expected survival > 1 year CHECK 2026 classes.

CRT: LBBB with QRS ≥ 150 ms and LVEF ≤ 35%. ESC 2026: CRT can be planned alongside starting FMT. Refer every HF patient with LBBB [STG].

Valves: surgery or TAVI; mitral TEER for selected severe secondary MR.

Revascularisation for ischaemic HFrEF where indicated.

LVAD or transplant: early advanced HF centre referral I A (I NEED HELP: Acute Handbook).

Mitigation · drug or device unavailable
  • No SGLT2i or ARNI: get ACEi, carvedilol and spironolactone to target doses. Each has a proven mortality benefit, and under-dosing is common.
  • Patient with a private SGLT2i supply (about R200–300 a month): continue it and record it DECISION
  • Waiting for an ICD or CRT: optimise FMT, keep K+ and Mg normal, avoid QT-prolonging drugs. Amiodarone for symptomatic VT (no mortality benefit).

Who: any clinician (doctor or nurse) working to this card · How often: every 1–2 weeks until target or maximum tolerated doses I C; frequent visits for the first 6 weeks after an admission I B2 [STRONG-HF]. HFpEF: see p. .

At every visit

  1. Symptoms: breathlessness (NYHA class), orthopnoea, dizziness, side effects.
  2. Fluid: weight against the dry weight, oedema, JVP, crackles.
  3. BP (standing as well if dizzy) and pulse.
  4. K+ and creatinine if an ACEi, ARB or MRA was started or increased 1–2 weeks ago.
  5. Adherence and other medicines: no NSAIDs; ask about traditional or over-the-counter remedies.
  6. Then use the traffic lights: all green → increase one or two drugs (next step on the ladder).

HFpEF: confirm it (ESC 2026 simplified echo criteria)

Symptoms or signs, LVEF ≥ 50%, and at least one of: LV mass index ≥ 95 g/m² (F) or ≥ 115 g/m² (M), or relative wall thickness > 0.42 · LA volume index > 34 mL/m² (sinus) or > 40 mL/m² (AF) · E/e' > 9 at rest · PA systolic pressure > 35 mmHg or TR velocity > 2.8 m/s. A raised NT-proBNP supports the diagnosis.

Exclude mimics: valve disease (rheumatic MS) · constrictive pericarditis (TB) · amyloid or HCM · high-output states (anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, beriberi) · lung disease or OSA · CKD with volume overload.

Self-care and follow-up (all HF)

  • Education for the patient and family. Weigh daily, or at least twice a week while doses are changing [SAFP 2025]. Gain of ≥ 2 kg in 3 days → clinic.
  • Salt restriction; fluid 1–1.5 L/day only if overloaded despite diuretics; alcohol ≤ 2 drinks/day, none if alcohol-related; stop smoking; regular exercise within symptom limits [STG 2024]. Cardiac rehabilitation once stable.
  • Avoid NSAIDs, verapamil or diltiazem (HFrEF), pioglitazone.
  • Women of child-bearing age: contraception (ACEi, ARB and MRA are contraindicated in pregnancy). After peripartum cardiomyopathy, counsel before any future pregnancy.
  • Stable: 3-monthly, then 6-monthly review. Plan advance care with patients in stage D.
Tertiary options · HFpEF

Amyloid work-up (bone scintigraphy, light chains), cardiac MRI, invasive haemodynamics or exercise testing when the diagnosis is uncertain, and valve or pericardial surgery.

Refer to GSH cardiology if a mimic is suspected

Mitigation
  • With no SGLT2i or GLP-1 RA available, the drug a clinic can add in HFpEF today is spironolactone, on top of diuretics and BP control.
  • No echo criteria available: treat the congestion and the comorbidities, and record “HF, LVEF ≥ 50%, criteria pending”.
