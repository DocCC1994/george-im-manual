RECOGNISE

New or worsening HF needing urgent treatment. ESC 2026 calls this decompensated HF (was “acute HF”).

Congestion (wet): orthopnoea, crackles, raised JVP, oedema, hepatomegaly, weight gain. Hypoperfusion (cold): cool peripheries, confusion, oliguria, narrow pulse pressure, lactate > 2.

FIND THE CAUSE

CHAMPIT: Coronary syndrome (ECG ASAP → STEMI / NSTE-ACS) · Hypertensive emergency · Arrhythmia (fast AF, heart block) · Mechanical (acute MR, valve or prosthesis failure → valvular heart disease; endocarditis) · PE · Infection (sepsis, pneumonia) · Tamponade (TB pericarditis).

Also: non-adherence, salt or fluid excess, NSAIDs, alcohol, anaemia, thyroid, thiamine deficiency, AKI, pregnancy or postpartum (peripartum cardiomyopathy).

INVESTIGATE

ECG and SpO2 ASAP. Finger-prick glucose. U&E, Mg (VBG). FBC, troponin (if ACS considered). ALT. TSH and HIV (all index presentations). VBG with lactate if cold. CXR.

POCUS: bilateral B-lines, effusions, LV function, pericardial effusion, IVC. Formal echo this admission for a first presentation or a new murmur.

FIRST HOUR
  1. Sit up, monitor, IV access. No routine catheter.
  2. Oxygen if SpO2 < 90% I C; target 94–98% (88–92% if CO2 retainer).
  3. CPAP/NIV early IIa B2; intubate if failing and an intubation candidate I C (see ICU protocol).
  4. Loop diuretic (furosemide) IV I A: diuretic-naive 40 mg; already on a loop, 1–2 × the total daily oral dose, IV.
  5. Nitrate if SBP > 110, especially hypertensive pulmonary oedema IIb C: nitrate (ISDN) SL, then IV GTN (dose box).
  6. No routine opioid (morphine). NEMLC removed it from the EML for pulmonary oedema because of harm [NDoH 2024]; ESC III C.
  7. Treat the cause now: STEMI pathway or NSTE-ACS · unstable fast AF: synchronised DC shock; otherwise IV antiarrhythmic (amiodarone), alternative: cardiac glycoside (no non-DHP CCB in HFrEF) (arrhythmias) · hypertensive emergencies · tamponade: pericardiocentesis.
CALL

Call IM if: SBP < 90 or signs of hypoperfusion · needs NIV, or SpO2 < 90% on oxygen · ACS or new murmur · tamponade · unstable arrhythmia · anuric AKI · pregnant or postpartum · poor response after the IV diuretic dose has been doubled (page 2 of 2).

Dose box

Furosemide IV: naive 40 mg; on oral loop 1–2 × daily oral dose (ESC 2026 suggests ~2 ×), as boluses 12hrly or infusion. Double if poor response. Usual max 400–600 mg/day; up to 1 000 mg/day if severe renal impairment [SAFP 2025]

CPAP 5–10 cmH2O, FiO2 to target; candidates per the George NIV and ICU SOP (2024)

Shock (with ICU): vasopressor (adrenaline) IIb C: the only vasopressor at George (ESC prefers noradrenaline, not available); inotrope (dobutamine) if hypoperfused despite adequate filling IIb C CHECK local doses

Isosorbide dinitrate 5 mg SL every 5–10 min while SBP > 110 CHECK STG §20.10

IV GTN 5–200 mcg/min (dosing table) CHECK dosing table to add: usually 50 mg in 200 mL, start at 5 mcg/min. PVC-free tubing [STG 2024]

No nitrates if: SBP < 90 · severe aortic stenosis · PDE-5i · RV infarct

Tertiary option

1. Cardiogenic shock: ICU; if ACS-related, emergency PCI at the receiving centre (STEMI page). Temporary mechanical support is tertiary only, for selected STEMI shock IIa B1; routine IABP is III B1 and not available in George.

2. Mechanical cause (acute MR, VSR, endocarditis with HF, prosthetic valve thrombosis): urgent cardiology and cardiothoracic referral.

3. Peripartum cardiomyopathy: cardiology (dopamine agonist (bromocriptine), anticoagulation).

How to refer: discuss with the IM consultant first; then GSH cardiology (they advise on cardiothoracics); typed letter co-signed by the treating consultant.

Mitigation · while waiting, or if declined
  • CPAP/NIV in the EC or ICU.
  • Agree a ceiling of care early and document it.
  • Low-dose inotrope: dobutamine can run peripherally initially in any setting in George.
DECONGEST
1 · Start IV furosemide (naive 40 mg; on a loop, 1–2 × daily oral dose). Aim for complete decongestion, not just relief.
↓
2 · Check the response: urine output over 6 h (> 100 mL/h), daily weight, and input/output balance.
↓
Adequate: repeat the same dose 12hrly. Reassess weight, congestion and U&E daily. Inadequate: double the dose and recheck in 2–6 h (dose box, page 1 of 2, for maximum).
↓
3 · Still inadequate: add thiazide (hydrochlorothiazide) 25 mg orally, 12hrly (sequential blockade) IIa B1. Monitor Na+ and K+.
↓
4 · Still congested: add MRA (spironolactone), up-titrating to 25 mg 12hrly; monitor K+ (full nephron blockade). Call the consultant (refractory congestion).
DAY 1 TO DISCHARGE
  • Daily weight and fluid balance while on IV diuretic. Keep K+ 4–5 mmol/L.
  • Monitor U&E ± CMP (not daily).
  • A small creatinine rise during effective decongestion is expected. Do not stop the diuretic while the patient is still congested; look for hypotension or hypovolaemia instead.
  • Salt restriction; fluid 1–1.5 L/day only if overloaded despite diuretics [STG 2024].
  • VTE prophylaxis (STG §2.14) unless anticoagulated. Thiamine 100 mg daily.
  • BB (carvedilol): continue unless shocked or bradycardic; halve rather than stop.
  • ACEi (enalapril): pause for SBP < 90, AKI or K+ > 5.5; restart once it settles.
  • Spironolactone: pause for SBP < 90, AKI or K+ > 5.5; restart once it settles.
  • SGLT2i (dapagliflozin) once stable, on a private script I B1 DECISION: not on EML
  • Change to oral furosemide when euvolaemic; ideally watch for 24 h on the oral dose.
DISCHARGE
  1. Do not discharge with residual congestion I C (it drives readmission and death) [SAFP 2025].
  2. No orthopnoea, no raised JVP, minimal oedema. Record the dry weight.
  3. Stable on the oral diuretic for 24 h; K+ and creatinine checked.
  4. Foundational therapy started if HFrEF (at least low dose) before discharge I B2: enalapril + carvedilol + spironolactone (+ dapagliflozin if available) → Reference Manual.
  5. Cause addressed; echo done or booked.
  6. Education: daily weights, salt, the fluid limit, no NSAIDs, when to return.
  7. Letter with the titration plan and target doses; first clinic visit in 1–2 weeks, then every 1–2 weeks for 6 weeks [STRONG-HF] → clinic up-titration card.
Avoid
  • Routine opioid
  • NSAIDs and COX-2 inhibitors III B2
  • Non-DHP CCB in HFrEF
  • Stopping a BB abruptly
  • Discharge while still congested
  • Discharge on the admission doses with no titration plan
