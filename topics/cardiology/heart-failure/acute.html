RECOGNISE

New or worsening HF needing urgent treatment. ESC 2026 calls this decompensated HF (was “acute HF”).

Congestion (wet): orthopnoea, crackles, raised JVP, oedema, hepatomegaly, weight gain. Hypoperfusion (cold): cool peripheries, confusion, oliguria, narrow pulse pressure, lactate > 2.

FIND THE CAUSE

CHAMPIT: Coronary syndrome (ECG now → STEMI / NSTE-ACS pages) · Hypertensive emergency · Arrhythmia (fast AF, heart block) · Mechanical (acute MR, valve or prosthesis failure, endocarditis) · PE · Infection · Tamponade (TB pericarditis).

Also: non-adherence, salt or fluid excess, NSAIDs, alcohol, anaemia, thyroid, thiamine deficiency, AKI, pregnancy or postpartum (peripartum cardiomyopathy).

INVESTIGATE

ECG and SpO2 now. Glucose, U&E, Mg, FBC, troponin, LFT, TSH, HIV. VBG with lactate if cold. CXR.

POCUS: bilateral B-lines, effusions, LV function, pericardial effusion, IVC. Formal echo this admission for a first presentation, a new murmur or suspected PPCM.

NT-proBNP (< 300 pg/mL makes decompensated HF unlikely, ESC 2021) is not available at George: rely on the clinical picture, POCUS and the response to diuretic.

FIRST HOUR
  1. Sit up, monitor, IV access. No routine catheter.
  2. Oxygen if SpO2 < 90% I C; target 94–98% (88–92% if CO2 retainer).
  3. CPAP/NIV early if RR > 25 and SpO2 < 90% despite oxygen IIa B2. Intubate if failing I C.
  4. Loop diuretic (furosemide) IV I A: diuretic-naive 40 mg; already on a loop, 1–2 × the total daily oral dose, IV.
  5. Nitrate if SBP > 110, especially hypertensive pulmonary oedema IIb C: nitrate (ISDN) SL, then IV nitrate (GTN) (dose box).
  6. No routine opioid (morphine). NEMLC removed it from the EML for pulmonary oedema because of harm [NDoH 2024]; ESC III C.
  7. Treat the cause now: STEMI pathway · unstable fast AF: synchronised DC shock; otherwise IV antiarrhythmic (amiodarone), alternative: cardiac glycoside (no non-DHP CCB in HFrEF) · hypertensive emergency page · tamponade: pericardiocentesis.
CALL

Call the IM consultant now if: SBP < 90 or signs of hypoperfusion · needs NIV, or SpO2 < 90% on oxygen · ACS or new murmur · tamponade · unstable arrhythmia · K+ > 6.0 or anuric AKI · pregnant or postpartum · poor response after the dose has been doubled (page 2 of 2).

Dose box

Furosemide IV: naive 40 mg; on oral loop 1–2 × daily oral dose (ESC 2026 suggests ~2 ×), as boluses 12hrly or infusion. Double if poor response. Usual max 400–600 mg/day; up to 1 000 mg/day if severe renal impairment [SAFP 2025]

Isosorbide dinitrate 5 mg SL every 5–10 min while SBP > 110 CHECK STG §20.10

IV GTN 5–200 mcg/min: start at 5, increase by 5 every 5 min to 20, then by 20 every 5 min. Hypertensive pulmonary oedema often needs higher rates. PVC-free tubing [STG 2024]

CPAP 5–10 cmH2O, FiO2 to target; candidates per the George NIV and ICU SOP (2024)

Shock (with ICU): ESC prefers a vasopressor (noradrenaline) IIb C, not stocked at George: catecholamine (adrenaline) instead DECISION; inotrope (dobutamine) if hypoperfused despite adequate filling IIb C CHECK local doses

No nitrates if: SBP < 90 · severe aortic stenosis · PDE-5i · RV infarct

Tertiary option

1. Cardiogenic shock: ICU; if ACS-related, emergency PCI at the receiving centre (STEMI page). Temporary mechanical support is tertiary only, for selected STEMI shock IIa B1; routine IABP is III B1.

2. Mechanical cause (acute MR, VSR, endocarditis with HF, prosthetic valve thrombosis): urgent cardiology and cardiothoracic referral.

3. Refractory congestion: ultrafiltration IIb C or dialysis IIa C.

4. Peripartum cardiomyopathy: cardiology (dopamine agonist (bromocriptine), anticoagulation).

How to refer: GSH cardiology (they advise on cardiothoracics); typed letter co-signed by the treating consultant.

Mitigation · while waiting, or if declined
  • CPAP/NIV started in the EC or ward and continued in EC resus while an ICU bed is found.
  • Agree a ceiling of care early and document it.
  • Telephone cardiology; send POCUS clips on an approved channel CHECK POPIA-safe route
  • Dobutamine, or adrenaline at low dose through a large-bore cannula in a big vein, may run outside ICU; central line as doses rise.
DECONGEST
1 · Start IV furosemide (naive 40 mg; on a loop, 1–2 × daily oral dose). Aim for complete decongestion, not just relief.
↓
2 · Check the response: urine output over 6 h ≥ 100 mL/h. ESC also uses spot urine Na+ at 2 h ≥ 70 mmol/L IIb B1, but NHLS George turnaround is too slow for it.
↓
Adequate: repeat the same dose 12hrly. Reassess weight, congestion and U&E daily. Inadequate: double the dose and recheck in 2–6 h (dose box, page 1 of 2, for maximum).
↓
3 · Still inadequate: add carbonic anhydrase inhibitor (acetazolamide) 500 mg IV daily (up to 3 days) [ADVOR] CHECK availability or thiazide (HCTZ) 25–50 mg orally, daily IIa B1. Check K+ and Na+ daily.
↓
4 · Still congested: call the consultant (refractory congestion; tertiary box, page 1 of 2).
DAY 1 TO DISCHARGE
  • Daily weight, fluid balance, U&E and Mg while on IV diuretic. Keep K+ 4–5 mmol/L.
  • A small creatinine rise during effective decongestion is expected. Do not stop the diuretic while the patient is still congested; look for hypotension or hypovolaemia instead.
  • Salt restriction; fluid 1–1.5 L/day only if overloaded despite diuretics [STG 2024].
  • VTE prophylaxis (STG §2.14) unless anticoagulated. Vitamin (thiamine) 100 mg daily if the cause is unexplained or alcohol is involved.
  • BB: continue unless shocked or bradycardic; halve rather than stop. ACEi/MRA: pause for SBP < 90, AKI or K+ > 5.5; restart once it settles.
  • SGLT2i (empagliflozin) once stable I B1 DECISION: not on EML CHECK agent
  • Change to oral furosemide when euvolaemic, and watch for 24 h on the oral dose.
DISCHARGE

Do not discharge with residual congestion I C: it drives readmission and death [SAFP 2025]. Checklist:

  1. No orthopnoea, JVP not raised, oedema minimal; record the dry weight.
  2. Stable on the oral diuretic for 24 h; K+ and creatinine checked.
  3. Foundational therapy started (at least low dose) before discharge I B2: ACEi (enalapril) + BB (carvedilol) + MRA (spironolactone) (+ SGLT2i if available) → Reference Manual.
  4. Cause addressed; echo done or booked.
  5. Education: daily weights, salt, the fluid limit, no NSAIDs, when to return.
  6. Letter with the titration plan and target doses; first clinic visit in 1–2 weeks, then every 1–2 weeks for 6 weeks [STRONG-HF] → clinic up-titration card.
Avoid
  • Routine opioid
  • NSAIDs and COX-2 inhibitors III B2
  • Non-DHP CCB in HFrEF
  • Stopping a BB abruptly
  • Discharge while still congested
  • Discharge on the admission doses with no titration plan
