RECOGNISE

Angina at rest > 20 min, new severe angina, crescendo angina, or angina after a recent MI. Equivalents: dyspnoea, epigastric, arm or jaw pain (often atypical in women, the elderly and people with diabetes).

ECG: ST depression, transient ST elevation, or T-wave inversion. A normal ECG does not exclude NSTE-ACS. If pain is ongoing and the ECG is normal, record V7–V9 and V4R: a posterior or RV occlusion is treated as STEMI.

NSTEMI = troponin rise/fall above the 99th centile. Unstable angina = the same picture with no troponin rise.

INVESTIGATE

ECG within 10 min; repeat it with any new pain. High-sensitivity troponin I at presentation, repeated as the NHLS report advises; use the rule-in and rule-out limits printed on the report.

K+, creatinine (enoxaparin dose), FBC, glucose, HIV, lipids; CXR. Echo if unstable, in heart failure or with a new murmur.

Troponin is also raised in PE, aortic dissection, myopericarditis, sepsis, heart failure and CKD, so rule out the mimics.

IMMEDIATE
  1. ABC, cardiac monitor, IV access. Avoid arterial and central punctures.
  2. Oxygen only if SpO2 < 94% CHECK: GRH 2021 ≤ 90%
  3. Antiplatelet (aspirin) 300 mg chewed
  4. P2Y12 inhibitor (clopidogrel) 300 mg load. Pretreatment is reasonable because angiography from George is unlikely within 24 h [ESC 2023].
  5. LMWH (enoxaparin) 1 mg/kg SC 12hrly (dose box).
  6. Pain: nitrate (isosorbide dinitrate) 5 mg SL every 5 min, up to 3–4 doses. If pain persists, IV nitrate (GTN). If pain doesn't respond to nitrates, call now, then give opioid (morphine) IV, max 10 mg total.
  7. Antiemetic (metoclopramide) 10 mg IV if nauseated.
RISK

EC, undifferentiated chest pain: HEART score. 0–3 = low risk (MACE 0.9–1.7%).

Confirmed NSTE-ACS: sort the patient into very high, high or not-high risk (see the tertiary box), which sets the urgency of referral. GRACE > 140 = high risk.

ONGOING
  • High care with monitoring until pain-free ≥ 24 h.
  • Aspirin 75 mg daily lifelong. Clopidogrel 75 mg daily DECISION: 3 months (STG) vs 12 months (ESC)
  • Enoxaparin for ≥ 2 days DECISION: GRH 2021 continued it to day 8 or discharge
  • Statin (rosuvastatin) 10 mg at night; with a PI, switch to atorvastatin 10 mg (interaction, not a formulary alternative).
  • Once stable (no HF, hypotension, bradycardia, asthma): BB (atenolol) 50 mg daily.
  • HF or LV dysfunction: ACEi (enalapril), titrate to 10 mg 12hrly; manage HF as in the heart failure topic.
REFER

Discuss every NSTE-ACS with GRH IM. A positive troponin or high-risk features mean cardiology referral for angiography [STG 2024]. Call the same hour if very high risk. Confirm the patient consents to angiography and possible surgery before transfer.

Dose box

Aspirin 300 mg chewed, then 75 mg daily

Clopidogrel 300 mg load, then 75 mg daily

Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC 12hrly; eGFR < 30: 1 mg/kg SC daily

Alternative: UFH 5 000 U IV bolus, then 1 000–1 200 U/h, titrated to aPTT

Isosorbide dinitrate 5 mg SL every 5 min, up to 3–4 doses

IV GTN 5–200 mcg/min: start at 5, increase by 5 every 5 min to 20, then by 20 every 5 min. Use PVC-free tubing and monitor BP [STG 2024]

No nitrates if: SBP < 90 · RV infarct · HR < 50 or > 100 · PDE-5i · severe aortic stenosis

Tertiary option · by risk

Very high risk → immediate angiography (< 2 h): shock or haemodynamic instability · refractory or recurrent pain · life-threatening arrhythmia · mechanical complication · acute HF · recurrent dynamic ST changes CHECK vs ESC table

High risk → early angiography (< 24 h), class IIa: confirmed NSTEMI · dynamic ST/T changes · transient ST elevation · GRACE > 140

Not high risk: selective, in-hospital or elective work-up.
