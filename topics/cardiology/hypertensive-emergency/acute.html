RECOGNISE

Severe hypertension: BP ≥ 180/110 on repeat measurement [STG 2024] [ESC 2024] DECISION: GRH 2021 used > 180/120. The organs decide what to do, not the number.

Look for damage, organ by organ: brain (headache with confusion, seizures, cortical blindness, focal signs) · eyes (flame haemorrhages, cotton-wool spots, papilloedema: malignant hypertension) · heart (chest pain, pulmonary oedema) · aorta (tearing chest or back pain, arm BP difference > 20, new AR murmur) · kidney (AKI, blood and protein on dipstick) · blood (haemolysis and low platelets: TMA) · pregnancy or up to 6 weeks postpartum.

Ask about: stopped medicines (central α-agonist (clonidine), BB) · tik, cocaine · alcohol withdrawal · NSAIDs, steroids, MAOI · pain, urinary retention.

INVESTIGATE

Repeat BP after rest with the right cuff, both arms. Fundoscopy. ECG. Urine dipstick and pregnancy test. U&E and creatinine, glucose, FBC with smear (fragments) and LDH, troponin if chest pain or ECG changes. CXR. HIV.

CT brain if any neurological sign. CT aortogram if dissection is possible; start BP and heart-rate control before the scan.

FIRST HOUR
(emergency)
  1. Resus or high care, monitor, IV access. BP every 5 min while titrating, then every 15 min; arterial line if available.
  2. Pick the target from the organ (table, page 2 of 2): dissection, pulmonary oedema and ACS fast; stroke and ICH hardly at all.
  3. Default (malignant hypertension, encephalopathy, AKI, TMA): lower by no more than 25% in the first hour, then towards 160/100 over the next 2–6 h, then to normal over 24–48 h [STG 2024] [ESC Council]. GRH 2021: < 180/120 in the first hour, < 160/110 by 24 h.
  4. IV α/β-blocker (labetalol) first-line (dose box). Nitrate infusion (GTN) instead if pulmonary oedema or ACS, or labetalol is contraindicated.
  5. Fluids: malignant hypertension is often volume-depleted. Loop diuretic (furosemide) only if overloaded DECISION: STG lists furosemide 40–80 mg IV for all
  6. Too fast a fall causes stroke, MI or blindness: if neurology worsens as BP falls, stop the infusion.
  7. Start oral therapy once BP is controlled and stable for 8–24 h, then wean the IV drug (page 2 of 2).
CALL

Call the IM consultant now if: suspected aortic dissection · GCS falling, seizures or new focal signs · ICH or stroke (stroke team) · pulmonary oedema needing NIV · ACS · TMA or AKI with K+ > 6.0 or anuria · pregnant or postpartum (and obstetrics) · BP not controlled after 300 mg labetalol or 1 h of treatment.

Dose box

α/β-blocker (labetalol) IV (5 mg/mL) DECISION: one regimen (the only IV BB at George)

  • Boluses (GRH 2021): 10–20 mg over 2 min, then 20–80 mg every 10 min to target; maximum 300 mg total.
  • Infusion (STG 2024): 2 mg/min (200 mg in 200 mL = 1 mg/mL at 120 mL/h) to a total of 1–2 mg/kg (GRH 2021: 0.5–2 mg/min, maximum 300 mg/24 h).
  • Avoid in: acute HF, asthma or COPD with bronchospasm, HR < 60, 2nd/3rd-degree block, stimulant toxicity.

Nitrate (GTN) infusion 5–200 mcg/min: start 5, increase by 5 every 5 min to 20, then by 20 every 5 min. 25 mg in 250 mL (about 100 mcg/mL): 3 mL/h = 5 mcg/min, 12 mL/h = 20, 60 mL/h = 100. PVC-free line [STG Table 3.3]

2. Refractory BP: ICU; alternatives: IV CCB, short-acting IV BB, direct vasodilator not EML CHECK which the receiving ICU stocks

NO ORGAN DAMAGE

Severe hypertension without acute organ damage is not an emergency. Rapid lowering causes harm and IV drugs are not needed [AHA/ACC 2025].

  1. Treat pain, anxiety and urinary retention; rest 1 h and repeat the BP [STG §3.6.1].
  2. Still ≥ 180/110: restart the usual medicines if stopped; otherwise start two oral drugs: thiazide (HCTZ) 12.5 mg plus CCB (amlodipine) 5 mg; alternative: ACEi (dose box) CHECK George first choice
  3. Symptomatic (headache, dizziness) or worsening organ damage without a life-threatening complication (STG “urgency”, §3.6.2): admit, two oral drugs, and lower the diastolic to 100 slowly over 48–72 h.
  4. No SL or short-acting CCB (nifedipine capsules) [ESC Council].
  5. Discharge to the level-one clinic for a BP check within 1 week [ESC 2024]; email the letter to the clinic doctor.
STEP DOWN AND DISCHARGE
  • Oral drugs once BP is stable for 8–24 h; wean the IV drug over hours.
  • Malignant hypertension or TMA: ACEi (enalapril) test dose 2.5 mg (first-dose fall), then up to 20 mg daily; K+ and creatinine in 24–48 h. Avoid if Na+ < 130 [STG].
  • Look for the cause: young, low K+, renal bruit, CKD, episodic symptoms, stimulants → Reference Manual.
  • Usually three drugs at discharge. Clinic every 2–4 weeks until at target; discuss adherence.
