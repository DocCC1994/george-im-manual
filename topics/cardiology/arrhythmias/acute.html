UNSTABLE?

No pulse: cardiac arrest algorithm. Adverse features (any one): shock (SBP < 90, cold, confused) · syncope · myocardial ischaemia (chest pain, ST change) · severe heart failure (pulmonary oedema).

Adverse feature caused by the arrhythmia → synchronised DC shock now, up to 3 attempts, sedated if conscious (dose box). Still unstable: amiodarone 300 mg IV over 10–20 min, then shock again [RCUK 2025].

A rate < 150 rarely causes shock on its own: look for sepsis, bleeding or PE. Never cardiovert or rate-control sinus tachycardia: treat the cause.

INVESTIGATE

12-lead ECG before any drug (unless unstable); run a rhythm strip during vagal manoeuvres and adenosine. Pads on. K+, Mg, Ca, U&E, glucose, FBC, VBG; troponin if ischaemia; TSH in new AF; digoxin level if on it. Keep K+ 4.0–5.0; correct Mg. POCUS: LV function, mitral stenosis, effusion.

SVT
  1. Modified Valsalva [REVERT]: semi-recumbent, strain (blow into a 10 mL syringe) for 15 s, then lie flat with legs raised for 15 s. Repeat once I B.
  2. Adenosine rapid IV push into a large antecubital vein with a 20 mL saline flush: 6 mg → 12 mg → 12 mg [STG 2024] DECISION: third dose 12 mg (STG) or 18 mg (ESC 2019, RCUK 2025). Flutter waves appear → treat as AF.
  3. Still in SVT: verapamil IV (normal LV, no beta-blocker, SBP > 100) IIa B or synchronised DC shock I B.

No adenosine if flutter is obvious (risk of 1:1 conduction) or the rhythm is irregular and broad.

VT

Stable VT: DC cardioversion under sedation is first-line [STG 2024]; ESC I B when sedation risk is low [ESC 2022]. Call for airway support.

Shock not possible, or VT recurs: amiodarone IV DECISION: 5 mg/kg over 30 min (STG) or 300 mg then 900 mg over 24 h (RCUK, ESC). Stable and amiodarone unsuitable: lidocaine (dose box).

Never verapamil in broad complex tachycardia; STG also says no adenosine. Treat ischaemia (STEMI or NSTE-ACS page); correct K+ and Mg.

BROAD IRREGULAR

AF with typical LBBB or RBBB, rate < 170: treat as AF (page 2 of 2).

Pre-excited AF (rate > 170, bizarre QRS that changes width beat to beat): DC cardioversion. No adenosine, verapamil, digoxin, beta-blocker or IV amiodarone: they can cause VF [STG 2024].

Torsades (polymorphic VT, long QT): magnesium sulphate 2 g IV over 10 min, repeat if it recurs; K+ 4.5–5.0; stop QT-prolonging drugs; if bradycardia-dependent, pace or run adrenaline to HR > 100. No amiodarone. Polymorphic VT with a normal QT: think ischaemia.

CALL

Call the IM consultant now if: any unstable tachycardia or a shock is needed · VT or any broad complex tachycardia · pre-excited AF · torsades · SVT not ended by adenosine · arrhythmia with ACS, heart failure or pregnancy.

Tertiary option

1. After any VT or broad complex tachycardia: refer once stable [STG 2024] for echo, ischaemia work-up and ICD assessment.

2. Electrical storm (3 or more VT episodes in 24 h): cardiology ICU.

3. EP study and ablation: any symptomatic WPW, recurrent or disabling SVT, recurrent flutter.

FAST AF: FIND THE DRIVER

Unstable because of the AF → shock (page 1 of 2). Otherwise look for the driver first: sepsis, hypovolaemia, bleeding, hypoxia, PE, alcohol or withdrawal, thyrotoxicosis, mitral stenosis (rheumatic), ACS, decompensated HF. In sepsis or hypovolaemia the fast rate is compensatory: treat the cause and do not chase the rate with drugs.

RATE

Target resting HR < 110 to start [ESC 2024].

  • LVEF > 40%, no HF: beta-blocker (atenolol orally; propranolol if thyrotoxic) or verapamil (asthma). Never both together.
  • HF, LVEF ≤ 40% or low BP: digoxin IV or orally; add amiodarone IV if still fast. No verapamil. Amiodarone may cardiovert: anticoagulate.
  • Flutter is harder to rate-control: discuss cardioversion early.
RHYTHM

Elective cardioversion only if onset is clearly < 24 h (ESC 2024) DECISION: STG uses 48 h, or after 3 weeks of therapeutic anticoagulation (STG: 4 weeks of warfarin), or once a TOE excludes thrombus. Most recent-onset AF converts within 48 h: rate control and waiting is reasonable [RACE 7]. Anticoagulate for ≥ 4 weeks after any cardioversion.

STROKE RISK

Score CHA2DS2-VASc: ≥ 2 → warfarin [STG 2024] DECISION: ESC 2024 CHA2DS2-VA (sex removed): ≥ 2 I, 1 IIa. Mitral stenosis or mechanical valve: warfarin whatever the score; rivaroxaban was worse than warfarin in rheumatic AF [INVICTUS].

Warfarin 5 mg daily, INR after 48 h, target 2–3 (lower start if old, frail, liver disease or interacting drugs) → Reference Manual. HAS-BLED finds risks to fix, not a reason to withhold. DOACs are not on the EML [NEMLC 2022].

BRADYCARDIA, AV BLOCK

Adverse features (shock, syncope, ischaemia, HF) or risk of asystole (recent asystole, Mobitz II, complete heart block with broad QRS, pause > 3 s) [RCUK 2025]:

  1. Atropine 0.5 mg IV, repeat every 3–5 min to 3 mg (STG: 0.6–1.2 mg). Often fails in broad-QRS complete heart block.
  2. No response: adrenaline infusion 2–10 mcg/min and/or transcutaneous pacing with analgesia and sedation. Isoprenaline if stocked CHECK.
  3. Still unstable: transvenous pacing; call now.

Reversible causes: inferior MI (STEMI) · hyperkalaemia (calcium first) · beta-blocker, verapamil, digoxin or amiodarone (toxicity or overdose) · hypothermia, hypoxia, hypothyroidism, raised ICP · new AV block in endocarditis = aortic root abscess.

Refer all second- and third-degree AV block for a pacemaker, symptomatic or not [STG 2024] DECISION: asymptomatic Mobitz I (Wenckebach). Pads on until paced.

CALL

Call the IM consultant now if: bradycardia with adverse features or risk of asystole · pacing needed · any second- or third-degree AV block · AF with WPW, mitral stenosis, pregnancy, HF or ACS · bleeding or INR > 5 on warfarin.
