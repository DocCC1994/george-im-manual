Broad complex tachycardia: VT or SVT with aberrancy?

Assume VT [STG 2024]. With previous MI, heart failure or structural heart disease, most broad complex tachycardia is VT. A normal blood pressure does not exclude VT.

Favours VTFavours SVT with aberrancy or pre-excitation
AV dissociation (P waves march through) · capture or fusion beats · QRS > 160 ms · extreme ("north-west") axis · concordance across V1–V6 · no RS complex in any chest lead · initial R in aVRTypical LBBB or RBBB shape · the same bundle branch block on an old ECG in sinus rhythm · rate-related block seen starting and stopping · delta waves on the sinus ECG (pre-excitation)

Other causes of a broad QRS: hyperkalaemia (slow, sine-wave; give IV calcium (calcium gluconate)) · sodium-channel poisoning (tricyclics, carbamazepine: QRS > 100 ms and a terminal R in aVR; give IV sodium bicarbonate, not an antiarrhythmic → TCA page) · paced rhythm · pre-excited AF.

Long QT and torsades

  • Measure the QT in II or V5; correct with Fridericia at fast rates (Bazett over-corrects). QTc > 500 ms, or a rise > 60 ms on a new drug, is high risk.
  • Common QT-prolonging drugs at George: bedaquiline, delamanid, clofazimine, moxifloxacin and levofloxacin (DR-TB); macrolides; haloperidol and other antipsychotics; ondansetron; methadone; citalopram and escitalopram; tricyclics; quinine and chloroquine; efavirenz; lopinavir/ritonavir CHECK list.
  • Risk multipliers: low K+, Mg or Ca; bradycardia; female sex; heart failure; two QT drugs together; congenital long QT (family history of sudden death, syncope with exertion or startle).
  • Acute treatment: Acute Handbook page 1 of 2 (IV magnesium, K+ 4.5–5.0, stop the drugs, raise the heart rate).
Tertiary options

1. EP study and ablation: all symptomatic WPW (delta wave on the sinus ECG) [STG 2024], recurrent or disabling SVT, recurrent flutter (high cure rate).

2. After VT: echo, ischaemia work-up, cardiac MRI if no cause, ICD assessment for secondary prevention [ESC 2022].

3. Congenital long QT or Brugada: cardiology and family screening.

Rate and rhythm control

  • Rate: BB (atenolol 50–100 mg daily; carvedilol if HF); add cardiac glycoside (digoxin 0.125 mg daily) if needed (trough 0.6–1 nmol/L; toxicity in the old, CKD, low K+). Alternative if a BB is contraindicated and LVEF > 40%: non-DHP CCB [STG 2024]. Lenient target < 110 at rest; tighter if symptoms persist or LV function is falling (tachycardia cardiomyopathy) [ESC 2024].
  • Cardioversion: onset < 24 h (ESC) DECISION: STG 48 h, otherwise after 3 weeks of therapeutic anticoagulation (STG: 4 weeks of VKA) or a TOE. Anticoagulate ≥ 4 weeks after, then by score. Recent-onset AF: waiting up to 48 h for spontaneous conversion is non-inferior to early cardioversion [RACE 7].
  • Class III antiarrhythmic (amiodarone), specialist-initiated, for severe symptoms despite rate control: 200 mg 8hrly for 1 week, 12hrly for 1 week, then daily. Halve the VKA and digoxin doses; TSH, LFT and eye review every 6 months [STG 2024].
  • Flutter: rate control is harder; DC cardioversion is the most effective treatment; no non-DHP CCB; anticoagulate as for AF; recurrent flutter → ablation [STG 2024].
Tertiary options

1. Catheter ablation: first-line option for symptomatic paroxysmal AF I, or after failed drug therapy [ESC 2024].

2. Pace and ablate (AV node ablation with a pacemaker) when rate control fails IIa.

3. Rheumatic mitral stenosis: balloon valvotomy or valve surgery → Valvular heart disease topic.

4. Early rhythm control in selected patients IIa.

Tertiary options

1. Permanent pacemaker, tertiary only [STG 2024]: Mobitz II, 2:1 with broad QRS, complete heart block, symptomatic sinus node disease or slow AF.

2. Transvenous temporary pacing when transcutaneous pacing is needed for more than a short bridge I C (refractory to drugs).

How to refer: centre, wait, transport with pads on CHECK

Transcutaneous pacing: how
  1. Pads front and back (or front and left side); ECG leads from the monitor connected. Analgesia and sedation first if conscious.
  2. Demand mode, rate 60–70. Raise the current from about 30 mA until every spike is followed by a broad QRS and a T wave (electrical capture), usually 50–100 mA.
  3. Confirm a pulse at the femoral artery (muscle twitch mimics a carotid pulse). Set the output about 10 mA above the threshold.
  4. It is a bridge: arrange transvenous pacing or transfer. Check capture often.
Mitigation · waiting for a pacemaker
  • Stable narrow-escape complete heart block: monitored bed, pads on, antimuscarinic (atropine) and catecholamine (adrenaline) drawn up.
  • Stop AV-blocking drugs; check K+, TSH and digoxin level.
  • Inferior MI: wait for recovery before referring for a permanent device CHECK how long.
  • Escalate if the escape rhythm broadens, pauses appear, or syncope recurs.
