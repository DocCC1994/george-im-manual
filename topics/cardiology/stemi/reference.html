Diagnosis (STG 2024 §3.2.1; ESC 2023)

STEMI = symptoms of ischaemia + persistent ST elevation (or new/presumed new LBBB). It signals an acutely occluded coronary artery, so treatment is never delayed for troponin.

Streptokinase-specific contraindications (use a fibrin-specific agent instead): any previous streptokinase (George uses the STG wording) · previous allergy to streptokinase · cardiogenic shock or hypotension (GRH 2023). Antibodies after a first dose make re-use ineffective and risk allergy.

Absolute contraindications (any fibrinolytic) [NDoH 2023; STG 2024]: previous allergy to the agent · stroke within 3 months · recent major trauma or surgery · bleeding in the past month · aneurysm or aortic dissection · recent brain or spinal surgery or head injury · active bleeding or a bleeding disorder.

Relative contraindications (consult a specialist): refractory hypertension · warfarin · recent retinal laser · subclavian central line · pregnancy · TIA within 6 months · traumatic resuscitation.

After lysis

  • Reperfusion check: ECG at 90 min after streptokinase starts (60 min after alteplase). A fall of < 50% in ST elevation in the lead with the greatest elevation = failed → urgent referral for rescue PCI [STG 2024].
  • Reperfusion arrhythmias (accelerated idioventricular rhythm, transient bradycardia) are common and usually self-limiting. Keep the patient on a monitor with a defibrillator at the bedside, including during transfer.
  • Even after successful lysis, ESC recommends angiography ± PCI of the infarct artery within 2–24 h (the pharmaco-invasive strategy) [ESC 2023]. In the Tygerberg referral network, 65% of STEMI patients were managed this way, with 30-day mortality of 6.9% [SA Heart J 2025].
Tertiary options

1. Rescue PCI (urgent). Failed reperfusion, cardiogenic shock, recurrent VT/VF, ongoing ischaemic pain.

2. Pharmaco-invasive angiography 2–24 h after successful lysis.

3. Primary PCI when lysis is contraindicated and a PCI centre can be reached.

4. Surgery for mechanical complications (acute MR, VSD, free-wall rupture).

5. Pacing for new RBBB with fascicular block, or persistent high-grade AV block.

How to refer: GSH cardiology, which also advises on cardiothoracic surgery. PCI is referred only after failed lysis, using the Paarl post-STEMI PCI referral criteria. Referral letter typed and co-signed by the treating consultant. Transport: see the transfer section of the introduction.

Mitigation · if transfer is delayed or declined
  • Document the discussion, the reason and the plan; re-refer if pain, shock or arrhythmia recurs.
  • Keep the patient in high care with continuous monitoring ≥ 24 h.
  • Continue LMWH (enoxaparin) up to 8 days, plus antiplatelets, statin, BB and ACEi as below.
  • Do an echo before discharge: LVEF, regional wall motion, LV thrombus, MR/VSD.
  • Manage arrhythmias by ACLS; give fluid for RV infarct with hypotension.
  • Follow-up within 1 month with an ECG: difficult patients at the Monday cardiac MOPD; stable patients at their level-one clinic with the electronic discharge letter.
