Valve disease at George

  • Rheumatic heart disease (RHD) is the commonest cause in young adults, mostly women: mitral stenosis (MS), mitral regurgitation (MR) or mixed mitral disease, often with aortic regurgitation (AR). Degenerative aortic stenosis (AS) and MR in the elderly; functional MR and TR from dilated hearts.
  • RHD presents with heart failure, AF and stroke, endocarditis (infective endocarditis) or decompensation in pregnancy. Many patients have mechanical valves and labile INRs.
  • Decompensated valve disease is treated as heart failure; fast AF as arrhythmias. This page is for the ward review after admission.

Acute rheumatic fever (revised Jones criteria, AHA 2015); WHO 2024

SA is a moderate- to high-risk population. Diagnose with evidence of a preceding group A streptococcal infection (raised ASOT or anti-DNase B, positive throat culture) plus 2 major, or 1 major + 2 minor criteria (recurrence: also 3 minor) CHECK against WHO 2024 (not uploaded)

MajorCarditis, including subclinical (valvulitis on echo only) · arthritis: polyarthritis, monoarthritis or polyarthralgia · Sydenham chorea · erythema marginatum · subcutaneous nodules
MinorMonoarthralgia · fever ≥ 38 °C · ESR ≥ 30 mm/h or CRP ≥ 30 mg/L · prolonged PR interval for age (unless carditis is a major criterion)
TreatBed rest and supportive care. Eradicate streptococci: long-acting penicillin (benzathine benzylpenicillin) 1.2 MU IM once, or aminopenicillin (amoxicillin) 1 g orally 12hrly for 10 days; severe penicillin allergy: macrolide (azithromycin) 500 mg daily for 3 days. Arthritis and fever: NSAID (ibuprofen) 400 mg 8hrly with meals [STG §3.7] CHECK. Then start secondary prophylaxis (box).

Ward workup

TestLook for
TTEEvery new murmur with symptoms, heart failure, AF or embolism. Lesion, severity (table below), LV size and EF, pulmonary pressure, LA thrombus (TTE misses most; TOE at GSH), rheumatic features (thickened leaflets, restricted posterior mitral leaflet, commissural fusion).
ECGAF, LA enlargement (P mitrale), LVH (AS, AR), RVH (MS with pulmonary hypertension), PR prolongation (rheumatic fever, aortic root abscess).
BloodsFBC, U&E, INR (on warfarin), HIV; TSH in new AF; ASOT, CRP or ESR if rheumatic fever is possible; blood cultures if febrile.
CXRLarge LA (double right heart border, splayed carina), pulmonary venous congestion, valve calcification.

Bedside clues to severity
  • MS: loud S1, opening snap close to S2 and a long diastolic rumble mean severe; AF, loud P2 and RV heave mean pulmonary hypertension.
  • AS: slow-rising, low-volume pulse, soft or absent A2, late-peaking murmur, narrow pulse pressure. Murmur loudness falls as the LV fails.
  • MR: displaced, thrusting apex; S3; soft S1.
  • AR: wide pulse pressure, collapsing pulse, long early diastolic murmur, displaced apex.
  • Exertional syncope or angina in AS, and pulmonary oedema in pregnancy with MS, are danger signs.

Murmur approach: Calgary Black Book pp 8, 16–17.

Secondary prophylaxis

Benzathine benzylpenicillin 1.2 MU IM every 3–4 weeks (preferred). Dissolve in 3.2 mL lidocaine 1% without adrenaline, or 3 mL water for injection.

Oral, if injections are refused: oral penicillin (phenoxymethylpenicillin) 250 mg 12hrly, or amoxicillin 250 mg daily. Severe penicillin allergy: azithromycin 250 mg daily.

Medical management while awaiting intervention

LesionDo
MSSlow the heart to lengthen diastole: BB (atenolol) or carvedilol; digoxin added for AF. Loop diuretic (furosemide) for congestion. Warfarin for AF, previous embolism or LA thrombus (below). Pulmonary oedema in pregnancy: BB and diuretic, refer.
ASNo drug changes the course. Gentle diuretics; avoid hypovolaemia and large drops in afterload (cautious with nitrates, ACEi, spinal anaesthesia). Treat hypertension carefully.
MR, ARPrimary lesions need surgery, not drugs. Secondary MR: full HF therapy first (heart failure), then reassess. Hypertension with AR: ACEi (enalapril) or dihydropyridine CCB (amlodipine).
AllSecondary penicillin prophylaxis for RHD; dental care (endocarditis prophylaxis); contraception and pregnancy planning (STG §6.3).

Refer for intervention (ESC/EACTS 2025); STG §3.7 CHECK against ESC 2025

  • STG: any RHD patient who needs significant diuretic doses or has had pulmonary oedema; every pregnant woman with symptomatic RHD.
  • Any severe lesion with symptoms.
  • Asymptomatic severe lesion with LV damage: AS with EF < 50%; MR with EF ≤ 60% or LV end-systolic diameter ≥ 40 mm, new AF or pulmonary pressure > 50 mmHg; AR with EF ≤ 50% or LV end-systolic diameter > 50 mm (> 25 mm/m²).
  • Clinically significant MS (area ≤ 1.5 cm²) with symptoms: percutaneous mitral commissurotomy if the anatomy suits, otherwise surgery.
  • Prosthetic valve dysfunction, new regurgitation, haemolysis or suspected valve thrombosis: urgent GSH discussion.

Bioprosthetic: aspirin, or warfarin for the first 3 months after a surgical mitral bioprosthesis; long-term anticoagulation only for another reason, such as AF.

Bridging (mechanical valve, warfarin stopped for surgery): stop 5 days before; start LMWH (enoxaparin) 1 mg/kg SC 12hrly or UFH when the INR falls below range; restart warfarin the evening after surgery. Bridge mitral valves and any valve with a risk factor CHECK CHECK (T10)

Pregnancy with a mechanical valve: tertiary team. LMWH only if anti-Xa levels can be checked weekly (STG).

Tertiary options

TOE, cardiac catheterisation, percutaneous mitral commissurotomy, valve surgery and TAVI, and thrombolysis or surgery for prosthetic valve thrombosis. For George: GSH cardiology first; they decide on cardiothoracic referral. Valve disease is managed medically unless there is an acute surgical indication.

If surgery is delayed or declined
  • Keep optimising: rate control and diuretics (MS), HF therapy (secondary MR), anticoagulation, penicillin prophylaxis.
  • Re-echo every 6–12 months for severe lesions, or sooner if symptoms change, and re-refer when a trigger appears.
  • Typed referral letter co-signed by the consultant, saved under "Referrals letters for GSH".
  • Contraception for women of child-bearing age; give a warfarin booklet to every patient on warfarin.
