Overview and definitions

  • Pericardial effusion: more than the normal 15–50 mL [Harrison]. Size by the echo-free space in diastole: small < 10 mm, moderate 10–20 mm, large > 20 mm. Chronic if > 3 months [ESC 2015] CHECK.
  • Tamponade: compression of the heart by fluid, pus, blood, clot or gas, with haemodynamic compromise. A clinical diagnosis; echo shows the effusion and its effect on the chambers [Herbst].
  • Effusive-constrictive pericarditis: a tense effusion plus a thickened visceral layer; venous pressure stays high after drainage. Constrictive pericarditis: a rigid pericardium limits filling, often after TB [Harrison].
  • South Africa: TB causes about 70% of large effusions and most constriction in Africa; mortality 17–40%, and constriction follows in a similar share [teaching note] CHECK. Most patients with TB pericarditis in SA have HIV CHECK. In high-income settings: idiopathic or viral, cancer, after procedures.
  • Higher risk: HIV (TB, Kaposi sarcoma, lymphoma, purulent), advanced CKD and dialysis, cancer, anticoagulation, recent MI or cardiac surgery, hypothyroidism.

Pathophysiology

  • J-shaped pressure–volume curve: a slow effusion stretches the sac (2 L tolerated); a fast one reaches the steep limb with 100–200 mL [Harrison].
  • Chamber collapse: pericardial pressure exceeds RA, then RV diastolic pressure; the thin RA collapses first (sensitive), the RV next (specific). Filling and stroke volume fall; tachycardia holds the output until it cannot.
  • Ventricular interdependence: inspiration fills the RV, the septum bulges left, LV stroke volume falls: pulsus paradoxus. Diastolic pressures equalise; the JVP has a prominent x and an absent y descent (in constriction the y is prominent).
  • Low-pressure tamponade: hypovolaemia (dialysis, bleeding, diuretics); JVP not raised; responds to fluid. Regional tamponade: loculated fluid after surgery compresses one chamber; classic signs absent.
  • Organs: cardiovascular (shock, PEA) · respiratory (breathless, clear lungs) · renal (prerenal AKI) · liver (congestion, raised enzymes) · gut (ascites, protein loss in constriction).

Stepwise work-up

  • Step 1, bloods: FBC, platelets, INR, PTT before any tap; U&E (urea for uraemic pericarditis); total protein, albumin, LDH and glucose to pair with the fluid; globulin and WCC for the Tygerberg score; CRP, troponin, TSH, HIV (CD4 if positive), blood cultures if febrile.
  • Step 2, imaging: ECG (low voltage, alternans; pericarditis changes). CXR (globular heart, clear lungs, pleural effusion, TB). POCUS in the EC and ICU and formal echo by the IM MOs: size, site, loculation, strands, collapse, IVC. Exaggerated respiratory variation in mitral (> 25%) and tricuspid (> 40%) inflow supports tamponade CHECK. CT for loculated fluid, thickening, a mass or dissection (contrast CT is mostly daytime; after hours for dissection with a doctor present). No MRI at George.
  • Step 3, pitfalls: pericardial fluid lies in front of the descending aorta, pleural fluid behind it [Herbst] · anterior epicardial fat looks like fluid · pulsus paradoxus is absent with an ASD, severe AR, a stiff LV, positive-pressure ventilation or regional tamponade CHECK · clotted blood is echogenic and missed · a negative Xpert or cytology does not exclude TB or cancer · ADA is also high in purulent pericarditis and lymphoma CHECK.

Pericardiocentesis pearls
  • Echo before every tap, and again just before puncture; agitated saline confirms the space [Herbst].
  • Drain as fully as safely possible; leave a pigtail if fluid may re-accumulate [Harrison].
  • Surgery, not a needle: type A dissection, free-wall rupture, clotted or loculated blood I C CHECK class.
  • Saline while preparing; it must not delay the tap [Harrison]. Big volumes help only low-pressure tamponade.

Management by cause

  • Tamponade: pericardiocentesis or surgical drainage now I C [ESC 2015] CHECK; immediate tap is mandatory [STG]. Adrenaline (George's only vasopressor) is a bridge, not a treatment. A large effusion without tamponade is drained for diagnosis, symptoms or suspected bacterial or malignant cause.
  • TB pericarditis: definite with organisms in fluid or tissue; probable with a lymphocytic exudate and high ADA, TB elsewhere, or a response to treatment. Treat as drug-sensitive TB for 6 months (RHZE by weight; pyridoxine in HIV) [STG 16.9]. By analogy with TB pleurisy, a lymphocytic exudate is enough to start in a high-burden setting [STG 16.10] DECISION.
    • Steroid: prednisolone for 6 weeks did not reduce death, tamponade or constriction overall; it reduced constriction and admissions but increased HIV-associated cancers [IMPI]. ESC: consider in HIV-negative patients only IIb C CHECK.
    • Constriction not improving after 4–8 weeks of TB treatment: pericardiectomy at GSH I C CHECK. Early constriction can be transient and resolve on treatment.
  • Uraemic and dialysis pericarditis: an acute dialysis indication [STG 7.1.4]; intensify dialysis, heparin-free; remove fluid slowly (low-pressure tamponade on dialysis); tap if tamponade; window if recurrent [Harrison].
  • Malignant: drain, send cytology and microbiology, refer all for definitive therapy [STG]. Extended pigtail drainage lowers recurrence; sclerosis, intrapericardial chemotherapy or a window are tertiary CHECK. Prognosis follows the cancer: plan the ceiling of care.
  • Purulent: fatal untreated. Full drainage, IV antibiotics, often intrapericardial fibrinolysis or surgical drainage (pericardiostomy, pericardiectomy) [ESC 2015] CHECK. Cover S. aureus and pneumococcus; SAASP has no pericarditis section DECISION.
  • Acute idiopathic or viral pericarditis: NSAID (ibuprofen) or aspirin for 1–2 weeks with a PPI, plus colchicine for 3 months I A; colchicine halves recurrence [COPE] [ICAP] CHECK. Steroid is second line and raises recurrence. About a quarter recur [Harrison].
  • Constrictive pericarditis: right heart failure with clear lungs, ascites more than oedema; often mistaken for cirrhosis (look at the neck veins). Pericardiectomy is the only cure; operative mortality 5–10% [Harrison].

Escalation and red flags
  • Call GRH IM: every tamponade, every large effusion, suspected purulent pericarditis, haemopericardium.
  • ICU (George ICU SOP [2024]): shock after drainage, decompression syndrome, ventilation needed.
  • GSH cardiology (cardiothoracics on their advice): dissection, rupture, clotted or loculated fluid, recurrence, constriction.
  • Never: a needle into a type A dissection; intubation before drainage if avoidable; diuretic or nitrate in tamponade.
