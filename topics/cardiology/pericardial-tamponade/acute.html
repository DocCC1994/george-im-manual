RECOGNISE

Tamponade is a clinical diagnosis: a pericardial effusion with haemodynamic compromise [Herbst]. Think of it in any shock with a raised JVP and clear lungs, in PEA arrest, and in the breathless patient with a big globular heart on CXR (TB, HIV).

Signs: tachycardia (almost always) · raised JVP · pulsus paradoxus (SBP falls > 10 mmHg on inspiration) · hypotension (late) · quiet heart sounds. Beck's triad is uncommon in slow effusions.

Speed matters: 100–200 mL can tamponade when it gathers fast (dissection, trauma, MI rupture, procedure, anticoagulant). Slow effusions (TB, uraemia, cancer, hypothyroidism) reach 1–2 L first [Harrison].

Mimics: tension pneumothorax (pleural emergencies) · massive PE · RV infarct (STEMI) · heart failure. STG groups them as obstructive shock: treat the cause [STG 20.12.4].

INVESTIGATE

POCUS now; do not wait for a formal echo. Effusion size in diastole: small < 10 mm, moderate 10–20, large > 20 mm. Tamponade signs: RA collapse in systole (early, over-sensitive), RV collapse in diastole, plethoric IVC (> 2.1 cm, < 50% collapse); a swinging heart CHECK cut-offs.

ECG: sinus tachycardia, low voltage, electrical alternans; PR depression and widespread ST elevation if pericarditis. CXR: globular heart, clear lung fields, pleural effusion.

Bloods: FBC and platelets, INR, PTT, U&E (uraemia), glucose, total protein, albumin and LDH (to pair with the fluid), CRP, troponin, TSH, HIV, blood cultures if febrile.

Chest or back pain with a pulse deficit: think type A dissection: urgent CT angiogram, not a needle (hypertensive emergencies).

IMMEDIATE
  1. Tamponade: call GRH IM now. Resus bay, monitor, 2 large-bore IV lines. Oxygen if SpO2 < 90%.
  2. Pericardiocentesis is the treatment, and is mandatory in tamponade [STG 21.1.3.2]. Ultrasound-guided, by the most experienced operator (box). Do not delay it for a formal echo or results in a peri-arrest patient.
  3. While preparing: fluid only if dry or hypovolaemic (dose box); no large volumes DECISION. No diuretic, nitrate or vasodilator.
  4. Avoid intubation and positive-pressure ventilation before drainage: they can cause arrest. If unavoidable, have the needle ready.
  5. Still hypotensive: vasopressor (adrenaline) as a bridge to the tap only (dose box).
  6. PEA arrest: CPR with cautious fluid [STG 20.1], and drain during CPR.
  7. Haemopericardium from dissection, MI free-wall rupture or trauma needs surgery: GSH cardiology now. Needle drainage only in small amounts to keep a pulse DECISION. On an anticoagulant: stop it; reverse (anticoagulant bleeding).

Effusion, no tamponade: large (> 20 mm): admit, formal echo within 24 h, examine and POCUS daily. Drain for diagnosis if TB, cancer or pus is suspected, or for symptoms CHECK. Small or moderate: find the cause (page 2 of 2).

REFER

Call GRH IM: every tamponade · every large effusion · suspected purulent pericarditis · haemopericardium. ICU (George ICU SOP [2024]) if still unstable after drainage. GSH cardiology (they advise on cardiothoracics): surgical cause, a loculated or posterior effusion out of needle reach, recurrence, constriction.

Dose box

Fluid (dry or hypovolaemic only): sodium chloride 0.9% 250–500 mL over 10–15 min, then reassess CHECK DECISION volume

Vasopressor (adrenaline), bridge to drainage: {{recipe:adrenaline}} CHECK

Local anaesthetic (lidocaine) 1%, skin to pericardium; maximum 3 mg/kg CHECK max

Drainage: remove enough to restore BP first; then drain in steps, about 1 L at the first sitting (decompression syndrome) CHECK

Pericardiocentesis · ultrasound-guided
  1. Sit at 30–45°. Scan subxiphoid, apical and left parasternal; choose the largest pocket nearest the skin with no lung or liver in the path. Note depth and angle.
  2. Aseptic, local anaesthetic. Advance on that line, aspirating; rescan just before puncture [Herbst].
  3. Confirm position: agitated saline seen in the pericardium, or wire seen around the heart. Then Seldinger pigtail drain.
  4. Blood that clots in the tube suggests a chamber: stop and re-image CHECK.
  5. Send fluid (page 2 of 2). Repeat POCUS; CXR for pneumothorax.

Risks: RV or coronary puncture, arrhythmia, pneumothorax, liver injury, pulmonary oedema after drainage.

Tertiary option

GSH cardiology (cardiothoracics on their advice): haemopericardium needing surgery; loculated, clotted or posterior effusion; pericardial window for recurrence; pericardiectomy for constriction or purulent pericarditis. Discuss with the IM consultant first; typed letter co-signed by the treating consultant.

Mitigation · while waiting, or if declined
  • Keep the pigtail in (extended drainage) to prevent re-accumulation.
  • Repeat ultrasound-guided taps for recurrence.
  • Malignant: palliative care team; agree a ceiling of care.

ONGOING
  • After the tap: POCUS or echo at 24 h and before the drain comes out. Record drain output 6hrly; remove when < 25 mL/24 h CHECK.
  • JVP still high after drainage (Kussmaul sign, pericardial knock): effusive-constrictive pericarditis, usually TB: GSH cardiology.
  • Pulmonary oedema or shock after a big drainage (decompression syndrome): treat as acute LV failure; call GRH IM.
  • TB pericarditis: notify; HIV care and ART per the STG (no PI-based regimens at George). Echo at 4–8 weeks for constriction CHECK.
  • Hold anticoagulants and antiplatelets unless there is a strong indication CHECK.
Acute pericarditis, no tamponade

Diagnosis (2 of 4): pleuritic pain, better sitting forward · rub · widespread ST elevation or PR depression · new effusion [ESC 2015] CHECK

Admit if: fever > 38 °C · subacute onset · large effusion or tamponade · HIV or other immunosuppression · trauma · on an anticoagulant · raised troponin (myopericarditis) · no response to NSAID in 1 week.

Treat (not in the STG) DECISION: NSAID (ibuprofen) 600 mg 8hrly with food for 1–2 weeks, then taper, + PPI (omeprazole); colchicine 0.5 mg 12hrly (0.5 mg daily if < 70 kg) for 3 months [ICAP] CHECK doses. Steroid only if these fail or are contraindicated (more recurrence). Uraemic: dialysis, not NSAID.

Avoid
  • Diuretic, nitrate or vasodilator in tamponade
  • Intubating before the effusion is drained
  • A needle in type A dissection
  • Anticoagulants in acute pericarditis without a strong indication
  • NSAID early after MI (use aspirin) or in uraemia CHECK
